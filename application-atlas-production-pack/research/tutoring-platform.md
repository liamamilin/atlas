# Research Notes — Tutoring Platform

Date: 2026-09-09
Leaf: Tutoring Platform (§23 Education, Research & Knowledge Institutions)
Slug: tutoring-platform

## Research Goal

Understand what a Tutoring Platform actually is as an Application Type: who uses it, what
objects exist inside it, how tutoring work flows through it, what states/rules matter, and
where its boundaries sit against neighboring Types (Virtual Classroom, Test Preparation
Platform, AI Tutoring Application, MOOC/LMS family, Service Marketplace, operator-side
tutoring-business management software).

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Hypothesis: a Tutoring Platform brokers and delivers human tutoring — tutors on one side,
  learners (students/parents) on the other, sessions as the unit of service.
- Likely confusions:
  - Virtual Classroom (the room vs the service)
  - Test Preparation Platform (prep vendors sell tutoring as a modality — forward flag from
    that pass requires discharge here)
  - AI Tutoring Application (that pass already drew the seam: software executes the pedagogy
    vs human tutors brokered through the app)
  - MOOC / LMS (asynchronous self-serve vs live human service — mooc pass's held seam)
  - Service Marketplace / Directory (generic two-sided commerce vs education-shaped service)
  - Operator-side tutoring business management software (Teachworks-class) — a different
    center of gravity entirely; directory has no leaf for it (taxonomy note)

## Research Questions

1. Who are the actors (learners, parents, tutors, platform operator, institutional payers/admins)?
2. What is the supply-side object of record? What does a tutor profile carry?
3. How does the learner↔tutor engagement form (browse-book, match, on-demand)?
4. What is the unit of service and what lifecycle does it carry (booking → session → confirmation → record)?
5. Where do sessions happen (in-platform classroom vs external tools vs in-person)?
6. How does money work (prepay/subscription/membership/institution-funded; payouts/commission)?
7. What rules matter (vetting, cancellation, conduct, anonymity, child safety)?
8. What accumulates over time (session records, progress, reviews, earnings)?
9. What is clearly variant, and what would break the Type if removed?

## Product Observations

### Preply — key observations (Evidence layer A unless noted)

Who:
- Students (learners), tutors, corporate admins (Preply Business), platform operator.

Supply side (tutor):
- Registration to teaching: profile photo, certificates, education, headline/description, video introduction, availability, price setting, specialties → the tutor profile is a rich structured object assembled at onboarding.
- Profile carries: teaching-style tags, profile score, visibility toggle (hide/show), reviews (with subcategory reviews + tutor replies), Super Tutor / Professional Tutor tier programs, ranking/visibility consequences for absences and cancellations (product-specific machinery), advertising options.
- Tutoring Standards Policy + harassment policy + block button + spam reporting → conduct/trust machinery.
- Payouts: commission model, Payoneer withdrawals, identity verification, taxes/VAT documentation.

Engagement formation (learner-driven discovery):
- "Find Tutors" search: subject → specialties → price range → native-speaker preference → schedule compatibility → sort. Self-serve browse-and-book.
- Trial lesson: paid, 25/50 min, one per tutor per student; tutors may accept/decline; booking windows/minimum notice; replacement-trial flow. (Trial mechanics = product-specific detail.)
- Subscription continues after trial; lessons tracked as a balance ("How many lessons do I have and why does the number change").

Session (unit of service):
- Scheduling + rescheduling + cancellation policy with cutoff windows (12h for trials — product-specific).
- Delivered in Preply Classroom: video, chat/messenger (also outside lessons), file sharing, screen sharing, whiteboard (desktop), notes, vocabulary/flashcards, homework feature, Lesson Insights; placement tests + learning plans; learning-materials library (EN/ES).
- Auto-confirmation after lesson end → releases tutor payment (product-specific timing, generalizable pattern: completion confirmation gates payout).
- Fallback documented to external tools (Zoom/Google Meet/Skype) — the classroom is preferred but not structurally exclusive.

Records/feedback:
- Public reviews of tutors (star + text, subcategory reviews, tutor replies); Lesson Insights; certificates for students; referral programs.

Institutional/B2B tier:
- Preply Business: corporate dashboard (employee management incl. bulk upload, balance management, activity monitoring), progress insights from student & tutor feedback, company goals & skills targets, SSO/provisioning. Learners are employees; funding is corporate.

### Convergent findings (Layer B — cross-product commonality)

1. **Tutors held as the supplying side of record.** All four products maintain tutors as persistent identified records — vetted through some admission gate (documented concretely at Preply [help-center policy pages] and Tutor.com [FAQ vetting list]), carrying instructional capability (subjects, qualifications) and availability.
2. **The engagement is formed through the platform, not outside it.** Browse-and-book (Preply), platform-match (GoStudent), on-demand assignment + favorites/scheduling (Tutor.com), instant connect + browse (Cambly). Three distinct formation mechanisms; the invariant is platform-mediated binding of learner to tutor.
3. **The tutoring session is the unit of service and of record.** All four: time-bound live human instruction between tutor and learner (1:1 dominant, small groups at some poles); all four persist session records (history/summaries/transcripts/recordings/feedback).
4. **Delivery runs through an in-platform virtual classroom, with the medium replaceable.** All four ship their own classroom (video, chat, files, whiteboard-class tools). Preply explicitly documents falling back to Zoom/Google Meet; Teachworks (operator side) integrates an external classroom. → the room is a standard capability, not the Type.
5. **Session completion connects to payment.** Preply documents confirmation gating tutor payout; Tutor.com is institution-funded (no learner payment) — money flows through the platform where learner-side payment exists; funding-side identity varies (consumer/family/corporation/institution).
6. **Trust-and-safety machinery.** Vetting at admission, conduct policies/codes, reporting/blocking; child-safety posture (safeguarding policy, parent reporting) where minors are served.
7. **Progress/reporting layer.** Lesson insights (Preply), Lesson Summaries + Education Report (GoStudent), progress surfaces (Cambly), institutional analytics (Tutor.com) — common, not definitional.

### Divergent / variant findings (Layer B/C)

- Formation philosophy: browse-led marketplace vs match-led managed service vs on-demand pool — a variant axis, not a Type split.
- Funding: learner/family pays vs institution funds (Tutor.com, Preply Business) — variant axis.
- Identity: anonymous sessions (Tutor.com) vs profile-rich mutual identity (Preply) — variant axis.
- Subject scope: language-only vs school-subjects vs broad — variant axis.
- Trial lessons: paid (Preply) vs free (GoStudent) vs none-surfaced (Tutor.com/Cambly) — variant.
- Group instruction: 1:1 dominant; small groups at Cambly (1:2) and Tutor.com HDT (program pole) — variant.
- Asynchronous adjuncts: drop-off review (Tutor.com), homework/tools between lessons (Preply), self-study library + AI add-on (GoStudent Learning) — variant/optional.
- Online-only vs offline: all sampled products are online; in-person agencies are the conceptual ancestor (offline shape) — recorded, not claimed as a current product pole.

## Canonical Model (L0 / L1 / L2 / L3)

### L0 — Defining Invariant (deliberately minimal)

Three jointly-held structures:

1. **The tutor roster — the supply side of record.** Tutors held as persistent identified
   instructional profiles (subjects taught, vetted qualifications, availability, commonly
   learner-facing ratings and/or prices) that the platform admits through vetting and books
   against. Remove → a tutor directory/listing with no service, or a school's staff list.
2. **Engagement formation — the platform binds learner to tutor.** A specific learner
   (student; child-with-guardian; employee; institution member) is bound to a specific tutor
   through learner-driven discovery-and-booking, platform matching, or on-demand assignment —
   creating a usable learner↔tutor relationship, not just a search result. Remove → content
   platform (nobody teaches you), or a lead-gen directory (binding happens off-platform).
3. **The tutoring session as the unit of service and record.** Scheduled live human
   instruction between that tutor and that learner or small group — delivered in an in-platform
   virtual classroom, an external video tool, or arranged in person — persisting as a session
   record (history, summaries/transcripts, commonly feedback) on which the relationship, the
   money and quality control run. Remove → ephemeral video calling, or a booking tool with no
   instructional service.

Jointly-held load-bearing tests:
- 1 alone = tutor directory/listing
- 2 without 1 = matchmaking with no supply of record
- 3 without 1+2 = generic video-call/booking tool
- 1+2 without 3 = lead-gen/directory-with-booking (the seam toward Service Marketplace/Directory)
- 1+3 without 2 = sessions not formed by the platform (virtual-classroom-with-tutors territory)
- 2+3 without 1 = anonymous dispatch with no tutor of record

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Virtual Classroom (§23, unprocessed).** Same media, different center of gravity. Every sampled tutoring platform ships a classroom, but the classroom is separable: Preply documents external-tool fallback; Teachworks integrates an external classroom; a bare classroom product has no tutor roster, no engagement formation, no service commerce. Remove the tutoring service from a tutoring platform → a virtual classroom; remove the room → the platform still exists (external tools, offline sessions). Held from this side; forward flag for the virtual-classroom pass.
2. **vs Test Preparation Platform — FORWARD FLAG DISCHARGED from this side.** That pass expected the discriminator "human-tutor sessions vs self-directed scored practice loop" and flagged joint review. Confirmed keep-both: the prep platform's organizing object is the external exam with a scored practice loop and self-directed postures; the tutoring platform's unit of service is the human session. Overlap is packaging: prep vendors sell tutoring as a modality (that pass's observation), and tutoring platforms sell exam-prep tutoring (Preply IELTS learning plans; Tutor.com AP/NCLEX areas) — the exam appears as a tutoring *purpose*, never as the organizing object. Removal tests: strip sessions/tutors from a tutoring platform and point content at an exam → prep product; strip the exam target from prep and add tutors → tutoring product.
3. **vs AI Tutoring Application — RATIFIED from this side.** Clean structural split already held by that pass (software executes pedagogy vs human tutors brokered). Hybrid packaging documented at GoStudent (AI-tutor add-on beside human tutoring), consistent with that pass's prediction. Keep-both, no taxonomy change.
4. **vs MOOC Platform.** Consistent with that pass's held seam: "live human service vs asynchronous self-serve". MOOC's object of record is the published course with open enrollment; tutoring's object is the tutor relationship and session. No conflict.
5. **vs LMS / Corporate LMS / Customer Training Academy.** LMS = institution-rostered course container with gradebook; academy = operator's external-audience catalog. Tutoring = personal service relationships without roster/gradebook center. Tutoring can be *delivered inside* these systems as a module (institution-funded pole) without collapsing the Types.
6. **vs Service Marketplace (§05.02) / Listings & Directory (§02.11).** Generic two-sided service commerce lacks the education-shaped supply (vetted instructional profiles), the session as unit of service, and learning-progress machinery. Contact-only tutoring directories (tutor listing + off-platform arrangement) sit at the seam: they carry leg 1 and the *idea* of leg 2 but fail leg 3 — classified as the directory side of the boundary, not this Type's core. (Superprof-class could not be fetched to observe first-hand; seam recorded at structural strength only.)
7. **vs operator-side tutoring-business management software (Teachworks-class).** Different center of gravity: the business's back office (student records, staff/payroll, invoicing, branches) vs the learner↔tutor service. TAXONOMY NOTE: the directory has no §23 leaf for tutoring-business management software, although the sibling family exists for other verticals (dance-studio-management, martial-arts, fitness-studio passes; school-management-system pass referenced "commercial/tutoring class-record tooling" as a distinct pole). Recorded as a Boundary Issue, not resolved here.
8. **vs Language Learning Application (unprocessed).** Human language tutoring (Preply, Cambly poles) is inside this Type; the self-directed language-practice loop belongs to Language Learning Application (per ai-tutoring pass's framing of the loop as a subject-domain sibling). Forward note for that pass.
9. **vs Appointment Scheduling Application (§03.09).** Generic booking machinery vs instructional service; scheduling is embedded standard machinery here (both directions hold).
10. **vs student-success-platform.** Consistent with that pass's note: tutoring appears there as an intervention destination inside the institution-wide success loop; here it is the product itself. No conflict.
11. **vs after-school-program-management.** That Type is operator-side program delivery management (sessions/attendance for a program); tutoring platform centers the tutor↔learner service relationship. Adjacent, no conflict.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check (§24)

- Paper-era tutoring agency: tutor card file with subjects/qualifications (leg 1), agency matching parent↔tutor by phone (leg 2), sessions held and logged in the agency ledger with fees (leg 3). Satisfies the core with zero software.
- University/college tutoring-center appointment book: tutor schedules by subject, student books a slot, session held and logged, no payment (institution-funded). Satisfies legs 1–3 without commerce machinery → confirms money is NOT definitional.
- Regional offline cram-school/agency matching (front-desk match, home sessions) fits.
- Modern online-only realizations (all four sampled products) fit with no additional core machinery.
→ The definition is not over-fitted to the current online marketplace implementation: no trial lessons, no subscriptions, no in-platform classroom, no reviews, no commission in the core.

## Uncertainties

## Final Synthesis

The Tutoring Platform is the platform for human tutoring as a service: it holds tutors as a
vetted supply side of record, forms learner↔tutor engagements through discovery, matching or
on-demand assignment, and runs the tutoring session as the unit of service and record — with
the classroom, the commerce and the progress reporting as standard (replaceable) machinery
around that spine. The Type spans browse-led marketplaces, match-led managed services,
instant-access pools and institution-funded deployments; what all share is the tutor roster,
the platform-formed engagement and the session as the record the whole service runs on.
