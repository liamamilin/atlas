# Research Notes — Telecom Product Catalog

Research date: 2026-09-10
Slug: telecom-product-catalog
Directory leaf: Telecom Product Catalog (§19 Energy, Utilities & Telecommunications)

## Research Goal

Understand what a Telecom Product Catalog application actually is from real products: what objects it holds (offers? specifications? both commercial and technical?), how the commercial offer is bound to technical service realization, what rules and pricing structures live in the catalog, how catalog entities are governed (lifecycle, versioning, effective dating) and distributed to consuming systems, who authors and who consumes, and where its boundaries lie against Telecom BSS, Telecom Order Management, Telecom Charging Platform, Telecom Provisioning, CPQ, PIM / Product Catalog Management / Digital Product Catalog (§05.04), Business Rules Management Systems, and Master Data Management.

Pre-hung seams to discharge (from prior §19 passes):

- telecom-bss (2026-09-10): "telecom-product-catalog = offer definition as a standalone discipline vs the chain's source of sellable offers" — the BSS pass documented the catalog as one of the suite's component leaves and deferred component depth to this pass.
- telecom-order-management (2026-09-10): its L0 leg 2 is "specification/catalog-driven decomposition", with the explicit note that "the catalog itself is a separate system/Type" — this pass owns the definition side of that seam.

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Expected: the BSS-side definition system that holds the operator's sellable offers (plans, bundles, options, prices) and the technical specifications that realize them (services, resources), feeding sales channels, order management, charging, and provisioning.
- Expected nearest neighbors: Telecom BSS (suite category), Telecom Order Management (primary runtime consumer), Telecom Charging Platform (pricing/rating seam), CPQ (selling-side consumer), PIM/Product Catalog Management (§05.04 retail siblings), Business Rules Management System, Master Data Management.
- Expected unknowns: whether the commercial and technical catalogs are one system or several; how much pricing lives in the catalog vs the charging engine; whether lifecycle/versioning is definitional or maturity depth; whether standalone (suite-independent) catalog products exist as a distinct market.

## Research Questions

1. What is the unit of record — offer, specification, or both? What exactly is an "offering" vs a "specification"?
2. What is the commercial→technical structure (product spec → service spec → resource spec; CFS/RFS lineage)? Where is it held and how is it expressed?
3. What relationships and rules does the catalog hold (bundles, requires, realized-as, composed-of, eligibility, decomposition rules), and who executes them?
4. What pricing content lives in the catalog (one-time/recurring, price lists, alterations, promotions, terms) vs in the charging engine?
5. How are catalog entities governed: lifecycle states, versioning, effective dating, publication gating?
6. Who authors (roles) and who consumes (systems and surfaces)? Through which interfaces and APIs?
7. What variants exist: unified commercial+technical catalog vs standards-split catalogs; suite module vs standalone; telecom-only vs multi-industry; B2C vs B2B depth?
8. What distinguishes this Type from PIM/retail product catalog management, from CPQ, and from generic rule/MDM platforms?

## Product B — ServiceNow Product Catalog (Order Management / TMT Sales CRM)

### Key observations (evidence layer A)

- Concept page ("Setting up specifications and product offerings"): "By creating and publishing versioned specifications and product offerings in a technical product catalog, your customers can order up-to-date versions of the products or services that they require... A product offering contains the product description, pricing, eligibility, and product options. You use a product catalog to group your product offerings so that your customers can order the products or services that they want. Only published product offerings can be added to customer contracts."
- "The relationship between product offerings and the product, service, and resource specification determine how a product is fulfilled."
  - Product specification: "defines the various aspects of a product... All the specification characteristic options that are available for a product, and how they should be fulfilled... Product specifications are customer-facing and are added to product offerings... Its relationship to other product, service, and resource specifications, and how a customer order of a product offering is delivered."
  - Service specification: "defines a service, and the technical information of that service... Service specifications can be customer-facing or resource-facing."
  - Resource specification: "defines the technical information for a resource... Resource specifications are not customer-facing... You can enable the product and service delivery by using the required resource."
- Specification relationships (TMT doc): "Specification relationships define the relationships and associations between the product, service, and resource specifications. They designate how an order that contains the source specification is decomposed, fulfilled, and delivered to the customer." Relationship types: **Bundles** (product→product etc.; "decompose into unique order line items for each of the target specifications bundled from that parent"), **Realized as** (product→service; service→service), **Requires** (product/service→resource; "To enable the delivery of the promised services in the specification, an order that contains the source specification and characteristics also requires the target resource specification"), **Composed of** (same-type composition). Fields: Mandatory flag ("the target specification is always included with the source specification" vs optional), Order, Minimum/Default/Maximum quantity, Quantity characteristic (quantity mapping: "The order decomposition process uses the mapped quantity to decompose the order... It then creates the required number of domain orders for the target specification").
- Decomposition rules: "Decomposition rules (often exclusion rules) determine when domain orders should or should not be created, based on characteristics or options present in the order line item." (Order decomposition doc.)
- Decomposition driven by the catalog (Order decomposition doc, verbatim): "This process is driven by definitions in the product catalog to ensure that all necessary components are provisioned correctly... The decomposition is guided by specifications, specification relationships, and decomposition rules defined in the product catalog." CFS/RFS: "A corresponding customer-facing service order (CFS)... A resource-facing service order (RFS)... Resource order."
- Product offering fields (Create product offerings doc): pricing method (One-time / Recurring with Periodicity monthly/annually, monthly recurring charges, non-recurring charges, price lists, ramps); Sellable flag ("sold as a standalone item... or as part of bundle but not as a standalone item"); Distribution channel ("you can specify web as a channel... multiple channels"); Start date / End date (availability window); State ("In Draft, Published, Retired, and Archived. You can update product offerings In Draft state"); Version / Initial version / Previous version; Product family; Configurable (CPQ Configurator); Transient products (one-time use; no MACD on their sold-product records); Contract term (12–60 months options); Product specification link ("Functional view of a product offering that drives order fulfillment. Only published specifications appear").
- Catalog structure (Create product offering catalog doc): "Catalogs are top-level entities in the catalog hierarchy. Catalogs have categories and categories can have sub-categories or product offers." Catalog lifecycle states: Draft, Published, Retired, Archived.
- Eligibility (Product Catalog Management page): "product offer eligibility: Filter your product catalogs, product offering categories, and product offerings so that agents sell customers only the products for which they're eligible... You define these rules using product eligibility matrices" (context: customer accounts, location, sales contracts).
- Versioning practice (TMT spec-version doc): "Create and publish a version of an existing specification in a Published state so that you can offer an improved version of it to your customers in your product catalog... update the specification relationships, decomposition rules, quantity mappings, and attribute propagation rules." And the concept page warns: "Failure to update the product specifications can lead to difficulties with MACD (Modify, Add, Change, Delete) operation when orders enter fulfillment."
- Roles: `sn_prd_pm.product_catalog_admin`, `sn_prd_pm.product_manager`, `sn_prd_pm.product_catalog_manager` (role-required annotations in task docs); order-side roles (order agent, fulfillment agent, fallout agent) are Order Management's, not the catalog's.
- Interfaces: CSM/FSM Configurable Workspace (catalog configuration lists/forms: Product Specifications, Offerings, Product Offering Catalogs, Specification Relationships tabs), product offering hierarchy + specification hierarchy views ("View the complete hierarchy of a product offering and any associated specifications (product, service, and resource). Use these views to verify that all entities have been defined and associated correctly"), catalog UI for agents browsing offerings in lead/opportunity/quote/order workflows.

## Corroborating industry standard — TM Forum TMF620 Product Catalog Management API v5.0.0

### Key observations (evidence layer A for the specification text)

- API family framing: "Product Catalog API is one of Catalog Management API Family. Product Catalog API goal is to provide a catalog of products."
- Purpose (user guide): "The Product Catalog Management API allows the management of the entire lifecycle of the catalog elements, the consultation of catalog elements during several processes such as ordering process, campaign management, sales management."
- Resources (OAS tags): productCatalog, category, productOffering, productOfferingPrice, productSpecification, importJob, exportJob, notification listeners (create/delete/state-change/attribute-change events per entity), event subscriptions.
- ProductOffering schema: "Represents entities that are orderable from the provider of the catalog, this resource includes pricing information." Attributes: isBundle ("determines whether a productOffering represents a single productOffering (false), or a bundle of productOfferings (true)"), isSellable ("A flag indicating if this product offer can be sold stand-alone... If this flag is false it indicates that the offer can only be sold within a bundle"), validFor (TimePeriod), version, lifecycleStatus, place ("places where the products are sold or delivered"), channel, marketSegment, productOfferingTerm ("a condition under which a ProductOffering is made available to Customers. For instance... multiple commitment periods"), productOfferingPrice, bundledProductOffering, bundledGroupProductOffering ("A group of ProductOfferings that can be selected for instantiation, e.g. between 2 and 7 from a list of 15 channel packs"), productOfferingRelationship, productOfferingCharacteristic ("A characteristic quality or distinctive feature of a ProductOffering... fixed at design (catalog authoring) time"), prodSpecCharValueUse (offering-level restriction/override of spec characteristic values: "a characteristic 'Color' might have values White, Blue, Green, and Red. But, the list of values can be restricted to e.g. White and Blue in an associated product offering"), policy, allowedAction ("List of actions that can be executed (in context of a product order) on products instantiated from this offering"), productSpecification (ref), serviceCandidate / resourceCandidate (refs into the service/resource catalog family), agreement, serviceLevelAgreement, attachment, externalIdentifier.
- ProductSpecification schema: "Is a detailed description of a tangible or intangible object made available externally in the form of a ProductOffering to customers or other parties playing a party role." Attributes: productSpecCharacteristic ("can take on a discrete value... a range of values... or can be derived from a formula... Certain characteristics, such as color, may be configured during the ordering or some other process"), serviceSpecification ("ServiceSpecification(s) required to realize a ProductSpecification"), resourceSpecification ("The ResourceSpecification is required to realize a ProductSpecification"), bundledProductSpecification, productSpecificationRelationship ("A migration, substitution, dependency or exclusivity relationship between/among product specifications"), validFor, version, lifecycleStatus, brand, productNumber, category, policy, targetProductSchema, intentSpecification.
- ProductOfferingPrice schema: "An amount, usually of money, that is asked for or allowed when a ProductOffering is bought, rented, or leased. The price is valid for a defined period of time and may not represent the actual price paid by a customer." Attributes: price (Money), percentage ("Percentage to apply if this Product Offering Price is an Alteration (such as a Discount)"), recurringChargePeriodType/Length ("The period to repeat the application of the price — Could be month, week..."), unitOfMeasure, isBundle, bundledPopRelationship, popRelationship ("Product Offering Prices related to this Product Offering Price, for example a price alteration such as allowance or discount"), validFor; term influence: "a productOffering may be cheaper with a 24 month commitment than with a 12 month commitment."
- Catalog-to-catalog machinery: importJob/exportJob resources; ODA page: "Provides a standardized solution for rapidly adding partners' products to an existing Catalog. It brings the capability for Service Providers to directly feed partners systems with the technical description of the products they propose to them." Conformance profile: "The Product Catalog API allows defining product catalog entities such as offerings and specifications, creating pricing for offerings, and transferring information between catalogs."
- Family note: service and resource specifications have their own catalog APIs in the same family (TMF633 Service Catalog, TMF634 Resource Catalog); TMF620's ProductOffering references them via serviceCandidate/resourceCandidate and ProductSpecification references serviceSpecification/resourceSpecification. The standards family splits commercial and technical catalogs; sampled products tend to hold them in one catalog.

### Cross-product findings

- **B (cross-product, 5/5 + TMF620)**: Every sampled product holds BOTH sellable offers AND technical specifications. The offer is the orderable, priced, customer-facing entity; the specification is the reusable definition (product, service, resource) from which offers are realized and by which fulfillment is driven. TMF620 formalizes exactly this pair (ProductOffering "orderable... includes pricing information" + ProductSpecification "required to realize").
- **B (5/5 + TMF620)**: The commercial→technical realization binding is the telecom signature: offers map to product specs, product specs realize as service specs (CFS/RFS), service specs require resource specs. Salesforce: "map these two entity types to one another so that Order Management can decompose"; ServiceNow: relationship types Bundles/Realized as/Requires/Composed of; Ericsson: "reusable product, service and resource components"; TMF620: "ServiceSpecification(s) required to realize a ProductSpecification."
- **B (5/5 + TMF620)**: The catalog is the single, governed source consumed at runtime by the surrounding chain. Ericsson: "single, consistent source of offerings, specifications, and underlying business rules to... drive fulfillment, charging and assurance" + "OSS/BSS and network systems to access catalog data in real time"; Amdocs: "unified source of product and service data"; Salesforce: "single source of truth"; Netcracker: "catalog-driven approach"; TMF620: "consultation of catalog elements during... ordering process."
- **B (4/5 + TMF620)**: Lifecycle states gate what is sellable/consumable: ServiceNow "Only published product offerings can be added to customer contracts" (Draft/Published/Retired/Archived); Salesforce "set it to Active and Orderable to make it sellable" + Draft in API; TMF620 lifecycleStatus + validFor on every entity. Ericsson/Amdocs/Netcracker assert lifecycle management at positioning level. Full versioning machinery depth varies (Salesforce gates it by license tier) — the state gate is universal, deep versioning is maturity.
- **B (4/5 + TMF620)**: Pricing content lives in the catalog at offer level: one-time/recurring charges with periods (ServiceNow, TMF620), price lists/price books per channel/market (Salesforce, ServiceNow, TMF620 channel/marketSegment), alterations/discounts/promotions (Salesforce, TMF620 percentage/alteration), commitment terms influencing price (ServiceNow contract terms, TMF620 productOfferingTerm). Usage rating logic is NOT catalog content — it belongs to charging (seam documented in the telecom-charging pass).
- **B (4/5 + TMF620)**: Bundling with structure and cardinality: bundles of child offerings/specs, mandatory vs optional components, min/default/max quantities, group selections (TMF620 "between 2 and 7 from a list of 15"), sellable-vs-bundle-only flags (ServiceNow Sellable, TMF620 isSellable).
- **B (3/5 + TMF620)**: Eligibility/availability rules scoped to customer, channel, market, place, time: ServiceNow eligibility matrices; Salesforce availability/eligibility rules frameworks; TMF620 channel/marketSegment/place/validFor/policy.
- **B (3/5)**: Catalog federation/consolidation is a named concern: Ericsson "federate existing catalogs into a single view"; Amdocs "a single catalog derived from over 20 catalogs"; TMF620 import/export jobs and partner-product onboarding; Salesforce wholesale page "integrating with third-party product catalogs."
- **A (ServiceNow)**: The catalog's definitions are runtime-executed logic, not just data: decomposition rules are exclusion rules keyed on order-line characteristics; quantity mappings generate domain-order counts; the vendor explicitly warns that stale specifications break MACD order fulfillment.
- **A (Salesforce)**: Reusable-component philosophy: object types, picklists, attributes, specs as templates; "Create product attributes, pricing components, and other standalone building blocks once, and then reuse them many times across the catalog."
- **A→B**: Roles converge: product designers/managers (author offers/specs/relationships), pricing designers/managers (price books, promotions), catalog administrators (structure, migration, permissions) — documented verbatim at Salesforce and ServiceNow; Ericsson names "product teams" and CSRs as consumers.

## Canonical Model (abstraction)

### Level 0 — Defining Invariant (minimal)

The Type is recognizable only with all three, jointly:

1. **The offer-and-specification catalog as the operator's system of record for what it sells.** Persistent, individually identified records in two linked kinds: sellable offerings (orderable entities carrying pricing, bundling structure, channel/market/place scoping, and validity windows) and specifications (reusable definitions of products, services, and resources with characteristics). Remove → a price list, a marketing brochure, or a spreadsheet of SKUs.
2. **The commercial-to-technical realization binding.** Each sellable offering is bound to the technical specifications that realize it — product specification realized as service specifications (customer-facing and resource-facing), which require resource specifications — together with the relationships and rules (bundle composition, requires, realized-as, eligibility, decomposition rules) that downstream systems execute at runtime. Remove → a retail product catalog / PIM (goods retail has no technical realization layer), or a disconnected technical spec library with nothing sellable.
3. **Governed publication feeding the commercial chain at runtime.** Catalog entities move through managed lifecycle states (draft → published/active → retired) with validity dating that gates what channels may sell and what fulfillment systems consume; the published definitions are the runtime source for sales/CPQ channels, order decomposition, charging, and provisioning. Remove → a static offer document or a design-time modeling tool nobody executes against.

Jointly-held load-bearing checks:
- 1 alone = product database / price list
- 2 without 1 = technical specification library with nothing sellable (OSS-side spec store)
- 3 without 1+2 = governance machinery over nothing
- 1+2 without 3 = an authored model no system consumes (a modeling tool)
- 1+3 without 2 = retail PIM with workflow (no telecom service semantics)
- 2+3 without 1 = specification management with no offers to sell

### Level 1 — Common Mature Structure (standard, not defining)

- Catalog/category hierarchy organization (catalogs → categories → offerings; product families)
- Bundles with cardinalities: mandatory vs optional components, min/default/max quantities, group selections, bundle-only (non-standalone) components
- Characteristics/attributes with design-time vs run-time (order-captured) values; offering-level restriction/override of specification characteristic values
- Offer-level pricing structures: one-time and recurring charges with periods, price lists/price books per channel/market/customer segment, alterations (discounts/allowances), promotions, commitment terms
- Eligibility/availability rules (who may buy what, in which channel/market/period), often rule-framework or matrix based
- Versioning and effective dating of catalog entities; change/version publication flows
- Graphical/no-code designer tooling; reusable building blocks (object types, picklists, attributes, templates)
- Import/export and catalog-to-catalog transfer; partner-product onboarding; federation of legacy catalogs into a single view
- TM Forum SID vocabulary alignment (offer/product/service/resource; CFS/RFS)
- Agent/CSR-facing browse and search over the catalog; customer-facing channel surfacing of offerings
- Role model: product/offer designers, pricing designers/managers, catalog administrators

### Level 2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- Catalog architecture pole: unified commercial+technical catalog in one system (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Ericsson, Amdocs, Netcracker) vs standards-family split (TMF620 product catalog + TMF633 service catalog + TMF634 resource catalog, linked by references) — both satisfy the binding
- Hosting: licensed module on a CRM/platform (Salesforce EPC), platform apps (ServiceNow), portfolio component deployable alongside other vendors' systems (Ericsson), suite component (Amdocs, Netcracker); standalone catalog products exist in the market's history but all sampled products are suite components or licensed modules
- Industry breadth: telecom-only vs multi-industry (Salesforce serves Communications, Media, Energy & Utilities; Netcracker claims telco + non-telco offerings)
- Tangible goods alongside services (devices, CPE — "tangible or intangible" in TMF620; smartphone/SIM example at Salesforce)
- B2B depth: enterprise offer hierarchies, site-level configuration, feasibility/pre-design hooks for CPQ
- Partner/wholesale catalog content (feeding partner systems with technical descriptions; TMF620 framing)
- Transient vs persistent offerings (one-time items vs MACD-manageable subscriptions — ServiceNow)
- Era-current: GenAI-assisted offer configuration (Netcracker), agentic offer design — not definitional

### Level 3 — Vendor-specific (research notes only)

- Salesforce: EPC vs Shared Catalog licensing split; Product Designer / Pricing Designer names; object types; four spec types (offer/product/service/resource); SID certification claim; Vlocity lineage (vlocity_cmt namespace, Vlocity Attribute/Picklist entities); Infiwave training narrative; "Design, test, launch, decompose, orchestrate" wholesale phrasing.
- ServiceNow: sn_prd_pm role names; CSM/FSM Configurable Workspace as the authoring surface; specification relationship type names (Bundles/Realized as/Requires/Composed of); decomposition rules as exclusion rules; quantity characteristic mapping; transient products; CPQ blueprint Configuration Status; product family trees; the MACD-staleness warning.
- Ericsson: Catalog Manager name and "more than a simple product catalog management system" positioning; CSR product-selection search engine; federation across "multiple fulfillment stacks"; Digital Monetization Platform membership (from the BSS pass).
- Amdocs: CatalogONE name; "single catalog derived from over 20 catalogs" operator case; Microservices360/DigitalONE foundation framing.
- Netcracker: unified Product Catalog inside Commerce Management; reusable templates + GenAI assistance; "zero-fallout orchestration" downstream claim (order-management side).

## Rejected Findings (anti-overfit)

- **"Telecom Product Catalog = PIM / retail product catalog"** — rejected. PIM manages product information/content for goods retail (descriptions, media, channel syndication) with no technical realization layer and no runtime fulfillment consumption. The telecom catalog's center is the offer→service→resource chain executed by order management, charging, and provisioning.
- **"The TM Forum SID model / TMF620 conformance is definitional"** — rejected. SID vocabulary is the dominant industry framing (Salesforce certified; ServiceNow CFS/RFS-aligned; TMF620 is the standard contract), but products predate and vary in conformance. The conceptual offer/specification split and realization binding are the invariants; the vocabulary is implementation.
- **"Unified commercial+technical in ONE catalog is definitional"** — rejected. The TM Forum family itself splits product/service/resource catalogs into separate APIs (TMF620/633/634) linked by references. The binding is the invariant; one-system vs standards-split is architecture.
- **"Deep versioning/change-management machinery is definitional"** — rejected. Salesforce gates versioning/lifecycle depth behind the EPC license while Shared Catalog still functions as the chain's catalog. The universal minimum is a lifecycle state gate (draft/published/active/retired) + validity dating; deep versioning is maturity.
- **"The catalog computes charges"** — rejected. The catalog holds offer-level prices (list, recurring, one-time, alterations, terms); usage rating against plans/balances at consumption time is the Telecom Charging Platform's territory (consistent with that pass's L0 and the BSS pass's chain framing).
- **"Graphical/no-code designers are definitional"** — rejected; era-current tooling. Paper-era tariff guides and service manuals satisfy the core (historical check below).
- **"GenAI/agentic offer design is definitional"** — rejected; era-current layer in 2026 pitches.
- **"The catalog must include tangible goods"** — rejected; services-only catalogs satisfy the core (TMF620's "tangible or intangible" phrasing marks goods as optional breadth).
- **"One product = the whole Type"** — rejected; the sampled implementations differ radically in hosting, licensing, and scope while sharing the three L0 structures.

## Boundary Findings

- **vs Telecom BSS**: component vs chain. The BSS pass documented the seam verbatim: the catalog is "offer definition as a standalone discipline vs the chain's source of sellable offers." BSS is the integrated commercial chain (sell → deliver → get paid → care) over shared records; the catalog is the definition system the chain's front end (channels, CPQ) and fulfillment systems consume. Remove the chain → catalog; remove the definition discipline → BSS category. TM Forum ODA places the product catalog in Core Commerce (BSS side).
- **vs Telecom Order Management**: the catalog defines; order management consumes at runtime. ServiceNow verbatim: decomposition "is driven by definitions in the product catalog"; the catalog holds the specifications, relationships, and decomposition rules; order management holds the order of record and orchestrates fulfillment. Remove order lifecycle/orchestration → catalog; remove offer/spec definition → order management. This discharge RATIFIES the telecom-order-management pass's note that "the catalog itself is a separate system/Type."
- **vs Telecom Charging Platform**: the catalog holds what the offer costs (offer prices, terms, alterations); charging computes what consumption costs (usage rated against plans/balances in real time). The catalog may publish price/rating references that charging consumes, but rating logic and balance state are not catalog content. Remove usage rating/balances → catalog; remove offer definitions → charging.
- **vs Telecom Provisioning Platform**: the catalog's service/resource specifications describe what must be activated and carry provisioning-relevant parameters (Ericsson: "leveraging pre-defined technical specifications and provisioning processes"); provisioning executes activation on the network. Remove execution → catalog; remove definitions → provisioning.
- **vs Product Information Management / PIM (§05.04)**: PIM is the retailer's product-content system (descriptions, media, attributes, channel syndication) for physical goods; no technical realization binding, no runtime fulfillment consumption, no subscription/service semantics. The telecom catalog's defining leg 2 (realization binding) and leg 3 (runtime consumption by fulfillment) are absent in PIM.
- **vs Product Catalog Management (§05.04)**: retail catalog management organizes sellable items for merchandising/sales; it lacks the service/resource specification layer and the decomposition/activation consumption. Same word "catalog," different object world.
- **vs Digital Product Catalog (§05.04)**: a customer-facing presentation surface (interactive catalog for shoppers); the telecom catalog is operator-side definition infrastructure, not a shopper surface.
- **vs Configure Price Quote / CPQ (§07)**: CPQ consumes the catalog to configure, price, and quote deals ("Shared Catalog comes out of the box with Industries CPQ"); the catalog is the foundation CPQ reads. Remove quoting/configuration flows → catalog; remove the definition record → CPQ.
- **vs Business Rules Management System (§10)**: catalog rules are offer-scoped and attached to catalog entities (eligibility, compatibility, decomposition); a BRMS is a general-purpose rule authoring/execution engine across domains. The catalog may embed rule frameworks but is not a rules product.
- **vs Master Data Management (§13)**: MDM governs cross-domain master data (party, product, location) for consistency; the telecom catalog is a domain system of record with offer semantics, realization bindings, and runtime chain consumption. Product master data may synchronize with MDM, but the catalog's identity is the offer/realization discipline.
- **vs ITSM service catalogs (Enterprise Service Management neighborhood)**: an ITSM service catalog lists requestable services for internal users with approval/fulfillment workflows; no commercial pricing structure, no technical realization binding to network resources, no market/channel scoping. Different world despite the shared word.
- **"去掉什么就变成另一个 Type" 判据**: remove the realization binding → PIM/retail catalog; remove runtime chain consumption → a modeling/design tool; remove offers (keep specs) → a technical/service spec store; remove telecom service semantics (keep goods) → retail catalog management.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check

- Paper-era practice: the operator's printed tariff guide / rate manual defined what could be sold (plans, options, distances, rates, terms) and was updated under controlled revision; service-order and plant manuals defined how a service request was technically realized (which plant, equipment, and work); billing charge codes keyed both together. This satisfies all three L0 structures — offer records, technical realization guidance, governed updates feeding order handling and billing — with no software at all. The modern graphical designer, SID vocabulary, APIs, and versioning depth are implementation, not definition.
- 1990s telco IT: service/charge-code tables and tariff tables inside billing/CRM systems played the catalog role before standalone catalog products existed; the standalone product category emerged as offers grew complex (bundling, convergence, shorter lifecycles, multi-channel) — Ericsson's own problem framing ("a vast and complex array of new products that... have shorter lifetimes and multiple channel dependencies") describes the pressure that created the product category, not the definition of the discipline.
- Regional/small operators: small ISPs/cable operators run plan tables + service-configuration guides consumed by ordering and billing — same three structures at small scale. (Reasoned check; kept at low assertion strength.)
- The check passes: the L0 does not require cloud deployment, SID vocabulary, graphical designers, TMF APIs, or deep versioning machinery.

## Uncertainties

- Amdocs CatalogONE: direct page fetch blocked (Incapsula 403); evidence is verbatim search-excerpt positioning plus the prior BSS pass's page capture. No operational detail asserted; CatalogONE's internal object model is unknown from reachable sources.
- Netcracker: marketing-tier only; the unified Product Catalog's internal structure (spec types, relationship vocabulary) is not publicly documented.
- Ericsson Catalog Manager: product-page tier; runtime-access and lifecycle claims kept at page strength; no operational manual reachable.
- Whether meaningful standalone (suite-independent) telecom catalog products hold market share today is not measurable from available sources; all sampled products are suite components or licensed modules. The Type definition does not depend on hosting.
- Exact lifecycle state vocabularies vary by product (Draft/Published/Retired/Archived at ServiceNow; Active/Orderable + Draft at Salesforce; open lifecycleStatus at TMF620); no universal state standard asserted.
- The precise split of pricing responsibility between catalog and charging engine varies by deployment (offer prices in the catalog; rate plans/rating logic in charging; some deployments co-locate more); documented as a seam, not resolved unilaterally.
- The market boundary between "product catalog" and "service catalog" products (some vendors market service-catalog-first tools for OSS-side service design) is blurry at the edges; the sampled evidence treats service/resource specifications as part of the same catalog discipline. Flagged as a taxonomy observation, not restructured.

## Final Synthesis

Telecom Product Catalog is the communications provider's offer-definition system of record. Its defining core is three jointly-held structures: (1) the offer-and-specification catalog — persistent identified records of sellable offerings (orderable, priced, bundled, channel/market-scoped, validity-dated) linked to reusable specifications of products, services, and resources with their characteristics; (2) the commercial-to-technical realization binding — each offer bound to the service specifications (customer-facing and resource-facing) and resource specifications that realize it, with the relationships and rules (bundle composition, requires, realized-as, eligibility, decomposition rules) that downstream systems execute at runtime; (3) governed publication feeding the commercial chain at runtime — lifecycle states and validity dating gate what channels may sell and what fulfillment systems consume, making the catalog the single runtime source of offer truth for sales/CPQ, order decomposition, charging, and provisioning. Around this core, mature products add: catalog/category hierarchies, bundle cardinalities, characteristic design-time/run-time semantics with offering-level overrides, offer pricing structures (one-time/recurring, price lists, alterations, promotions, terms), eligibility rules, versioning and effective dating, designer tooling with reusable components, catalog federation/import-export, SID vocabulary, and role separation (product designers, pricing designers, catalog administrators). Architecture (unified vs standards-split catalogs), hosting (licensed module, platform apps, portfolio component, suite component), industry breadth, tangible goods, B2B depth, and AI assistance are variants, not definitions. The Type is a BSS component with its own directory leaf: it defines what the operator sells and how services are realized; the chain that sells, fulfills, charges, and bills those offers belongs to Telecom BSS and its component leaves — above all Telecom Order Management, whose runtime decomposition is executed against this catalog's definitions.

Historical check passed: paper-era tariff guides + service-order manuals + charge codes satisfy the core with no modern machinery.
