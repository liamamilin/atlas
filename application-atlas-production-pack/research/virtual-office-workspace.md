# Research Notes — Virtual Office Workspace

## Research Goal

Understand what a "Virtual Office Workspace" is as an Application Type, from real products: what the central object is (a place? a space? a room?), how presence works, how live conversations start, what the desk/room means, what work happens inside the space, and where the Type's boundaries lie — especially against the two processed siblings (Team Workspace Platform, Collaborative Workspace) and the forwarded flag from the virtual-meeting-platform pass.

This leaf carries two prior flags that must be discharged from this side:

1. **team-workspace-platform pass (2026-09-09):** "sibling virtual-office-workspace remains unprocessed — spatial-presence pole, expected distinct (presence-space vs assembly-container), joint review recommended when processed."
2. **virtual-meeting-platform pass (2026-09-09):** "spatial-venue reading REJECTED (the venue family self-labels 'virtual office' [Kumospace] / 'virtual & hybrid event platform' [Airmeet], never 'virtual meeting platform' — that population belongs to Virtual Office Workspace §03.12 [unprocessed] and §26 event leaves; flag forwarded: the office venue is a different Type from the convened meeting, and products sell both as separate modes)."

## Initial Boundary Hypothesis (before research)

- Working hypothesis: a Virtual Office Workspace is a persistent virtual place that recreates the team's office — rooms, zones, personal desks — where teammates appear continuously while working, and live audio/video conversations form by co-location (walking over, entering a room) rather than by dialing.
- Nearest neighbors: Team Workspace Platform (assembly container), Video Conferencing / Virtual Meeting Platform (explicit calls), Team Messaging (async text), Virtual Classroom (education), Coworking/Flexible Workspace Management (the "virtual office" naming collision — real-estate services, not software), Social/event platforms (same mechanics, different purpose).
- Known risks: (1) the spatial map might be implementation, not invariant (a presence-first product with minimal spatial rendering may still be the same Type); (2) proximity audio vs room-entry audio — which is definitional?; (3) "virtual office" is a heavily colliding market term.

## Research Questions

1. What is the central object — how do products name and structure the place (space/office/map/rooms/desks/floors)?
2. What does presence mean — continuous during the workday? What states exist? What does "being in the office" signal?
3. How do live conversations start — proximity, room entry, pull mechanics (Get/Knock/wave/invite-to-join)? What ends them?
4. What is the desk's role (claiming, assignment, availability signaling)?
5. What work surfaces exist inside the space (screen share, whiteboards, embedded apps, games)?
6. How do membership and guests work (roles, check-in, domain rules)?
7. What connects the office to outside tools (calendar, chat, app status)?
8. Which capabilities are standard across the sample vs optional vs vendor-specific?
9. Historical check: would older-generation products (Sococo-class, 2010s) still fit the definition without game-like maps and spatial audio?
10. Discharge both prior flags with direct evidence.

## Canonical Model (L0 / L1 / L2 / L3)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

A Virtual Office Workspace is recognizable only if all three hold:

1. **The persistent workplace-shaped place** — the product's unit of record is a standing virtual place structured as a workplace: partitioned into rooms/zones with office semantics (meeting rooms, common areas) and personal spots, persisting across sessions and days (you don't create it per meeting — you "arrive" at it), and editable as a whole by its owners (layout/areas/rooms). (Gather: office with Studio-edited areas; Sococo: the online office map; Kumospace: the Space with floors/rooms; oVice: the Space; Teamflow: the virtual office.) Remove → a floor-plan drawing tool or nothing office-like.
2. **Continuous embodied presence** — each member appears in the place, at a position, while they work: visible avatars/representations carrying availability states; the place shows who is in, where they are, and (in mature products) what they are doing. Presence is ambient and continuous during the workday, not per-call. (Gather: "see who's free"; Sococo: "blinking avatars… colleagues working"; oVice: "bird's eye view"; Kumospace: "walk the floor… see how your team is getting their work done".) Remove → a presence list inside a chat tool, or an org chart; the "office" reading collapses.
3. **Co-location-initiated live conversation** — live audio/video connections form because of shared location (entering the same room, coming near someone) or an in-place pull ("Get", "Knock", "wave over", "invite to join me" — realized as bringing the person's presence to you), and end when participants leave; there is no dialed "call object" between two endpoints. (All five sampled products document this loop in their own words.) Remove → explicit call setup = Video Conferencing territory; the office metaphor loses its defining behavior.

Jointly-held load-bearing analysis:

- 1 alone = a floor-plan/office-map editor with nobody in it.
- 2 without 1 = a presence/status roster (chat-tool territory).
- 3 without 1+2 = dialed video calls (conferencing).
- 1+2 without 3 = a visible office nobody can talk in by co-location — an awareness dashboard, not an office.
- 1+3 without 2 = incoherent (proximity requires visible positions).
- 2+3 without 1 = ephemeral group calls with presence — a meeting tool, no standing place.

Remove-what tests:

- Remove the persistent place (per-meeting rooms) → Video Conferencing / Virtual Meeting Platform.
- Remove continuous presence (join only for scheduled calls) → conferencing.
- Remove co-location as the conversation trigger (dial/call objects) → conferencing.
- Remove the workplace semantics (rooms become topic channels, presence becomes "online") → chat/community territory.
- Remove live conversation (text only) → Team Messaging.
- Remove the team scope (public strangers) → social VR / event-platform territory.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

Present across the sample (evidence B), expected in mature products but not definitional:

- **Personal desks/spots** — claimable/assignable personal locations; "home base" where colleagues look for you (Gather Tier-1; Kumospace/oVice/Teamflow visually; Sococo room-based instead — desk is common, not universal).
- **Availability/status layer** — free/busy/focus states, custom status, calendar-driven status, app-usage status (Gather Tier-1; oVice/Kumospace presence framing).
- **Meeting rooms + scheduled meetings** — calendar integration, room booking/links, meeting view, recording (Gather Tier-1; Sococo town halls; Kumospace meetings mode; oVice calendar integration).
- **Screen sharing** — commonly multiple simultaneous shares (Gather, Teamflow, Kumospace, Sococo, oVice all document it).
- **In-space work surfaces** — whiteboards, embedded apps/documents, interactive objects/games (Gather, Teamflow, Kumospace).
- **Text chat** — auxiliary to live conversation (Gather chat overview; Sococo; oVice; Kumospace team chat as separate mode).
- **Guest access** — links/check-in for outsiders (Gather Tier-1; Sococo; Kumospace meeting links).
- **Member/guest roles + admin** — office settings, desk management, membership administration (Gather Tier-1; Kumospace SSO; oVice space admin implied).
- **Privacy controls** — lock conversation/room, private desks/rooms, mute-by-default (Gather Tier-1; Kumospace private rooms).
- **Integrations** — calendar, chat tools, app-status (all five).
- **Usage analytics** — who collaborates, activity dashboards (Sococo, Kumospace, oVice; plan-gated in some).
- **Audio/video controls** — noise cancellation, backgrounds, device toggles (Gather, oVice, Kumospace).

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- **Spatial rendering style**: game-like top-down map with a movable avatar (Gather) vs schematic floor plan with click-to-move (Sococo) vs video-tile spatial (Kumospace) vs (positioning-only) minimal presence-first (Tandem). The map's visual style is implementation, not invariant.
- **Audio realization**: distance-attenuated spatial audio vs room-membership audio vs proximity falloff — the invariant is co-location determining the connection, not any specific acoustic model.
- **Audience**: company office (dominant) / education (Sococo classrooms, oVice academic) / community (oVice community) / events & socials (Gather event spaces, Kumospace virtual events).
- **Scale**: small teams → thousand-person multi-floor buildings (oVice customer quote).
- **Function-specific floors**: sales floors (Teamflow), classrooms, event halls.
- **Delivery**: browser-only (Sococo) vs desktop app (Gather/Teamflow/Kumospace/oVice) vs mobile (oVice Go, Kumospace).
- **Pricing**: per-seat (Sococo) vs space-based (oVice) vs freemium (Kumospace) vs trial+plans (Gather/Teamflow).
- **Enterprise posture**: SSO, domain-based membership, admin controls (Gather, Kumospace).
- **Analytics depth**: none → plan-gated dashboards → management "walk the floor" framing.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check (§24)

Would older, regional, or differently positioned products still fit the three-leg L0?

- **Sococo (2010s generation, the sample's oldest)**: floor-plan map + rooms + avatars + Get/Knock — satisfies all three legs with **no game-like rendering, no spatial audio, no app-status, no mobile apps**. This is the decisive §24 result: the game-like map and spatial audio cannot be definitional.
- **The pre-software practice**: a company's physical office — a standing place with rooms and desks, "who's at their desk" knowledge, walking over to talk — is the conceptual ancestor. The Type digitizes the standing workplace; the definition does not require any specific rendering, acoustic model, or always-on video (Gather: mic/camera off by default; "Be present without being on camera").
- **Regional pole (oVice, Japan/Korea)**: same core under different market packaging. ✓
- **Presence-first minimal-spatial pole (Tandem)**: positioning confirms the category self-label; structural fit unverified (source limitation) — recorded as an uncertainty, not evidence.
- **Room-grid vs desk-grid**: Sococo's room-based model (no personal desks in the fetched material) satisfies the core with rooms alone → personal desks are common, not definitional.

Conclusion: the definition is not over-fit to the Gather-shaped game-map present. The one deliberate abstraction vs the naive market image: the defining property is **co-location in a standing workplace-shaped place with continuous presence**, not any specific visual/acoustic implementation.

## Vendor-specific / Rejected Findings

Rejected from the canonical core (with reasons):

- **Game-like top-down map with movable avatars** — Gather's signature, but Sococo's schematic floor plan and Kumospace's video-spatial model satisfy the core; the map style is implementation. L2.
- **Spatial audio (distance attenuation)** — Kumospace markets it as the headline; Gather has proximity audio; Sococo is room-based. The invariant is co-location-determined connection. L2.
- **Always-on video** — no product requires it; Gather defaults mic/camera off and markets "be present without being on camera". L1/L2.
- **Desks** — very common (Gather Tier-1; visually in Kumospace/Teamflow/oVice) but Sococo's room-based model lacks them in the fetched material; the room is the more universal unit. L1.
- **App-status / calendar-driven status** — Gather-specific depth; presence states generally are L1, app-level status L2/L3.
- **Embedded apps/whiteboards/games** — conversation furniture; present in 3 of 5 sampled; not definitional. L1/L2.
- **People analytics** — 3 of 5, plan-gated; management framing, not the office core. L1/L2.
- **Events/socials/games** — variant use of the same core; when the audience becomes public and one-off, the product drifts toward event-platform/social-VR territory. L2.
- **Multi-mode packaging** (Kumospace selling meetings/webinar/chat beside the office) — suite packaging around the office core. L2/L3.

## Boundary Findings

### vs Team Workspace Platform — DISCHARGES the joint-review flag from this side

The team-workspace pass expected this leaf as the "spatial-presence pole, distinct (presence-space vs assembly-container)". **Confirmed and ratified: keep-both.**

- Team Workspace Platform's core: a persistent team-scoped container hosting a **multi-surface assembly** (conversations + files + tools + meetings); the container-as-assembly is the product; the user's home surface is the conversation stream/app rail.
- Virtual Office Workspace's core: a persistent workplace-shaped **place** with continuous presence and co-location conversation; the place-and-people is the product; the user's home surface is the space/map itself.
- Remove tests both directions: remove the spatial presence from a virtual office → you get an assembly container (team workspace). Remove the assembly of surfaces from a virtual office → **still fully recognizable as a virtual office** (a bare office with rooms, presence, and walk-over talk is the Type's cleanest form — Gather's core is exactly that). Neither core subsumes the other.
- Overlap zone: workspace products add "rooms"/huddle spaces and virtual offices embed apps/whiteboards — convergence recorded, but the organizing principle (presence-in-a-place vs surfaces-in-a-container) and the home surface (the space vs the stream/rail) discriminate.

### vs Video Conferencing / Virtual Meeting Platform — DISCHARGES the virtual-meeting-platform pass's forwarded flag

That pass rejected the "spatial-venue" reading of its own leaf and forwarded the venue population here, noting "the office venue is a different Type from the convened meeting, and products sell both as separate modes." **Confirmed with direct evidence**: Kumospace's own navigation separates "Online Meetings — run productive virtual meetings" from "Virtual Office" as different product modes; Gather's Meeting Overview separates "hold regular meetings like Zoom or Google Meet" from "instantly talk to people around you"; Sococo/oVice integrate external meeting tools rather than replacing the office with them.

- Discriminator: the convened meeting is a **session object** with its own address, joined by invitation; the virtual office is a **standing place** where conversations form by co-location without a call object. A virtual office can host convened meetings (as one behavior inside rooms); a meeting product has no persistent place or ambient presence.
- Keep-both ratified; the two Types are different frames that legitimately co-exist in one vendor's portfolio.

### vs Team Messaging Application

Messaging's core is persistent async text threads; the virtual office's core is live co-located conversation in a standing place. Chat exists inside virtual offices as an auxiliary surface; conversation history is not the unit of record here. Remove live co-located conversation → messaging; remove async threads → office.

### vs Collaborative Workspace

Content-centric container (authored items) vs presence-centric place. A virtual office holds no content tree; its "content" is furniture and embedded surfaces serving conversations. Distinct without joint review.

### vs Virtual Classroom

Education is an audience variant here (Sococo classrooms, oVice academic). When pedagogy machinery (class rosters, attendance, hand-raising as class control, structured breakout as class mechanism) becomes the center, the product is Virtual Classroom territory. The office core with a classroom skin stays in this Type.

### vs Event platforms / Social VR

The same place+presence+proximity core serves events and socials (Gather event spaces, Kumospace virtual events). Variant while the space serves a standing team or a recurring community; drift toward event-platform/social-VR territory when the space is one-off, public, and entertainment-centered.

### Naming collision — "virtual office" in commercial real estate

In real-estate services, a "virtual office" is a service package (business address, mail handling, phone answering) sold by coworking operators — recorded in the coworking-flexible-workspace-management pass as a business line ("Deliveries/mail (virtual office supply chain)"). That is a **service offering managed by Coworking/Flexible Workspace Management software**, not this software Type. The collision is terminological only; no Type overlap. Recorded for the taxonomy owner.

### vs Digital Whiteboard / Diagramming

Whiteboards appear as in-space furniture/surfaces serving conversations; the whiteboard canvas is not the product's organizing structure. Distinct.

## Final Synthesis

A Virtual Office Workspace is defined by three jointly-held structures:

```text
The persistent workplace-shaped place
  (standing space with rooms/zones + personal spots; persists across days;
   editable as a whole by its owners)
└── Continuous embodied presence
    (each member visible at a position while they work, carrying availability;
     the place shows who is in, where, and doing what)
    └── Co-location-initiated live conversation
        (audio/video forms by entering the same room or coming near —
         or by an in-place pull that brings the person's presence to you —
         and ends when participants leave; no dialed call object)
```

The Type's organizing principle: **the office as a standing digital place that carries the team's ambient presence and turns co-location into conversation** — in deliberate contrast with Team Workspace Platform (a container assembling work surfaces), Video Conferencing (the convened call as the unit), and Team Messaging (async threads as the unit).

Standard capabilities layered on the core: personal desks, availability/status layers, meeting rooms with calendar scheduling, screen sharing (often multiple simultaneous), in-space work surfaces (whiteboards, embedded apps, games), text chat, guest access, member/guest roles with administration, privacy controls (locking, private rooms/desks, mute-by-default), integrations (calendar/chat/app-status), usage analytics, audio/video controls.

Variants: spatial rendering style (game map / floor plan / video-spatial / minimal), audio realization (spatial / proximity / room-based), audience (company / education / community / events), scale (team → multi-floor enterprise), delivery (browser/desktop/mobile), pricing philosophy, enterprise posture, function-specific floors (sales floors, classrooms).

Both prior flags are discharged: keep-both vs Team Workspace Platform on the presence-place vs assembly-container seam; keep-both vs Virtual Meeting Platform on the standing-place vs convened-session seam, with Kumospace's own multi-mode navigation as direct evidence that the market sells the two as separate products.
