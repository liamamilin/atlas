## Research Goal

Understand the Application Type **Fundraising Management Platform** (§25) from real products: what the organization-side "fundraising program machinery" actually consists of (initiatives/campaigns/events/auctions as managed units, donor-facing capture machinery, gift/transaction records, tracking), who does what (fundraising staff vs donors vs supporters), how a fundraising initiative runs end-to-end, which rules govern money and records, and where the Type sits in the §25 fundraising family grid against its four processed siblings (Donor Management System, Online Donation Platform, Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform, Peer-to-peer Fundraising Platform), plus University Advancement Platform, Nonprofit Event Management, Nonprofit Management Platform, and grantmaking.

## Initial Boundary

Working hypothesis before research (inherited from the sibling passes' family grid):

- The four §25 fundraising leaves form a grid on two axes: **who runs the fundraiser** (the organization vs its supporters) × **record center** (the transaction vs the campaign vs the whole program). This leaf holds the **org-side program machinery** cell: the system the fundraising office uses to plan, run, and track its fundraising activity across initiatives.
- Likely users: development/fundraising staff (primary), donors and event guests (donor-facing surfaces), finance (money handoff).
- Nearest confusion risks: Donor Management System (constituency database), Online Donation Platform (standing give capability), Nonprofit Crowdfunding (public campaign container), P2P (supporter-run pages), Nonprofit Event Management (event logistics center), University Advancement (higher-ed constituency/pipeline), Nonprofit Management Platform (umbrella suite).
- Unknowns: what the managed unit of fundraising work actually is in the market (campaign? event? auction? all?); whether multi-mechanic composition is definitional; how deep the native donor record goes; money-flow posture; whether a tracking loop is definitional or merely universal.

## Research Questions

1. What do vendors that sell "fundraising software/platform" actually center on — what is the managed unit of fundraising work?
2. What initiative types exist (campaigns, events, auctions, appeals, raffles, P2P, giving days) and how are they composed?
3. What donor-facing capture machinery does the platform operate (forms, giving sites, ticketing, bidding, text)?
4. What happens to a transaction — recording, receipts, money destination?
5. What does the org-side console do (setup, configuration, donor records, reporting, tracking across initiatives)?
6. How deep is the native donor record, and how does it connect to donor-management systems and accounting?
7. Where is the line vs the four §25 siblings, event management, advancement, and umbrella suites?

## Canonical Abstraction Hierarchy

### L0 — Defining Invariant (candidate, minimal)

Three jointly-held structures:

1. **The fundraising initiative as the org-operated unit of fundraising work.** The organization's fundraising staff create, configure, and run defined fundraising initiatives — campaigns, events, auctions, appeals — inside the platform: each initiative carries its own page/branding, timing (start/end, event date), goal, and a composition of fundraising mechanics. The initiative is the container the staff works on; the portfolio of initiatives is the fundraising program.
   - Remove → a donation form builder (no initiative container) or a donor database (no fundraising work).
2. **The donor-facing capture machinery operated by the organization.** The platform provides and operates the surfaces through which donors participate and give — donation forms/pages, giving sites, event registration/ticketing, auction bidding, raffle/sweepstakes entries, text-to-give — each bound to an initiative, with every transaction recorded and the money settling to the organization.
   - Remove → a planning/tracking tool with no money loop; without the initiative binding → the standing give capability of the Online Donation Platform.
3. **The program-level tracking loop.** The organization tracks performance across initiatives and over time — totals against goals, donor activity, per-initiative results — and uses it to steward donors and plan the next round. The tracking is at the initiative and program level, not just per-transaction.
   - Remove → a payment/capture processor with no program view.

Jointly-held load-bearing: 1 alone = an event/campaign planner with no money; 2 without 1 = the standing give capability (Online Donation Platform territory); 3 without 1+2 = fundraising analytics over imported data; 1+2 without 3 = capture machinery with no program memory; 1+3 without 2 = a plan nobody gives through; 2+3 without 1 = a donation platform's console.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure (B-evidence)

- **Multi-mechanic composition** inside one initiative: auctions (live/silent/mobile bidding), raffles, sweepstakes, appeals/paddle raises/fund-a-need, ticketed entry, merchandise stores, P2P overlays — composed and stacked (RallyUp's Fundraising Stack is the explicit form; OneCause's event feature inventory the packaged form).
- **Donor-facing pages/sites** with org branding; giving sites; personal fundraising pages (org-context P2P as a module).
- **Ticketing/registration** with QR check-in, tables/seating, promo codes, order forms; contactless check-in/out; event balances.
- **Sponsorship packages and activation** (sell packages online, logo tiers, impression tracking).
- **Donor records** (light-to-medium) with CRM integrations/handoff (Salesforce-class) and accounting handoff; wealth screening in the CRM-heavy pole.
- **Receipts and tax documentation**; thank-you machinery (automated and personal).
- **Text-to-give** as a channel; livestream/virtual/hybrid event support; live leaderboards/scoreboards/thermometers.
- **Reporting/dashboards** per initiative and across the program; analytics feeding the next cycle.
- **In-platform payment processing** (common posture; donor-covers-fees option in the family).
- **Services layer**: campaign experts, done-for-you campaign building, onsite event support, training — a services-heavy posture typical of this Type.
- **AI assistance** (item descriptions, content generation, forecasting) — era-typical.

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- **Center-of-gravity poles**: event/auction-heavy (OneCause, GiveSmart) vs online-campaign/CRM-heavy (Funraise) vs multichannel mechanics stack (RallyUp) — packaging, not identity.
- **Initiative-type mix**: galas, golf events, runs/walks/rides, giving days (#GivingTuesday), raffles, sweepstakes, a-thons, storefronts, DIY supporter campaigns, tributes/memorials, membership drives, capital campaigns (GiveSmart blog evidence at positioning level).

- **Donor-record depth**: donor profiles (RallyUp) → donor-management feature (OneCause) → separate donor-CRM product line (GiveSmart) → native full nonprofit CRM (Funraise) — a gradient toward donor-management-system territory at the far end.
- **Customer-tier packaging**: enterprise / mid-size / small & grassroots / schools / corporate giving (RallyUp explicit); K-12 and faith-based verticals (client evidence).
- **Business model**: annual subscription with unlimited fundraisers (GiveSmart), suite license (OneCause/Bonterra), free tier + fees (Funraise free signup), no-contract/no-subscription (RallyUp).
- **Regional/legal regimes**: raffles and sweepstakes are regulated by jurisdiction; compliance posture is product-level (not asserted in depth).

## Vendor-specific vs Rejected Findings

- **Rejected: "fundraising management = donor management."** The donor pass's market-internal evidence (DonorPerfect's own FAQ separating the two) is confirmed from this side by packaging: GiveSmart ships the donor CRM as a separate product/login; Funraise ships the CRM as one feature group beside Fundraising; OneCause ships Donor Management as one feature; RallyUp ships donor profiles + CRM integrations. The constituency-cultivation center belongs to donor-management-system.
- **Rejected: "fundraising management = payment processing."** Money mechanics are common (in-platform processing) but not the center; the family's BYO-processor pole (Raisely/Give Lively) shows the platform can hold the capture/record/console layer without being the processor of record. Consistent with the donation and donor passes.
- **Rejected: "the campaign here = the crowdfunding campaign."** The crowdfunding sibling's center is the public crowd-facing campaign container with social-proof machinery. Here the initiative is the org-side managed unit — it may be a public giving site, a gala, a raffle, or an internal appeal; the crowd dynamic is one variant, not the center. RallyUp ships Crowdfunding as one Component among many — module evidence.
- **Rejected: "events/auctions are the definition."** They are the dominant pole (OneCause, GiveSmart) but RallyUp centers online mechanics stacks and Funraise centers online campaigns + CRM; the initiative vocabulary (campaign, event, auction, appeal, raffle, giving day) is broader than any one mechanic.
- **Rejected: "P2P/supporter pages are part of the definition."** P2P appears as a module/solution family in all four sampled products; the supporter-run page center belongs to peer-to-peer-fundraising-platform.
- **Rejected: precise operational claims** (fee percentages, payout timing, thermometer uplift figures, conversion rates, revenue-growth stats) — observed only as single-product marketing figures; kept L3, not asserted in the final document.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Donor Management System (§25, processed — DISCHARGES its joint-review flag).** Keep-both confirmed from this side. The seam is the **center**: the DMS's center is the org-side constituency database (constituent → attributed gifts → cumulative history → cultivation work); this Type's center is the **fundraising program machinery** (initiatives run + capture operated + program tracking). Market-internal evidence aligns: the donor pass recorded a fundraising vendor's own FAQ separating "managing donor relationships and supporter data" from "running campaigns, processing donations, tracking performance"; this pass records the packaging gradient (donor profiles → donor-management feature → separate donor-CRM product → native CRM) — donor records here are capture-side and program-side, deepening toward DMS territory only at the Funraise pole. Removal tests: strip the initiative/campaign machinery from a DMS → still donor management (the donor pass's own removal test); strip the constituency-cultivation depth from this Type → fundraising program machinery remains. **Verdict: keep both Types.**
2. **vs Online Donation Platform (§25, processed — DISCHARGES its flag).** Keep-both confirmed. The donation platform's center is the **standing, evergreen donor-facing give capability** (transaction of record; no campaign container needed); this Type's center is the **initiative-bound program machinery** (campaigns/events/auctions as managed units composing mechanics, tracked as a program). Removal tests re-run from this side: strip the initiatives (campaigns/events/auctions) from a sampled product → a standing donation capability remains (every sampled product ships evergreen donation forms/pages); strip the standing give capability → the program machinery remains (event/auction-only poles are market-real in the family). Suite overlap is real and bidirectional: donation platforms ship campaign/P2P modules (sibling-pass evidence); fundraising platforms ship donation forms/pages (this pass's evidence). **Verdict: keep both Types; transaction-center vs program-center discriminator stands.**
3. **vs Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform (§25, processed — DISCHARGES its forward flag).** Keep-both confirmed. The crowdfunding sibling's center is the **public crowd-facing campaign container** (story-bearing public page, social-proof progress machinery, share-out dynamics). Here the campaign is **one managed initiative type within the org's program** — the center is the org's operation of the whole program, not the public crowd dynamic of one container. RallyUp ships Crowdfunding as a Component; OneCause ships giving days as a P2P campaign type — module evidence both ways. Crowdfunding platforms ship org-side slices (donor records, reports, email — sibling-pass suite-drift note). **Verdict: keep both Types; the family grid's campaign-of-record vs program-machinery cells stand.**
4. **vs Peer-to-peer Fundraising Platform (§25, processed — DISCHARGES its flag).** Keep-both confirmed. The P2P center is the **supporter-held page + delegated solicitation** (page-owner = supporter; the supporter does the asking). Here the organization runs the fundraiser; supporter pages appear only as modules that roll up to the org (OneCause Personal Fundraising Pages + P2P solution family; RallyUp P2P Component "used to promote every kind of fundraiser"; Funraise P2P feature; GiveSmart P2P solution). **Verdict: keep both Types; page-owner/solicitor discriminator stands.**
5. **vs University Advancement Platform (§23, processed — DISCHARGES its forward flag).** Keep-both confirmed. ## §24 Historical / Market-Sample Check

- **Development-office analog (paper era)**: a fundraising office runs a program — the annual appeal calendar, the capital campaign, the gala with a silent auction, the golf tournament, the raffle; each initiative is planned (goal, date, theme), run (reply envelopes, pledge cards, bid sheets, ticket sales), and tracked (campaign thermometer, ledger, tote board); gifts are logged per giver with receipts issued; results feed the next year's plan. Satisfies all three L0 legs at analog level: initiatives as managed units, operated capture machinery, program-level tracking. ✓
- **The church bazaar / school fair**: the same structure at small scale — a defined fundraising occasion composing sales, raffles, and donations, tracked by the organizers. ✓
- **Early web era**: a charity running a giving-day microsite + an auction page + a donate form, tracked in a spreadsheet — legs 1–2 thin, leg 3 manual; the mature platform folds the tracking loop into the product. The definition names no specific implementation. ✓
- Era-bound machinery correctly excluded from L0: QR check-in, livestreams, AI content generation, wealth screening, event balances, scoreboards, text-to-give, done-for-you services.

## Final Synthesis

A **Fundraising Management Platform** is the organization-side program machinery of nonprofit fundraising: the fundraising office creates, configures, and runs a portfolio of defined fundraising initiatives — campaigns, events, auctions, appeals, raffles, giving days — each carried by the platform as a managed unit with its own page, timing, goal, and a composition of fundraising mechanics; the platform operates the donor-facing capture machinery (donation forms and giving sites, ticketing and registration, auction bidding, raffle entries, text-to-give) bound to those initiatives; every transaction is recorded as a gift or participation purchase, money settles to the organization, and receipts/tax documentation are issued; and the organization tracks performance across initiatives and over time — totals against goals, donor activity, per-initiative results — to steward donors and plan the next round. The defining core is small (initiative as org-operated unit + operated capture machinery + program tracking loop) and survives the historical check: the development office's calendar of appeals, galas, and campaigns — run with reply envelopes, bid sheets, and a wall thermometer — satisfies the same three-legged core, so the definition names no digital implementation. Everything else — mechanic stacking, sponsor packages, event logistics, live displays, text-to-give, donor records of varying depth, services layers, AI — is mature common structure or variant. Where the record center sits separates it from its siblings: the transaction (online donation), the public crowd campaign (crowdfunding), the supporter-run page (P2P), the constituency database (donor management), the advancement pipeline (university advancement), and the event's logistics (event management).
