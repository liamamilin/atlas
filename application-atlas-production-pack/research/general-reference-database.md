# Research Notes — General Reference Database

Research date: 2026-09-07
Slug: general-reference-database
Directory leaf: General Reference Database (§02.05 Reference & General Knowledge)

---

## Research Goal

Understand what a General Reference Database is as an Application Type: what its corpus and unit of content look like, how lookup works across works, what surfaces users operate, what rules govern access and content, and where the boundary lies with neighboring reference Types (Online Encyclopedia, Dictionary Application, Knowledge Graph Explorer) and with adjacent delivery Types (Digital Library Platform, Academic Search Engine, Public Data Portal, Information Portal).

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Core use: consult authoritative background/factual content by searching or browsing a collection of reference works; retrieve the relevant entry/article with its source attribution.
- Name reading: "reference database" is library-science usage — a database *of reference material* — not a structured-data/query database. The corpus is editorial prose (entries, articles, chapters), not datasets or tables.
- Users: students, librarians, general readers, researchers; access typically mediated by an institution (school, university, public library).
- Nearest neighbors: Online Encyclopedia (one work vs many), Dictionary Application (lexical entries vs topical entries), Knowledge Graph Explorer (entity graph vs document corpus), Digital Library Platform (whole-book delivery vs entry retrieval), Academic Search Engine (periodical literature vs reference works), Public Data Portal (datasets vs prose), Information Portal (links to external content vs held content).
- Open questions: is this Type distinct from Online Encyclopedia or merely its multi-work variant? Where does the eBook-platform pole (Gale eBooks) sit relative to Digital Library Platform? Is "general" (multidisciplinary scope) definitional or scope-only?

## Research Questions

1. What is the unit of content (entry / article / chapter / topic page), and what does it contain?
2. What is the corpus structure — how are works organized, how many, what work types, whose works?
3. How does lookup work — cross-work search, faceting, subject browse, A–Z, visual/associative aids?
4. What entry-level machinery exists — cross-references, related entries, media, citation data?
5. What user features exist — saving, folders/packs, sharing, persistent links, print/download, accessibility?
6. What access models exist — free web, subscription, institutional (IP / library card / barcode / SSO / access codes), personal?
7. What institution-side machinery exists — collection curation, administration, MARC records, LMS/discovery integration, usage reporting?
8. Who are the users and in what context do they consult the database?
9. Where are the boundaries with encyclopedia / dictionary / digital library / academic search / data portal / portal Types?

## Product Observations

### Gale eBooks (Layer A — official product page + official training transcript)

Positioning and corpus:

- "Gale eBooks provides unlimited 24/7 access to nonfiction eBooks purchased through Gale." The institution buys titles and owns them ("you have them forever… pay that small hosting fee each year").
- Pre-made topical collections offered (examples named in training: America at 250, financial literacy, African American studies).
- Unlimited simultaneous access: "no holds or returns"; entire class or book club can read the same title at once; mobile responsive.
- The platform is per-institution: "yours is going to be unique to mine, depending on your collection that you have built."

Institution-side machinery:

- Bookshelf Curation: a staff login to organize how titles are shelved and displayed to patrons/students; weed collections; create custom collections (dedicated tip sheets/tutorials exist: "Create Custom Collections With the Bookshelf Curation Tool", "Organize Your Collection with the Bookshelf Curation Tool", "Weed Your Collection").
- Gale Admin (institution administration), Gale Usage Portal / Usage Dashboard (usage reporting), MARC records and title lists on the support site (library-system integration).
- Purchase flow documented ("Purchase eBooks Through the Gale eBooks Platform" tip sheet).

Search and retrieval:

- Basic search operates at the **chapter level** and "cross-searches my entire eBook collection", returning chapters that mention the term — "very reminiscent of that database type searching".
- Results list chapters; a side panel shows the titles they come from; filters on the right use subject indexing terms (example from transcript: within "machine learning" — algorithms, robots, privacy, data security).
- Get Link tool produces a "persistent URL, meaning it's never going to break" — to a chapter or to a search-results page with filters applied.
- Topic Finder: a visual tile/heat-map tool that groups results into topics; selecting a tile shows results linking to chapters.

Reading surfaces:

- Two views for every e-book: **book view** ("high-quality scanned-in image of the book", two-page mode, zoom, full screen, page skipping) and **text view** (same content reflowed, with the accessibility toolbar).
- Book landing page: table of contents, citation information about the publication, an index of the title, a list of illustrations; "View the eBook" opens the whole book.

Accessibility:

- Text view: translate into 50+ languages; read-aloud (ReadSpeaker) including in translated languages; MP3 download of the audio; font size, font choice (OpenDyslexic named), spacing, background color; page masking; keyboard shortcuts; readability settings persist via device cookies.
- UI itself can be translated (separate from content translation).

Annotation, export, sharing:

- Highlights and notes can be added to text; **they do not persist in the platform** — they "time out after 20 minutes of inactivity" (product-specific number); users are told to save/export as they go.
- Export ("Send to") to Google Drive or Microsoft OneDrive creates a platform-named folder; the exported chapter carries highlights, notes, and the source citation. Download chapter as PDF (includes highlights/notes); print at chapter level; share via email. "Unlimited download, unlimited print, unlimited shares."
- Google/Microsoft sign-in is one-way (save content out; the vendor cannot see user data). Google Classroom integration; LMS embeds for Canvas and Schoology documented as tutorials.
- Cite tool (Gale Tools — Cite tutorial) generates citations.

Convergence observed across the sample (Layer B):

- A curated corpus of multiple reference works is the managed object in every product, whatever the ownership model.
- Retrieval is entry/article/chapter-level, never whole-work checkout; every retrieved unit carries its source work (title/citation) — citation machinery is universal where documented.
- One search box spanning the corpus is the signature interaction; faceting/filtering (subject, work, type, era) is the common refinement pattern.
- Access is institution-mediated (library card, access codes, SSO, IP) in every product that documents access; personal accounts exist for saving/sharing, not for owning the corpus.
- LMS and library-discovery integration is universal across the documented products.
- Editorial authority ("expert-reviewed", "verified", "trusted") is the universal positioning axis — the products sell trust, not comprehensiveness of the open web.

Divergences (implementation variance):

- Who owns the corpus (publisher / aggregator / institution).
- Content form (born-digital text vs page images vs both).
- Whether search extends beyond the corpus (federated) or stays closed.
- Whether an AI answer layer sits on top.
- Whether non-reference genres (journals, news, primary sources, video) are bundled.

## Canonical Model (four-layer abstraction)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

Three properties. Remove any one and the product is no longer recognizable as a General Reference Database:

1. **A curated corpus of multiple reference works** — the managed object is a collection of distinct, editorially compiled reference works (encyclopedias, dictionaries, handbooks, directories, quotation collections, atlases, almanacs), each identified by title and source. Remove the multi-work corpus → a single-work encyclopedia or dictionary (different Type). Remove the reference-work nature → a periodical/article database or a web index.
2. **Entry-level retrieval with source attribution** — the unit users find, read, and take away is the entry/article/chapter, always presented as belonging to a named work so it can be evaluated and cited. Remove entry-level retrieval (deliver whole works) → an eBook lending platform / digital library. Remove attribution → a content index, not a reference source.
3. **Cross-work lookup as the primary interaction** — one search or one browse path spans the entire corpus and returns matches from many works side by side. Remove it → a shelf of separate single-work sites or a portal of links.

The reference function itself — consultation for background and factual context, not cover-to-cover reading, not conversational answering — is the purpose frame that binds the three structures.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

Present in most mature modern products; not required to recognize the Type:

- faceted search refinement (subject, work/title, publication type, era, reading level)
- subject browse paths and A–Z indexes alongside search
- entry-page machinery: cross-references / related entries, images and media
- citation generation and export to citation managers
- personal saving/collecting (saved entries, folders, resource packs) under a personal account
- sharing/export: persistent links, email, PDF download, print
- accessibility: text-to-speech, translation, display controls
- institutional access machinery: library card / barcode / access codes / SSO / IP
- LMS integration (LTI, Google Classroom, embeds) and library-discovery indexing; MARC records
- institution-side administration and usage reporting
- reading-level or audience tiering of the corpus

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

Depends on segment, ownership model, era, or business model:

- corpus ownership: single-publisher works vs multi-publisher aggregation vs institution-owned titles on a hosting platform
- audience tier: academic / school (K-12) / public library / family-consumer
- content form: born-digital HTML entries vs page-image fidelity vs dual views
- scope: general/multidisciplinary (canonical) vs subject-specific collections (same structure, narrowed corpus)
- access economics: free web vs subscription vs institutional license vs one-time purchase + hosting
- federated search beyond the corpus into the library's other databases
- visual/associative search aids (concept maps, topic tiles)
- AI answer layers over the corpus
- bundled adjacent genres: journals, news digests, primary-source archives, video, images
- platform-native / CD-ROM-era bundling of reference works (historical form)

### L3 — Vendor-specific Structure (Research Notes only)

- Credo: Mind Map; Credo Essentials subject sets; LTI 1.3 positioning; federated search as a headline feature.

## Rejected Findings

- **"Reference database = structured data/query database"** — rejected. The name is library usage for a database of reference *documents*. No sampled product exposes tables, queries, or datasets as its primary object. (Dataset-oriented consultation belongs to Public Data Portal / data-analytics Types.)
- **"Unlimited simultaneous access is definitional"** — rejected as L0; documented explicitly only in Gale eBooks. Kept as a strong L1/L2 observation of the consultation (non-lending) model, phrased cautiously.
- **"AI answering is part of the Type"** — rejected; only one sampled product documents an AI answer layer, and it is positioned as an addition to the corpus, not the corpus itself.
- **"Federated search is definitional"** — rejected; single-product headline feature (Credo).
- **"General (multidisciplinary) scope is definitional"** — rejected as structure; subject-specific reference collections share the identical structure with a narrowed corpus. Treated as a scope variant, with the leaf's canonical scope being general.

## Boundary Findings

- **vs Online Encyclopedia**: an encyclopedia is one reference work — its article corpus is the whole product. A reference database aggregates multiple works and crosses them. Test: remove the multi-work corpus (keep one encyclopedia) → Online Encyclopedia. Suite drift is real: Britannica Academic is encyclopedia-led but bundles thesaurus/world data/timelines/atlas/primary sources; the encyclopedia remains the center of gravity, so it stays encyclopedia-led rather than corpus-led. Conversely Credo and Gale eBooks have no single central work — corpus-led.
- **vs Dictionary Application**: dictionaries hold lexical entries looked up by word form; a reference database holds topical entries/articles retrieved by subject across works. Dictionaries commonly appear *inside* reference databases as works.
- **vs Digital Library Platform (eBook platform)**: the load-bearing test is the unit of delivery. Whole-work reading/lending with holds/returns → digital library. Entry-level cross-work consultation → reference database. Gale eBooks sits near this seam: it is marketed as an eBook platform, but its documented interaction (chapter-level cross-search, subject indexing, citation export, no holds/returns) is reference-consultation interaction over a reference/nonfiction corpus. Recorded as a boundary observation, not a taxonomy conflict.
- **vs Academic Search Engine / research databases**: those index periodical literature (journal articles, papers); a reference database holds reference works. Britannica Academic bundles journal articles — suite drift, encyclopedia/reference still the center.
- **vs Knowledge Graph Explorer**: that Type's object is entities and relations traversed as a graph; here the object is documents (entries) retrieved and read. A Mind Map is an associative aid over documents, not a graph database surface.
- **vs Public Data Portal**: datasets/files vs editorial prose entries. "World data references" and statistics inside a reference suite are entries *about* data, not data services.
- **vs Information Portal / Directory Application**: portals link out to external resources; a reference database holds the content itself.
- **vs Answer Engine**: answer engines synthesize responses; a reference database returns existing editorial entries. An AI layer (ASK Britannica) is capability drift on top of the corpus.

### Historical / Market-Sample Check

Would older, regional, platform-native products still fit the L0?

- CD-ROM-era bundled reference collections (a dictionary + thesaurus + encyclopedia + almanac + quotations + atlas on one disc, searched from one box) satisfy all three invariants: multi-work corpus, entry-level retrieval with source attribution, cross-work lookup. Platform-native reference bundles (e.g., OS- or office-suite-bundled reference shelves) likewise fit.
- Early web cross-search reference aggregators (the late-1990s/2000s "search many reference works at once" services, some free, some subscribed) fit the same structure with free-web access economics.
- The Gale eBooks platform itself is the continuation of a two-decades-old virtual reference library line (the product's own support slug and icon still carry the predecessor name) — the structure predates the current cloud/LMS packaging.
- None of the invariants depend on cloud delivery, LMS integration, AI, or any specific access technology. The historical check passes.

## Uncertainties

- In-platform behavior of Britannica Academic and World Book Online could not be observed (access-gated); their evidence is product-page + access-surface only. Claims about them are kept at positioning level.
- The free consumer aggregator pole (encyclopedia.com-class sites) was unreachable; its existence is common knowledge but no product-specific claims are made here.
- Whether institutions perceive Gale eBooks as "a reference database" or "an eBook platform" varies; this research treats the documented interaction pattern as decisive, but the boundary with Digital Library Platform deserves a joint review if that Type is processed later.
- Oxford Reference's current free/premium split and corpus size are unverified here; no numbers are stated anywhere in the outputs.
- Reading-level tiering is directly evidenced only as World Book's grade positioning and Britannica Library's marketing claim (sibling product); its depth inside products is unverified.

## Final Synthesis

A General Reference Database is a consultation system whose managed object is a **curated corpus of multiple reference works** and whose defining interaction is **cross-work lookup at entry level with source attribution**. One search or browse path spans encyclopedias, dictionaries, handbooks, directories, quotation collections, and atlases; the user retrieves the entry, reads it, and takes away a citable unit tied to its named source work. Everything else commonly present — faceting, subject browse, citation export, personal collections, persistent links, accessibility, institutional access machinery, LMS/discovery integration, curation tools, AI answer layers — is mature packaging around that core. The Type is distinct from the Online Encyclopedia (one work), the Dictionary Application (lexical entries), the Digital Library Platform (whole-work delivery), and the Academic Search Engine (periodical literature); its closest seam is the eBook-platform pole, where the entry-level cross-search interaction is the discriminator.
