# Research Notes — Friend Discovery Application

## Research Goal

Understand what a Friend Discovery Application is as an Application Type: the products whose organizing purpose is helping individuals form new **platonic friendships** with strangers, and how that purpose is realized in profiles, discovery surfaces, contact formation, and rules. Produce a vendor-neutral canonical model and hold the boundaries against Dating Application, Social Networking, Community, and Live Social Types.

Context carried in from prior processing: the dating-application pass recorded a straddle note — friend-discovery products share the dating skeleton (profile → discover strangers → interest → contact) with weaker consent gating and friendship intent, and one dating product embeds friend-finding as a first-class second mode. This pass must ratify or refute that note with direct evidence.

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Core use: find and make new friends (not partners) among strangers.
- Users: consumers, often in life transitions (new city, new parents, post-school, niche identities).
- Nearest Types: Dating Application (same loop, different intent), General/Interest-based Social Network (graph-anchored contact), Community/Interest platforms (container-anchored), Live Social (live rooms), IM (existing graph).
- Unknowns: consent-gating variance; discovery mechanics variance; group surfaces; monetization; safety machinery; whether the Type is stable or collapsing into dating/social products as modes.

## Research Questions

1. What is the core loop from profile to friendship?
2. What objects exist (profile, candidate, interest expression, connection, chat, groups)?
3. What drives discovery (proximity, interests, life stage, language, psychographics, algorithm)?
4. Is mutual consent required before contact? (the dating straddle note claims the gate is weaker here)
5. What safety/anti-harassment machinery exists, and how is the platonic frame enforced?
6. How does the friendship outcome materialize (chat only, IRL meeting, groups)?
7. What identity model (real photo/name vs pseudonym), age gates?
8. Monetization shapes?
9. Boundary vs dating: what exactly differs, and how do multi-mode products behave?
10. Historical/regional check: do pen-pal-style and other older/regional products fit the same definition?

## Product A — Bumble BFF (Bumble For Friends / BFF Mode)

### Key observations (Layer A unless noted)

**Positioning and packaging (multi-mode history):**
- Bumble historically carried friend-finding as **BFF Mode** inside the dating app; a separate **Bumble For Friends** standalone app existed; in 2025 (US) both were consolidated into a new standalone **BFF** app ("Bumble For Friends and BFF Mode have evolved into a more powerful and community-focused experience—BFF!"). Outside the US, BFF Mode / Bumble For Friends app still operate. (Changes article)
- BFF Mode inside Bumble: "Your BFF profile is separate from your dating profile, so you can tailor your bio, photos, and interests to focus on friendship." Switching modes via 'Type of connection' setting; "you'll only see profiles in that mode"; switching from Date to BFF removes existing matches. (Switching to BFF Mode)
- Law Enforcement Guidelines snippet: "Bumble for Friends (BFF) is the friendship app built to help you create authentic friendships."
- New BFF app: "designed specifically for friendship—with group chats, events, and features that help you find your people even faster"; "build deeper friendships, both one-on-one and in groups." Availability: US only right now.

**Core people-discovery loop:**
- "The People tab is designed to help you meet new friends in your area. You can scroll through profiles, view profile details, and decide who you'd like to connect with."
- Complete profile required before you can appear in or browse People Discovery.
- Discovery opt-out: 'Show me in People Discovery' toggle in privacy settings.
- Feed shows "all profiles in your area that match in some way"; filters by age and distance.
- Profile contents: photos, bio, interests, other shared details.
- **Wave** = interest expression (from card or profile; undoable; badge shown on your card to the recipient; incoming waves listed under 'Says Hi').
- **Match** = mutual wave → "you'll match and be able to start a conversation." No active "no" — "just keep scrolling."
- Conversation opens in **Chats tab**; new matches remain visible for 72 hours (product-specific number). DM deletion removes the message for both people.
- Report a profile from the three-dot menu.

**Group/community surfaces (the 2025 evolution):**
- Discover page tabs: **People** ("connect one-on-one… start individual friendships"), **Groups** ("shared interests, activities, or locations… connect with multiple people at once"), **Says Hi** (incoming waves), **Filters**.
- Groups machinery: subgroups, member applications ("get to know new members and make sure they're a great fit"), group rules members must agree to before joining, transfer/delete group, media gallery per group.
- Rooms: chat/post rooms, polls, **Video Rooms** (up to 16 people), **Audio Rooms** (up to 100 people) — group hangout surfaces.

**Rules and safety (Community Guidelines):**
- 18+ only; ID verification possible; underage banned.
- Authenticity: at least one profile photo depicting only you, full face clearly shown; name must be an authentic everyday representation (initials/nicknames OK, legal name not required); no celebrity/fictional names, no symbols/emojis as name, no social-media handles in profile.
- Platonic frame enforced by rules: no nude/sexual content; no commercial exchange of romantic or sexual activity; no "sugar" arrangements; no scams including "faking romantic intentions"; no unsolicited commercial/promotional use ("Our platform is not a marketplace").
- Harassment/bullying/hate prohibitions; "repeated unwanted contact" prohibited; doxxing prohibited.
- Off-platform conduct matters: "If we're made aware of harm between members on dates, meetups with friends, via text message or direct messaging platforms… we may take action as if it happened on our platform." (Note the phrase "meetups with friends" — IRL meeting is an expected product outcome.)
- Moderation: "human moderators and automated systems"; member reporting central; enforcement ladder: remove content → warning → ban (partial or across Bumble Inc. apps).
- Connections are called **friends**; there is a "remove the friend" action alongside Block & Report.

## Product B — We3

### Key observations

**Positioning:**
- "The smart way adults make friends. We3 privately matches you into groups of 3 — with people you just click with." "It's FREE • Not for Dating."
- "Two is a date. Three is a party." — the group-of-3 format is explicitly an anti-dating design: "Since you're always matched in groups, you don't have to worry about unwanted advances or someone's ulterior motives."
- Anti-swipe philosophy: "Stop swiping on people. Friendship is more than anyone's selfie game. Get matched on what matters."

**Mechanics (FAQ, Layer A):**
- Sign-up: valid email + profile photo + age + location required; Facebook/phone not required. Minimum age 13; under-16 needs parent/guardian authorization.
- Flow: "Step 1: You quiz yourself" (psychographic quizzes — taste, pet peeves, etc.) → "Step 2: We3 does the matching" (algorithm "incorporates hundreds of factors"; groups show "shared traits, mutual interests and common goals") → "Step 3: Chat & hang out" ("safely vibe in the group-chat before deciding to meet up. We encourage hanging out in the real world").
- Groups: "always 3 highly compatible people in a group chat. Users can have multiple groups." User initiates via "Match me in a group"; other members have 24 hours to join (product-specific number). Notification by push or SMS when a group is ready.
- Status control: **Invisible** — stop being added to new groups (distinct from account deletion).
- Profiles never public: "Only the users that you are compatible with will be able to see you." Visible: first name, age, profile picture, optional work/school/About Me. Not visible: email, last name, phone. "Be invisible to friends" (avoid being seen by people you already know).
- Messaging: text, photos, images, GIFs, emojis (video/location/contact card not supported at documentation time). 1:1 messaging inside a group requires the paid tier.
- Safety: report via info icon in group / ellipsis on profile / Pass flow; can leave a group and/or block someone.
- Matching policy: sensitive attributes (ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation) "don't play a role in most cases", but matches may be prevented to protect users from suspected abuse/intolerance.
- Monetization (**We3 Plus**): daily group limit 1→3; see closest matches and build groups from scratch; exclusively match with people who don't share your gender; match in a different city; message group members individually.

## Product C — Slowly

### Key observations

**Positioning:**
- "Penpals Reimagined… Match with someone that shares your passions, write a letter, and collect stamps from around the world."
- "Letters take time to be delivered — the farther away your pen pal lives, the longer it will take to reach them." (asynchronous, distance-scaled delivery — product-specific mechanic)
- "A nickname & an avatar is all you need… *No swiping involved here*." (pseudonymous identity, no photo-swipe)
- "Matches are based on language and common interests."
- Global, not proximity-based (distance is a feature, not a filter to minimize).

**Contact formation (help center, Layer A):**
- Two ways to add friends: **by Slowly ID** (requires friend-request acceptance; pending requests listed on Home) and **by Matchings** ("matching from Open Letters, Profiles, and Auto-match — you will receive their letters directly. There are no friend requests or acceptances needed").
- Discovery surfaces: Profile Suggestions, Auto-match (can be disabled), Open Letters (public letters as a discovery surface), Save for Later (profile bookmarking), search by Slowly ID.
- Daily matching limit exists; paid tier (Slowly Plus) increases it (title-level evidence).
- "Decline" button exists; friends can be removed (notification behavior documented); "I have too many friends!" article implies friend-count pressure is a managed concern.
- Presence hidden by design: "Last Online: Not Available" article.
- Verification: blue badge / verification flow exists.
- Letters have delivery check marks; photos shareable; letters cannot be edited after sending (title-level).

**Rules (Community Guidelines, Layer A):**
- "Slowly is not a dating app. Romantic or sexual advances should not be made right away. Build a genuine pen pal connection first, and ensure any approach is mutual and respectful." (platonic-first with a consent-gated romance exception — softer than BFF's blanket prohibition)
- "Asking to Switch to Instant Messaging in Initial Contact" prohibited — the product enforces its own communication medium and pacing.
- "Faking Location" prohibited — location authenticity matters to matching.
- Solicitation prohibited (money, gifts, jobs, loans, visas, crowdfunding, sugar relationships); advertising prohibited; multiple accounts prohibited; false reporting prohibited.
- Values framing: Authenticity, Kindness, Inclusivity, **Patience** ("you've signed up for a slower way to connect"), Thoughtfulness.
- Stamps: collectible by meeting new pals, travel, seasonal logins (gamification of friendship formation).

## Canonical Model (four-layer abstraction)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

1. **Friend-seeking profile** — the user maintains a persistent self-representation written to be evaluated as a potential friend (identity signals, interests, life context; photos or avatar).
2. **Stranger-candidate discovery** — the application surfaces specific unknown people (or places the user among them) as potential friends, selected by compatibility signals.
3. **Contact formation** — a path from discovery to a direct conversation channel with a candidate (interest expression, request, algorithmic placement, or first message).
4. **Friendship as the organizing intent** — the product's declared purpose and enforced rules aim at platonic friendship formation, not romantic pairing, community membership, or broadcast.

Remove #1 → anonymous/random chat. Remove #2 → messaging over an existing graph (IM). Remove #3 → a people directory without a formation path (not a friendship-formation application). Remove #4 → Dating Application.

Historical check: the pen-pal tradition (classified-ads pen-pal clubs, letter correspondents) realizes the same four properties without software; Slowly is its direct digital realization. ICQ-era user directories + chat fit. The definition does not depend on swipe, photos, proximity, or apps.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

- Compatibility inputs exposed to the user or the algorithm: interests, age, location/proximity, language, life-stage/identity attributes, quiz answers.
- Preference filters (age, distance) or their algorithmic equivalents.
- Incoming-interest inbox (waves/likes/requests/suggestions).
- Friends/connection list with management (remove, block).
- Chat layer with media (text, photos; richer media varies).
- Report/block safety machinery; moderation (human and/or automated).
- Profile authenticity rules and age gates.
- Discovery opt-out / visibility controls.
- IRL-meeting encouragement or safety guidance.

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- Consent gating: mutual-match gate (BFF) vs algorithmic placement with exit (We3) vs direct first-message with recipient decline (Slowly matching channels). **The gate is a spectrum, not an invariant** — this is the load-bearing difference from Dating Application, where the mediated contact gate was found definitional.
- Discovery interaction: swipe/wave deck vs scrollable feed vs no-browse algorithmic placement vs open-letter/public-content discovery.
- Group surfaces: interest/location groups, chat/post rooms, audio/video rooms (BFF's 2025 evolution) — secondary to the people loop in the sampled flagship; group-of-3 as the primary unit (We3).
- Identity substrate: real photo + everyday name (BFF) vs first name + photo (We3) vs pseudonym + avatar (Slowly).
- Age tier: 18+ (BFF) vs 13+ with guardian consent (We3).
- Monetization: freemium limits (daily matches/groups), subscriptions unlocking visibility, cross-gender/cross-city matching, 1:1 messaging (We3 Plus, Slowly Plus).
- Gamification (stamps), pacing mechanics (delivery time by distance).
- Packaging: standalone product vs mode embedded in a dating product (BFF Mode inside Bumble outside the US) vs mode embedded in a social product.
- Audience tuning: women-focused, parent-focused, teens, pen-pals, language learners (several poles under-researched — see Uncertainties).
- Regional availability (BFF app US-only at research time; BFF Mode elsewhere).

### L3 — Vendor-specific (research notes only)

- BFF: "wave" with undo and badges; 'Says Hi' tab; 72-hour new-match visibility; DM deletion for both sides; Rooms (video ≤16, audio ≤100); subgroups; member applications; group transfer; US-only availability; "Type of connection" mode switch that clears matches.
- We3: exactly-3 groups; 24-hour join window; SMS notification from a specific number; "Restart Levels"; "Tribes" naming; sensitive-attribute abuse-protection matching policy; We3 Plus feature list.
- Slowly: delivery time scaled by distance; stamps collection; Slowly ID; Sent:Received Ratio (title-level); Open Letters; Auto-match toggle; hidden last-online; prohibition on asking to switch to IM; Slowly Plus daily-limit increase.

## Vendor-specific Findings

See L3 above. Additionally: Bumble's multi-mode heritage (Date mode + BFF in one app, separate profiles per mode, mode switch clears matches) is vendor-specific packaging but evidences a general pattern: friend-finding can ship as a mode of a dating product with a separate profile space.

## Boundary Findings

- **vs Dating Application** — sharpest seam. Same skeleton (profile → discover strangers → interest → contact). Discriminators: (1) organizing intent — friendship vs romantic partnership, declared and enforced in rules (BFF: blanket sexual/romantic-commerce prohibitions; We3: "Not for Dating" as design; Slowly: "not a dating app" with consent-gated romance exception); (2) consent gating — definitional in dating, variable/spectrum here (mutual gate, algorithmic placement, direct letter). Multi-mode products (Bumble Date + BFF) are one product spanning two Types by mode, with separate profiles per mode. Test: remove the romantic orientation from a dating product → it becomes this Type; add it → dating. **The dating-application straddle note is ratified with direct evidence.**
- **vs General Social Network** — social networks center a persistent identity + feed + follow/friend graph; contact is graph-anchored and discovery of strangers is secondary. Friend discovery centers the candidate loop over strangers; there is no content feed as the primary surface in any sampled product. Test: remove the candidate loop → social network; remove the identity/feed graph → friend discovery.
- **vs Interest-based Social Network / Community Platform** — communities organize around a shared container (group, forum, interest) where relationships are a byproduct; friend discovery organizes around the person-match. BFF's Groups are a secondary surface inside a people-first product (the Discover page still leads with People). Test: if the container becomes primary and 1:1 matching dissolves → community Type.
- **vs Instant Messaging** — IM presupposes an existing reachability graph; friend discovery manufactures new edges. After formation, conversation continues in a chat layer, but edge formation is the product.
- **vs Live Social / Random Video Chat** — live rooms or anonymous random contact as the primary surface are different Types; no persistent friend-seeking profile → not this Type. (Yubo-class suspected straddle; not directly researched.)
- **vs Neighborhood Social Network** — place-based community container vs person-candidate loop; not directly researched; boundary held structurally.
- **Suspected straddle (unverified)** — language-exchange apps (HelloTalk/Tandem class): profile → discover partners → chat → friendship, with language practice as the framing. Fetch failed; recorded as an open question, not used in the model.
- **Market volatility** — Patook (prominent strictly-platonic product) domain parked/defunct; several sampled-category products unreachable. The Type's long tail is unstable; the flagship (BFF) is consolidating and bundling community surfaces.

## Uncertainties

1. BFF monetization specifics not directly observed (Bumble's paid-features articles cover the dating app; BFF-specific pricing unverified).
2. Women-focused (Peanut, Hey! VINA), parent-focused, Gen-Z-ecosystem (Wink/Hoop), and language-exchange poles under-researched due to unreachable sources; the sample is 3 products.
3. We3's current operational status and currency of its documented behavior unverified (site copy appears dated).
4. Slowly Sent:Received Ratio semantics unverified (article fetch failed; title-only).
5. Whether algorithmic placement (We3-style, no browse) remains a live pattern in the current market — only one sampled product documents it.
6. Group surfaces in friend discovery: only BFF documents them richly; whether they are becoming standard across the Type is unknown.

## Final Synthesis

A Friend Discovery Application is a consumer application whose organizing purpose is forming new platonic friendships between strangers. Its defining core is four properties: a friend-seeking profile; surfacing of specific stranger-candidates by compatibility signals; a path from discovery to direct contact; and friendship as the declared, enforced intent. Everything else — swipe mechanics, proximity, photos, mutual-consent gates, groups, subscriptions, gamification — is implementation or variant. The Type is real and distinct from Dating Application (intent + gate variance), from Social Networks (candidate loop vs graph center), and from Community platforms (person-match vs container). The market shows two structural pressures to watch: dating companies shipping friend modes (multi-mode products), and friend-discovery flagships bundling community surfaces — both are packaging drift, not core change, as of this research.
