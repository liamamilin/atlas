This pass must test whether the UGC-only venue is a distinct Type or a scope variant of Brand-Creator Marketplace, and discharge both flags.

## Research Goal

Understand what a "UGC Creator Marketplace" is as an Application Type: what the two sides actually exchange, what objects exist inside the system, how a content job flows from brief to paid delivery, which rules govern the exchange (rights, approval, payment protection), and where the boundary lies against the neighboring creator-economy Types.

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Working hypothesis: a two-sided marketplace where the **deliverable is ad-usable content** (photos/videos made to a brand brief, delivered as assets with usage rights) rather than **publication to the creator's own audience**. The creator's follower count is largely irrelevant; content-production ability is the qualifying attribute.
- Nearest neighbors: Brand-Creator Marketplace (sibling venue), Influencer Campaign Management (brand-side execution), Influencer Marketing Platform (brand-side program tool), Affiliate Network (commission deals), Service Marketplace (generic freelance), Creator CRM (single-sided).
- Known risk: the market straddles — hybrid products serve both UGC and influencer posting; the seam must be held on the deliverable axis, not vendor labels.

## Research Questions

1. What exactly is the deliverable — content assets, audience posts, or both? What role do usage rights play?
2. Who are the creators (audience size, professionalism, vetting) and what qualifies them?
3. What objects exist: profile, brief, order/job, submission, revision, rights, payment?
4. How does the flow work on both sides (brand loop and creator loop)? Where is the approval gate?
5. How is compensation handled (escrow, balance, wallet, payout) and is pricing negotiated or fixed?
6. Is posting to the creator's own audience part of the core or an optional add-on?
7. What variants exist (self-serve vs managed, pure UGC vs hybrid, business models, platform-native)?
8. Where are the boundaries vs Brand-Creator Marketplace, Influencer Campaign Management, Affiliate Network, Service Marketplace?

## Canonical Model (abstraction hierarchy)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

A two-sided venue whose defining core is four jointly-held structures:

1. **Creator supply as first-class marketplace participants** — individuals onboarded onto the platform as content producers, each holding a creator-side account (portal/app) through which they discover, receive, and work opportunities. Remove → brand-side content tooling or an agency roster; the venue disappears.
2. **Brand-side content demand** — a brand/agency specifies wanted content (a brief/order/campaign: what to shoot, for which product, in what styles/formats, by when). Remove → a creator portfolio site or directory.
3. **The content job binding one brand × one creator** — a bounded engagement carrying the deliverable spec and the compensation; the venue's unit of work. Remove → an unstructured directory or lead-gen list.
4. **Marketplace-mediated execution ending in brand-usable assets** — production → submission → brand review/approval → usage rights transferring to the brand → platform-executed compensation to the creator. Remove the mediation → classifieds; remove the rights/approval-to-payment path → influencer posting territory.

Jointly-held is load-bearing: (1) alone = creator portfolio community; (2) alone = a brief template; (3) alone = a contract template; (4) without 1–3 = a payment rail; 1+2+3 without 4 = a classifieds board; 2+3+4 without 1 = brand-side campaign tooling (Influencer Campaign Management).

**Negative invariant (what the core does NOT require):** publication to the creator's own audience. Audience size is not a qualifying supply attribute (explicitly waived in 3 of 4 sampled products; hybrid pole retains it only for its influencer side). Content-only delivery is the standard job; posting is an optional, separately priced layer.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

- Vetting/onboarding of creators (application review, certification, education, portfolio check)
- Creator profiles/portfolios with niches, ratings/reviews, past work; creator levels/tiers
- Dual consoles: brand web console + creator mobile app
- Structured briefs with templates/predefined options; deliverable menus (video types, photo types, formats/crops, hooks/CTAs)
- In-platform chat bound to the job; revision loops before approval
- Product logistics: free shipping or reimbursed purchase; products kept by the creator
- Payment protection: escrow/protected balance; wallet; payout on approval; contracts/invoices handled by the platform
- Fixed/no-negotiation pricing posture
- Standard usage-rights terms (full/perpetual rights as the default commercial promise)
- Raw + edited deliverables; multiple formats/crops for placements
- Performance/analytics layer (views, engagement, conversions/GMV, CTR/Hook Rate); spark-code/partnership-ads enablement for paid amplification
- Rehire/relationship continuity; creator success/support roles
- Managed-service layer; agency features (white label, multi-brand accounts)

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- Scope: pure UGC venue vs UGC+influencer hybrid (Insense pole; JoinBrands/Billo straddle via packages/add-ons)
- Initiation direction: creators apply to brand campaigns vs brands browse-and-hire directly vs both mechanisms in one product (JoinBrands ships both)
- Compensation form: cash per asset vs cash + kept product vs reimbursement purchase
- Business model: per-project/per-order, prepaid balance/packs, SaaS subscription, managed-service packages
- Distribution add-ons: organic posting, partnership ads/whitelisting, spark codes — optional, sometimes creator-priced
- Geography/eligibility restrictions (country lists, age, device requirements)
- Platform-native marketplaces (social-platform-operated) vs independent venues
- Agency white-labeling
- AI-generated creator content as an emerging supply category (listed as a creator type at one sampled product)
- Ad-destination breadth: paid social, CTV, Amazon/shoppable, email/web

### L3 — Vendor-specific (Research Notes only)

- Trend: set budgets; iOS-only, US-based creators; immediate PayPal payout on approval; creator waitlist; "no stolen or lost products" guarantee; soona merger context.
- Billo: prepaid packs/balance; twice-monthly PayPal payouts; Premium tier after 14 videos with strong stats; CreativeOps trained on "326k+ ads / $500M purchase value"; 30-day posting/partnership-ad windows; photos-as-a-service and crop add-ons.
- JoinBrands: escrow with refund guarantee; 5-day image / 10-day video upload deadlines after product receipt; "4 million creators" campaign-pool claim; per-asset earning figures ($60+/video, $15+/image, $100+/post); creator levels; tipping in chat; white-label domain.
- Insense: 48-hour application / 14-day delivery figures; "lifetime rights" recorded via Payments & Copyrights workflow; 100k+ creators across 35+ countries; education program; Meta/TikTok partner badges.

## Vendor-specific Findings

- The performance-data layer (CreativeOps-style brief/creator matching from ad outcomes) is strongest at Billo and structurally present at JoinBrands/Insense; absent from Trend's fetched pages. Held as common-mature, not definitional.
- Creator-set pricing for add-ons (Billo: "list these services, set your price") is a distinct compensation mechanic inside the fixed-price base — product-specific.
- JoinBrands' posting-required campaign formats (TikTok Jobs, Amazon Shoppables, etc.) show the hybrid edge: when the deliverable REQUIRES audience posting, the job is influencer-shaped even inside a UGC marketplace. The content-only package remains the UGC core.
- "AI Creators" as a supply category appears at JoinBrands; Billo explicitly markets the opposite ("human creators, not AI avatars"). Emerging axis, not settled.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Brand-Creator Marketplace (sibling §27 leaf) — the sharpest seam; joint-review flag DISCHARGED.** The venue test from that pass is satisfied here (all four sampled products give creators first-class participation: own portal/app + incoming opportunities + platform-executed compensation), so this leaf is a sibling venue Type, not a brand-side tool. The seam holds on the **deliverable axis**: Brand-Creator Marketplace's collaboration record centers on publication to the creator's own audience (reach is the product; audience attributes are first-class selection criteria), while this Type's job centers on **content assets delivered to the brand with usage rights** (audience attributes are irrelevant to the core; content-only is the standard job). Hybrid products (Insense; JoinBrands/Billo posting packages) straddle by selling both; the UGC-only pole is this leaf. Consistent with the prior pass's own framing ("if publication to the creator's own audience is removed entirely and the deliverable is purely an ad asset, the leaf becomes UGC Creator Marketplace").
2. **vs Influencer Campaign Management (§06, processed) — flag DISCHARGED.** That Type's defining object is the campaign container with per-creator participation records carried through a managed workflow; the venue question is absent (no creator-side marketplace participation as the core). UGC-campaign-shaped tools that give creators a venue to apply and get platform-paid belong here; brand-side campaign containers with content binding belong there. The "UGC-only deliverable = content-format variant there, ad-asset venue here" framing is confirmed from this side.
3. **vs Influencer Marketing Platform (§06 sibling)** — brand-side program system of record (creator database, relationship management, program measurement) vs two-sided venue. Sampled hybrid products ship marketplace modules inside platform positioning; the venue test (creator-side portal + platform-executed compensation) separates the marketplace core from the program layer.
4. **vs Affiliate Network / Affiliate Management** — compensation form: negotiated fee per content deliverable vs commission per conversion. Affiliate appears as an add-on module inside sampled products (Insense affiliate/TikTok Shop), not the core.
5. **vs Service Marketplace (§05.02, processed)** — generic freelance services vs marketing-content assets with standardized usage-rights transfer, product logistics, approval gates, and ad-performance context. A UGC marketplace is a domain-specialized venue; the seam is the deliverable semantics (brand-usable ad assets under platform-standard rights terms), not the marketplace mechanics themselves.
6. **vs Creator CRM / Creator Media Kit Builder** — single-sided creator tools; no brand-side demand or venue execution.
7. **vs stock-asset libraries (no directory leaf)** — library licensing of pre-existing assets vs per-brand commissioned production with product shipping and briefs. Different unit of sale.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check

The Type is young (sampled products founded ~2019–2021; the category rode the short-video/DTC ad wave). Checks applied:

- **Era check:** the four L0 legs do not require any TikTok-era machinery (spark codes, GMV dashboards, whitelisting, CTR analytics are all L1/L2). A pre-short-video "content creation marketplace" — brief-based commissioned photo/video from a vetted pool, rights transfer, platform-mediated payment — would satisfy the core. The definition does not freeze the Type to the 2020s DTC pattern.
- **Platform-native check:** a social platform operating its own branded-content/creator marketplace would satisfy legs 1–4 as long as the job is content-asset delivery with platform-executed compensation; if the platform's job is audience posting (branded content), it falls on the Brand-Creator Marketplace side of the seam.
- **Regional check:** eligibility restrictions (US/UK/CA/AU/DE in-sample) are L2; a regional venue with the same four legs qualifies.
- **Scale check:** "vetted network" is not definitional — an open-enrollment venue with the same four legs would still be this Type (vetting held at L1 because all four sampled products practice it but the anti-overfitting rule forbids promoting a shared implementation pattern to the invariant).

## Uncertainties

1. Whether a major open-enrollment (unvetted) UGC marketplace exists at scale — untested this pass; vetting held at L1 partly for this reason.
2. Insense's pricing/business-model mechanics (self-serve SaaS vs managed) were not fetched; no pricing claims asserted.
3. Revision-loop mechanics at Trend (whether brands can request reshoots/edits and how many rounds) — implied by the approval gate but not documented on fetched pages; not asserted.
4. The stability of the "AI creators" supply category — single-product signal, emerging.
5. Platform-native UGC marketplaces (e.g., social platforms' own creator marketplaces) were not directly sampled; the boundary is held structurally, not on direct evidence.

## Final Synthesis

The UGC Creator Marketplace is the **content-asset venue** of the creator economy: a two-sided marketplace whose unit of work is a content job binding one brand to one creator, executed through the platform from brief to approval, ending in brand-usable photo/video assets with usage rights transferred to the brand and compensation executed by the platform. Its defining negative invariant is equally important: publication to the creator's own audience is not part of the core — audience size is not a qualifying attribute, content-only delivery is the standard job, and posting/amplification are optional priced layers. Around this core, mature products add vetting, portfolios and levels, dual consoles (brand web + creator mobile app), structured briefs, in-platform chat and revisions, product logistics, escrow-style payment protection with fixed pricing, raw+edited multi-format deliverables, performance analytics with spark/partnership-ads enablement, rehire mechanics, managed services, and agency features. The market realizes the Type across four stable philosophies — curated vetted networks, performance-data-led ad factories, volume self-serve marketplaces, and UGC+influencer hybrids — and the seam against Brand-Creator Marketplace holds on the deliverable axis (ad asset vs audience publication), discharging both pre-hung joint-review flags from the sibling passes.
