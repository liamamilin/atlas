## Research Goal

Understand what an Estate Planning Application really is from real products: what objects exist inside it, who uses it, how a plan is created and maintained, what rules govern validity, and where its boundaries lie against legal document automation, financial planning software, and estate administration software.

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Core use: help a person (or their advisor/attorney) create and maintain an estate plan — wills, trusts, powers of attorney, healthcare directives, beneficiary designations — deciding what happens to assets, dependents, and decisions at death or incapacity.
- Likely users: individuals; financial advisors; estate planning attorneys; possibly executors/trustees.
- Neighboring types suspected: Financial Advisor Platform, Wealth Management Platform, Legal Document Automation, Law Practice Management System, estate/trust administration software (post-mortem), Retirement Planning Application.
- Unknowns: is post-mortem administration in-type? Is advisor-facing planning the same type as consumer D2C document creation? How deep does asset aggregation go? Is storage/vault definitional?

## Research Questions

1. What are the core objects? (plan, instruments, beneficiaries, fiduciaries, assets, guardians)
2. What is the primary workflow from start to legally effective documents?
3. What roles exist, and how does the product differ for consumer vs advisor vs attorney?
4. What lifecycle states do documents have (draft → review → sign → executed → amended/revoked)?
5. What jurisdiction rules shape the product (state-specific content, witnesses, notarization)?
6. What is the boundary vs legal document automation, vs financial planning suites, vs estate administration?

## Product A — FreeWill (consumer self-serve, free)

Evidence layer: A (directly observed on official site + help center).

Key observations:

- Product line (individuals): Last Will & Testament; Revocable Living Trust (offered for California only); Advance Healthcare Directive ("living will"); Durable Financial Power of Attorney; Beneficiary Designations (401(k)s, IRAs, life insurance — assets a will does not cover).
- Stated workflow (3 steps): (1) fill out online questionnaire step-by-step; (2) answers combined with "precise legal language" → customized printable document; (3) print, sign, make official per provided instructions; "keep it somewhere safe but accessible."
- State-tailored: user review says "tailored for everyone's specific state of residence"; product pages are state-aware.
- Not-a-law-firm posture: "online self-help solutions"; flags where an attorney may be a better fit; offers an easy-to-read summary to bring to an attorney.
- Asset Inventory (help center): add major assets to the plan "to help the executor"; asset types: Financial Account, Retirement Account, Life Insurance, Real Estate, Vehicle, Brokerage Account, Cash/valuables; classified into probate (distributed by will) vs non-probate (jointly owned, 401(k)/pension/IRA, life insurance); non-probate assets listed on a separate "Beneficiary Plan" document.
- Account model: "Your Estate Plan" section with per-document "View/Edit" — documents are revisable objects in a personal account.
- Execution formalities (help center article titles): "How to get your documents notarized", "Witnesses and Notaries", "Remote will notarization with Proof" (integration with an online notary service), "I've been nominated as a witness, now what?", "Executors of Your Will".
- Amendment paths: "Codicils and FreeWill", "Amending Your Trust", "Pour-Over Wills", "How to create a mirror will" (couples), pet trust via RLT tool, funeral wishes.
- Business model: free to consumers; funded by 2,400+ nonprofit partners; charity bequest emphasis (planned giving); also sells "Estately" (advisor-facing estate planning platform) and a lawyers offering.

## Product B — LegalZoom (consumer self-serve, paid, attorney network)

Evidence layer: A (directly observed on official product page).

Key observations:

- Estate plan line: Estate Plan Bundle; Will; Living Trust; Power of Attorney; Healthcare Directive; plus a free "Estate Plan Check" tool.
- Will packages (individual): Basic Will (will + pet care provisions + secure online document storage + unlimited revision for 30 days + one printed/shipped set); Pro Will (adds healthcare directive, medical POA, financial POA, HIPAA authorization); Premium Will (adds 1-year revisions, unlimited 30-min attorney consultations, annual 1-hour estate planning review with an attorney). Couple versions double the documents.
- Stated workflow: (1) answer quick questions (~15 minutes) — choose who settles your affairs (executor), what to leave to loved ones, guardian for minor children; (2) documents created from answers, viewable in the LegalZoom account (typically downloadable within 2–3 business days — product-specific); (3) review on your own or with an attorney over the phone, then print and sign.
- State-specific: wills "designed to meet the specific laws and requirements of each U.S. state"; "accepted in all 50 states"; "signing instructions based on your state's laws."
- Validity criteria (FAQ): sound mind; own free will without undue influence; signed and witnessed according to applicable state law.
- Amendment/revocation (FAQ): codicil must be signed and witnessed the same way as the original; revoke by new will or by physically destroying the old one; change will on major life events.
- Storage guidance (FAQ): original will needs to be filed in court after death; keep in a safe deposit box, fireproof safe, or with an attorney; make sure the executor knows where it is.
- Estate plan components (FAQ): last will and testament; living trust (probate avoidance, requires asset retitling and ongoing maintenance); financial power of attorney; advance healthcare directive.
- Post-mortem education: executor role, probate process, intestate succession explained in FAQ.
- Not-a-law-firm posture: "not a law firm and does not provide legal advice, except where authorized through its subsidiary law firm LZ Legal Services, LLC"; attorney network subscription ("Assist").

Key observations:

- Positioning: "AI-powered estate & tax planning platform" for financial advisors, broker-dealers, private banks; audiences: advisors, enterprises, attorneys, trust companies. Clients can only access through an advisor; invited clients get a portal (view reports, upload estate documents, connect assets/liabilities).

- Single source of truth: "Work from a single source of truth. Built to keep you up to date, not buried in outdated versions."
- Integration of documents with the rest of the platform: visualize plan structure, simulate what-if scenarios (Scenario Builder: tax implications, estate distributions, planning opportunities against the client's current plan), share with collaborators.
- Ester (AI): extract, summarize, and visualize complete estate plans and tax documents (analysis of existing documents).
- Vault: "Share and store documents securely via the Vault."
- Use cases: Understand the Estate / Optimize the Estate / Manage the Estate.
- Integrations: Salesforce, eMoney, Orion, Wealthbox, Redtail, Addepar, BlackDiamond, Schwab; asset linking via Carta, Coinbase, Zillow.
- Not-a-law-firm posture: "Wealth.com is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice."
- Full-journey claim: "client education, document creation, execution, and ongoing monitoring of future planning and tax opportunities."

## Product D — Estateably (professional estate/trust administration — boundary probe)

Evidence layer: A (directly observed on official site).

Key observations:

- Positioning: "Estate and Trust Administration Software" — for estate/trust attorneys, trust officers, CPAs, family office professionals, solo practitioners.
- Modules: Estate Administration (probate), Trust Administration, Power of Attorney administration, Fiduciary Accounting (transactions, assets, liabilities, statement import, compensation fees), Vault (secure planning document storage), Notice to Creditors, Task Management, Form & Document Automation, Intake, Contacts, Distributions, Reporting.

### Stable commonalities across the planning-side products (FreeWill, LegalZoom, Wealth.com)

1. The unit of work is a person's estate plan realized as a set of formal legal instruments (will; trust; financial POA; healthcare directive; guardianship nominations).
2. Instruments designate people (executor, trustee, agent, guardian) and disposition (who receives what, including charities and pets).
3. Content and execution formalities are jurisdiction-bound (state-specific templates, signing/witnessing/notarization instructions).
4. Authoring is questionnaire/guided-intake driven, generating documents from attorney-crafted templates.
5. Documents live in a persistent, revisable account ("Your Estate Plan" / account documents / single source of truth) — the plan of record.
6. The path to effectiveness (review → sign → witness/notarize) is an explicit, supported stage — not left to the user to figure out.
7. Secure storage of executed documents is a product feature or explicit guidance.
8. Non-probate assets (beneficiary designations on retirement accounts, life insurance, jointly owned property) are acknowledged as outside the will and commonly tracked.
9. All planning-side products disclaim being a law firm; attorney involvement is an add-on layer (network, consults, review), not the default author.

## Canonical Abstraction

### L0 — Defining Invariant (minimal)

An Estate Planning Application exists only if all four hold:

1. **Person-centered plan of record** — the application maintains one person's estate plan as persistent, revisable records (not one-off anonymous forms). Remove → legal forms library / document template store.
2. **Death & incapacity legal instruments** — the plan is realized as a set of formally recognized instrument types (will, trust, financial power of attorney, healthcare directive; guardianship nominations for minors). Remove → generic document automation.
3. **Designations of people and disposition** — the instruments' content is the appointment of fiduciaries (executor, trustee, agent, guardian) and the disposition of property to beneficiaries. Remove → empty shells, not a plan.
4. **Jurisdiction-bound formal validity** — instrument content and the execution path (signing, witnessing, notarization) are bound to the law of a jurisdiction; the application exists to produce instruments that are valid there. Remove → generic legal document automation (a different Type).

### L1 — Common Mature Structure (cross-product, evidence B)

- guided questionnaire / intake as the primary authoring surface
- document generation from attorney-crafted, jurisdiction-optimized templates
- persistent account holding the document set; per-document view/edit
- revision and amendment support (revision windows, codicils, trust amendment/restatement)
- asset inventory with probate vs non-probate classification
- beneficiary designations for non-probate assets (retirement accounts, life insurance)
- progress tracking of documents toward signature (review states)
- attorney review / consult access as an optional layer
- notarization support (instructions; mobile/remote notary in some products)
- secure document storage / vault
- executor-facing support (asset inventory for the executor; storage-location guidance)
- education content (probate, intestacy, what instruments do)

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- authorship model: self-serve consumer vs advisor-led vs attorney-facing
- business model: free nonprofit-funded; paid D2C with packages; advisor-firm subscription; professional licensing
- scenario/tax modeling of wealth transfer (advisor pole)
- AI analysis of existing estate documents (extraction/summarization)
- integrations with financial-planning, CRM, custody systems; asset linking (equity/crypto/real estate)
- couples/joint planning (mirror wills)
- charity bequest emphasis / planned giving alignment
- trust funding support; sub-trusts; pet trusts; funeral wishes
- printed/shipped document sets; remote online notarization integrations
- post-mortem handoff features (executor instructions, "manage the estate" surfaces)

### L3 — Vendor-specific (kept out of the final document)

- Wealth.com: Ester AI, Scenario Builder, Vault naming; advisor-only access model; specific integration list; "understand/optimize/manage the estate" use-case framing
- FreeWill: "Beneficiary Plan" companion document; Proof remote-notarization integration; nonprofit funding model; California-only RLT
- LegalZoom: package tiers and prices; "Assist" attorney subscription; LZ Guarantee; 2–3 business day document availability; printed/shipped docs; LZ Legal Services subsidiary
- Estateably: named court-form libraries (DE-111, 74A…), fiduciary accounting module, notice-to-creditors publishing, SOC 2/data-residency claims

## Rejected Findings (considered, not promoted)

- "Estate planning = will creation" — rejected: every sampled product spans incapacity instruments (POA/directive) alongside death instruments; the Type's scope is death AND incapacity.
- "Beneficiary designations are definitional" — rejected: will-only products (e.g., a basic will package) are still clearly estate planning applications; designations are L1.
- "Secure cloud vault is definitional" — rejected: FreeWill's core flow ends in printed paper with storage *guidance*; desktop-era products stored files locally. Storage is L1.
- "Advisor-led access is definitional" — rejected: two of three planning-side products are consumer self-serve. Advisor-led is a variant (L2).
- "AI document analysis is definitional" — rejected: single-product (Wealth.com) differentiator; L2/L3.
- "Probate avoidance via trusts is the purpose" — rejected: wills without trusts remain in-type; trust-vs-will is a plan-design choice the products support, not the definition.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Estate/Trust Administration software (Estateably pole)** — Planning: a living person creates and maintains instruments that will operate at death/incapacity. Administration: after death (or during incapacity), a fiduciary executes the plan — probate petitions, creditor notices, fiduciary accounting, distributions, trust termination. Remove the living-person planning orientation and add court/fiduciary operations → administration, a different Type. Overlap is limited to document storage (both have "Vault") and POA (administration manages an *active* POA; planning creates it). **Taxonomy note:** the directory has no estate/trust administration leaf; such products currently have no obvious home (nearest: §11 Law Practice Management). Recorded in STATUS Boundary Issues.
2. **vs Legal Document Automation / Legal Drafting Platform (§11)** — Generic legal document automation serves many document classes with no person-centered plan of record. Remove the estate binding (plan of record + death/incapacity instrument class) → legal document automation. LegalZoom is a general legal platform whose estate-planning line is an estate planning application; the same company's LLC formation line is not.
3. **vs Financial Advisor Platform / Wealth Management Platform (§08)** — Financial planning suites commonly embed an estate module; the standalone estate planning application centers on legal instruments and their validity, not on portfolios, budgets, or cash-flow. Wealth.com is advisor-facing but its core objects are instruments, designations, and scenarios over the estate — not portfolio management. Remove instruments → financial planning.
4. **vs Retirement Planning Application (§08)** — Retirement planning is accumulation/decumulation during life; estate planning is disposition at death/incapacity. Shared advisor and client; different core objects.
5. **vs Law Practice Management System (§11)** — Practice management runs the firm (clients, matters, billing, documents); estate planning application runs the person's plan. Attorney-facing drafting tools sit between; the plan-of-record anchor distinguishes them.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check

- Desktop-era self-help software (Quicken WillMaker class, not fetchable — reasoned from category knowledge only, so kept weak): questionnaire → state-specific will → print → sign with witnesses; documents stored as local files. Fits L0 (plan of record = local document set; instruments; designations; state validity). No vault, no advisor, no AI — still the Type.
- Paper-era: attorney with precedent forms is a service, not an application; not in scope.
- Regional: UK/Canada will-writing tools generalize "state" to "jurisdiction" (province/country); L0's jurisdiction-bound validity holds without US-specifics (witness counts, notarization variants, community property, etc. are all L2/regulatory detail).
- The L0 passes the check: nothing in it depends on the current US D2C web pattern.

## Final Synthesis

An Estate Planning Application is the system of record for one person's estate plan: it maintains a persistent, revisable set of death-and-incapacity legal instruments (will, trust, financial power of attorney, healthcare directive, guardianship nominations) in which the person designates fiduciaries and beneficiaries, generates those instruments from attorney-crafted templates through a guided questionnaire bound to the person's jurisdiction, and carries them through the formalities (review, signing, witnessing, notarization) that make them legally effective, then stores and maintains them as life changes.

The defining core is small (plan of record + instrument class + designations + jurisdiction-bound validity). Everything else — vaults, advisor portals, scenario modeling, AI analysis, integrations, business models — is common mature structure or variant. Post-mortem execution of the plan (probate, fiduciary accounting, distributions) is a different Type (estate/trust administration), even though products increasingly bridge both sides.
