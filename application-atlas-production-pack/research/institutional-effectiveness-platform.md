# Research Notes — Institutional Effectiveness Platform (§23 Education, Research & Knowledge Institutions)

Research date: 2026-09-08

## Research Goal

Understand what an "Institutional Effectiveness Platform" is as an Application Type in the
directory's education context: what the institution's system actually contains, how the
institutional planning–assessment–improvement loop runs in real products, and where it
differs from the sibling leaves that share its vocabulary and — in practice — its vendors.

Two prior passes constrain this one:

- **Academic Accreditation Management** (researched 2026-09-06) recorded the strongest
  internal tension: the same products (Watermark P&SS, Nuventive, SPOL, Weave) market as
  both "accreditation management" and "institutional effectiveness". Its working
  distinction: the accreditation leaf is organized around the *external accreditor's*
  standards and cycle (standards → responses → evidence → report → reaffirmation), while
  an IE platform is organized around the institution's *own* planning/assessment/
  improvement loop even where no accreditor is involved. It flagged this leaf for joint
  review. This pass tests that distinction from the IE side.
- The §25 (accreditor-side) and §11 (corporate seeker-side compliance) passes do not
  collide with this leaf; both are different seats of the accreditation relationship.

Working hypothesis: an Institutional Effectiveness Platform is the higher-education
institution's system of record for running its own continuous cycle of planning
(missions/goals/outcomes per unit), assessment (measures/results per cycle), improvement
(actions taken on findings), and demonstration (evidence and compiled reports for
internal/external accountability) — with accreditation support as a major driver and
reporting surface, not the organizing structure.

## Initial Boundary

- The subject is the *institution* (college, university; also programs and non-academic
  units inside it), not the student and not the accreditor.
- Obvious confusion set:
  1. §23 sibling **Academic Accreditation Management** — same vendors, convergent
     products; the boundary must be settled explicitly (see Boundary Findings).
  2. §23 siblings **Assessment Platform / Course Evaluations** — student-learning
     measurement machinery; here assessment results are consumed as findings.
  3. §23 sibling **Curriculum Management** — curriculum change/catalog process; here
     curriculum mapping appears as alignment machinery.
  4. Corporate **strategy/OKR/goal platforms** (§10 territory) — the strategic-planning
     layer looks similar; the education assessment substrate is what differs.
  5. **BI / dashboards / institutional research** — data reporting without the
     improvement loop and the accountability record.

## Research Questions

1. What objects does the system hold for the *institution and its units*? (missions,
   goals, outcomes, plans, tactics, measures, findings, actions, evidence)
2. How does the recurring cycle work? (reporting years/cycles, assessment plans,
   results collection, review, next-cycle continuity)
3. How is "closing the loop" implemented (findings → actions → tracked change)?
4. How do strategic planning, program review, and accreditation/self-study relate to the
   core loop — definitional or adjacent surfaces?
5. Who uses it (IE office, IR, accreditation liaisons, provost/deans, chairs, faculty,
   administrative-unit leads) and with what roles/permissions?
6. What data integrations feed it (LMS, course evaluations, SIS/ERP/BI)?
7. Where is the boundary vs the accreditation sibling, assessment platforms, curriculum
   management, corporate strategy tools, and BI?
8. Does the historical / pre-software / non-US reality still fit the abstraction?

Cross-product observations:

- **(B)** All sampled products run the same four-part loop: unit-level
  missions/goals/outcomes → recurring measurement (results per cycle) → documented
  improvement actions → a cumulative demonstrable record (evidence + compiled reports).
- **(B)** The unit (department/program/administrative unit) with a **lead** and a
  **mission statement** is the standing organizing unit; planning and assessment objects
  attach to units, and assignments (tactics, measures, plan sections) are pushed down to
  them with progress reported back up.
- **(B)** The recurring cycle is explicit: reporting years/cycles, year-over-year
  comparison, "institutional memory" across cycles.
- **(B)** Accreditation appears in every product — but always as *one driver and one
  reporting surface* (standards-linked self-study), never as the only organizing
  structure; the loop runs for strategic plans, administrative units, and program review
  with no accreditor involved.
- **(B)** Data integration (LMS pulls, course-evaluation feeds, BI/SIS/ERP mapping) is
  the standard way results arrive; faculty manual entry remains a universal fallback.
- **(B)** "Closing the loop" is the named, load-bearing concept in every product's
  language (actions taken, what changed, which actions led to progress).

## Canonical Model (L0 / L1 / L2 / L3)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

The smallest structure without which the product is no longer recognizable as an
Institutional Effectiveness Platform:

1. **Unit-level mission-and-outcome/goal hierarchy** — the institution is modeled as
   units (academic programs, departments, administrative divisions), each carrying
   stated intent (mission, goals, outcomes) that anchors everything assessed below it;
   the institution level holds its own mission and priorities. Remove → org chart with
   statements, no subject to assess; generic BI.
2. **Recurring assessment cycles producing findings against those outcomes/goals** —
   per cycle (reporting year/period), measures with results (direct/indirect) are
   attached to outcomes and judged. Remove → static plan documents or a dashboard;
   strategy-execution tool territory.
3. **The improvement loop — findings drive documented, owned actions** — each finding
   can be answered with an action/improvement ("closing the loop"), assigned to an
   owner, tracked, and visible in the next cycle. Remove → assessment data collection
   and reporting; the "effectiveness" half is gone.
4. **A cumulative, demonstrable effectiveness record** — evidence (documents, data,
   narratives) is retained and compiled into institutional reports (assessment reports,
   program reviews, self-studies) that the institution can show to internal and
   external audiences; the record persists across cycles. Remove → private worksheets;
   the accountability purpose of the Type disappears.

Jointly-held tests: 1 alone = mission-statement profile site; 1+2 without 3 = assessment
reporting/analytics; 1+3 without 2 = OKR-style goal tracker; 2+3 without 1 = anonymous
improvement tracker; 1+2+3 without 4 = internal worksheets with no demonstrable record.

Note: the institution's own loop is the invariant — accreditation is one *external*
consumer of the record (and a major market driver), not a defining structure. A product
that only structures accreditor-standards responses belongs to the sibling Type
(Academic Accreditation Management).

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

- Strategic-planning layer as a first-class plan type (priorities → goals →
  tactics/action items → assigned to units → progress roll-up)
- Program review as a recurring template-driven workflow with contributor and reviewer
  roles and structured feedback
- Curriculum mapping: learning outcomes aligned to courses; gap identification
- Direct + indirect measures; course evaluations as an indirect feed
- LMS integrations pulling results; faculty score-entry prompts; result uploads
- Evidence library shared across reports and cycles (store once, cite many)
- Self-study/report composer: narratives + evidence + (where accreditation-relevant)
  standards linkage, compiled output
- Roles and permissions: system admins, unit leads, contributors, reviewers, leadership
  viewers; granular per-unit access
- Dashboards: cycle progress, gap analysis, mission/goal alignment, longitudinal trends
- Multi-year continuity: reporting years, year-over-year comparison, institutional
  memory across cycles
- Non-academic/administrative unit assessment (student affairs, operational units),
  sometimes with standards packs (CAS)
- AI assistance for measure analysis and action suggestions (era-current)

## Rejected Findings

- **"IE platform = accreditation management."** Rejected as a merge (coordination with
  the 2026-09-06 sibling pass). The accreditation leaf is organized around the external
  accreditor's standards and cycle; the IE platform is organized around the
  institution's own loop, which runs with no accreditor involved (strategic plans,
  administrative units, program review). Products converge and one product often serves
  both — recorded as a ratified boundary, not a merge.
- **"IE platform = corporate strategy/OKR platform."** Rejected: the strategic-planning
  layer alone is an OKR-tool shape; the education assessment substrate (learning
  outcomes, program review, accreditation evidence, unit missions) is what makes the
  Type.
- **"IE platform = assessment platform."** Rejected: instrument delivery, scoring, and
  learning-measurement machinery belong to Assessment/Course-Evaluation Types; the IE
  platform consumes results as findings. The data-first LMS-embedded end (eLumen) is the
  boundary case.
- **"Budgeting is core."** Single-product module → optional (L2).
- **"Faculty qualifications are core."** Packaging varies three ways across the sample →
  common/optional.
- **"Curriculum mapping is core."** Common (Watermark, Blackboard Outcomes, Weave) but
  absent from the core framing of others (SPOL, Nuventive) → L1, not definitional.
- **"The improvement database/institutional memory is a vendor feature."** Rejected as
  vendor-specific: it appears in every sampled product's own language (chronological
  narrative, growing record, institutional memory) → supports L0 leg 4.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Academic Accreditation Management (§23 sibling)** — ratified keep-both from the
   IE side, using the sibling's working distinction and confirming it against IE-side
   evidence: the organizing question differs. Accreditation management is structured by
   the *external accreditor's standards and cycle* (standards → standard-by-standard
   responses → evidence → report → reaffirmation); an IE platform is structured by the
   *institution's own mission-outcome-assessment-improvement loop*, which runs whether
   or not an accreditor exists. Removing the accreditor's standards from an IE platform
   leaves it fully functional (strategic planning, assessment, program review all
   remain); removing the institution's own loop from an accreditation product leaves
   only the standards-response machinery. Both products typically ship from the same
   vendors and interlock (IE record feeds accreditation self-studies) — same market,
   different organizing structure. Joint-review flag can be considered answered from
   this side.
2. **vs Assessment Platform / Course Evaluations (§23 siblings)** — data supplier vs
   loop owner. Assessment products measure student learning (instruments, delivery,
   scoring); course-evaluation products collect indirect feedback. The IE platform
   attaches results (pulled or entered) to institutional/program outcomes and runs the
   improvement/accountability loop. eLumen-style LMS-embedded assessment suites are the
   boundary: strong substrate, thin planning/accountability layer.
3. **vs Curriculum Management (§23 sibling)** — curriculum change/catalog process vs
   alignment machinery; curriculum mapping inside IE is evidence/alignment, not the
   change process.
4. **vs Corporate strategy/OKR/goal platforms (§10)** — shares the
   goals→actions→progress skeleton; lacks (and does not need) learning-outcome
   assessment, program review, and accreditation evidence. The education substrate is
   the seam.
5. **vs BI/dashboards/institutional research** — BI reports data; the IE platform
   structures the cycle the data must serve (plans, findings, actions, evidence) and
   explicitly *connects* to BI rather than replacing it (Nuventive's "connect the
   numbers with the narrative").
6. **vs Student Success & Engagement platforms** — individual-student progression vs
   institutional/program effectiveness; different subject of record.
7. **"去掉什么就变成另一个 Type" tests**: remove the accreditor's standards as organizing
   structure → this Type stands, accreditation management collapses; remove the unit
   mission/outcome hierarchy → BI/analytics; remove recurring assessment → strategy
   execution tool; remove the improvement loop → assessment reporting; remove the
   demonstrable record → private worksheets.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check

Would older, regional, platform-native, or differently positioned products still fit
the L0?

- **Pre-software practice**: institutional effectiveness as a practice long predates the
  software — IE/IR offices ran the cycle with mission statements per unit, outcome
  lists, assessment results in spreadsheets, improvement notes, and evidence binders
  assembled into accreditation self-studies and program reviews. All four L0 legs are
  present in the paper practice; the software digitizes and connects them. Historical
  check passes.
- **Software lineage**: Watermark's help center still documents Taskstream, LiveText,
  and Tk20 — the 2000s-generation assessment/IE platforms — as heritage products;
  Blackboard Planning's URLs carry the Anthology lineage. The Type's continuity across
  two decades of consolidation is directly observable.
- **Non-US / other segments**: the reachable sample is US higher-ed heavy. K-12 district
  improvement planning and non-US quality-assurance contexts plausibly share the core
  (they share the loop) but were not verified; recorded as variants of uncertain
  coverage, not structure.
- The L0 does not depend on LMS integrations, dashboards, AI, standards packs, or any
  specific accreditor's framework.

## Final Synthesis

An Institutional Effectiveness Platform is the higher-education institution's own
system of record for its continuous planning–assessment–improvement cycle. The
institution is modeled as units, each holding missions/goals/outcomes; recurring
assessment cycles attach measures and results to those outcomes; findings must be
answered with owned, tracked improvement actions; and the whole loop accumulates into a
persistent, demonstrable record — evidence libraries and compiled reports (assessment
reports, program reviews, self-studies) that serve internal leadership and external
accountability audiences. Accreditation is the most important external driver and a
common reporting surface, but not the organizing structure: the loop runs for strategic
plans, administrative units, and program review with no accreditor involved. The
