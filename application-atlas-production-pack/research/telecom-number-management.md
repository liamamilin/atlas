## Research Goal

Determine what Telecom Number Management is as an Application Type: what the unit of record is (the individual number, the range/block, the pool), what lifecycle numbers move through, who works the population, what rules govern it (uniqueness, quarantine, regulatory requirements, portability), and where its boundaries sit against the neighboring leaves — SIM/eSIM Management, Subscriber Management, Telecom Inventory Management, Telecom Provisioning Platform, Telecom Order Management, Telecom Charging Platform, Telecom BSS, IPAM/DNS-DHCP Management, and consumer-facing virtual-number products. This pass discharges two pre-held obligations: (1) the subscriber-management pass's forward note ("sim-esim-management + telecom-number-management seam = credential/number resource lifecycles vs the subscriber record that binds them"); (2) the telecom-inventory-management pass's boundary note #8 (numbers as a specialized population that "can live inside an estate product as modules; the specialized leaves should hold their own centers").

## Initial Boundary (hypothesis before research)

- Hypothesis: Telecom Number Management is the communications provider's system of record for its telephone-number resources — the numbers and number ranges it holds (from national numbering authorities, regulators, upstream suppliers, or its own private plans), allocated to network elements, reserved, assigned to subscribers/services, ported in and out, recycled, and reported on.
- Two market readings suspected going in: (a) operator-side — number inventory as a BSS/OSS resource (blocks from the numbering plan, assigned to switches and subscribers); (b) CPaaS/developer-side — "number management" as search/buy/configure/release APIs over a provider's number inventory.
- Likely confusions: SIM/eSIM management (parallel credential resource); subscriber management (the record that binds numbers); telecom inventory management (the general estate that may carry numbers as a slice); provisioning (the activation act); number-portability clearinghouse systems (the inter-operator transaction); consumer virtual-number apps (end-user products, not provider-side resource systems); IPAM (the same structural pattern over IP addresses).

## Research Questions

1. What is the unit of record — the individual number, the range/block, the pool/batch? What attributes and bindings does each carry?
2. What does the lifecycle look like: acquire → hold/reserve → assign → activate/in service → quarantine/release/recycle → port? Which legs are definitional?
3. Who works the population: numbering administrators, provisioning, CSR/order capture, developers, resellers?
4. What rules matter: uniqueness, quarantine/holding periods, regulatory documentation requirements, reclaim of unused numbers, port-out authorization?
5. How does the number record relate to money (per-number charges, vanity/premium pricing) — binding or computation?
6. Is the CPaaS "number management" pole the same Type as the operator pole, or a different one?
7. Where are the seams vs Subscriber Management (the binding record), SIM/eSIM Management (parallel resource), Telecom Inventory Management (the general estate), and Provisioning (the activation act)?
8. Historical check: would paper-era numbering-plan ledgers and switch allocation records still fit?

## Product Observations

### Oracle Communications BRM — Number Manager / Number Administration Center (A-evidence)

- Positioning: "This chapter describes how to manage telephone number inventory in your Oracle Communications Billing and Revenue Management (BRM) system by using Number Manager and Number Administration Center." Number Manager = "the number management opcodes, utilities, configuration files, and storable classes"; Number Administration Center = "a GUI application used by operations personnel to manage telephone number inventory." Customer Center (the CSR application) is where numbers are assigned to accounts/services.
- **The population is built in blocks**: "To create an inventory of telephone numbers in the BRM database, you use Number Administration Center to create blocks of numbers. Blocks of numbers usually correspond to geographic regions or business entities." A block = start number + end number (any common format: "1-800-555-1212", "18005551212"…) + a unique block name + attributes shared by all numbers in the block.
- **Block attributes** (assigned to every number in the block): the **network element** ("also known as home location register, or HLR" — operator-defined list); the **number category** ("configurable field… for example… business or residential numbers"; defaults "None and Reserved"); the **vanity type** ("can be used for customized pricing and rating"); the **service type** ("to customize business policies").
- **Block operations**: modify name/prefix; extend/shrink (new range "must be in a continuous range and not overlap with any other existing blocks"); split ("you can split a block into smaller blocks, but you cannot join blocks").
- **Number device states** (the lifecycle): "Telephone numbers have the following device states: new, assigned, quarantined, and unassigned." Created → New; assigned to a customer → Assigned; "when a customer cancels the service associated with the number, the device state is Quarantined"; "by default, a number is unquarantined after 90 days. After a number has been unquarantined, the device state changes to Unassigned." States change automatically with account/service events, not by free editing ("You cannot use Number Administrator to change the number device state"). Custom states possible ("You can create your own number device states").
- **Quarantine management**: the quarantine period is configurable; a utility exists to unquarantine manually "if you run out of new or unassigned numbers"; a policy opcode can forbid assigning quarantined numbers to accounts.
- **Uniqueness**: "By default, BRM prevents the creation of duplicate numbers."
- **Numbers as devices**: "After creating the numbers as devices in the BRM database…" — numbers are first-class storable objects associated with services; "changing a telephone number or changing any of its attributes does not create a new /device/number object… instead, the existing number is updated"; deletion "is not recommended… you could… accidentally delete a number that is in use."
- **Reporting**: "You can get information about number inventory and status by running the Number Manager reports"; per-customer "history of a customer's number" viewable in Customer Center.
- **Assignment in order flow** (Customer Center): "Assign telephone numbers to services. Change a customer's telephone number. Display the history of a customer's number."

### VC4 IMS — Telephone Number Management (TNM) (A-evidence)

- Positioning: "VC4's Telephone Number Management feature enables operators to manage and allocate their telephone numbers as well as associating them to IMS objects like circuits, customers, nodes, and sites. This makes for a powerful method of tracking numbers, making it ideal for reporting as well. Reporting can be based not only on allocation to other objects, but by usage (allocated or not) and dates i.e., created dates and expiry dates. TNM can also be integrated through APIs with 3rd party systems."
- **Numbering-plan substrate documented in the guide**: US/Canada numbers carry NPA (Numbering Plan Area / area code) and NXX (central office code); "phone companies were assigned numbers in blocks of 10,000. However, more recently, NANPA… has been breaking up NXX blocks into groups of 1,000." The guide points to NANPA as the authoritative source.
- **Four-step workflow** (directly observed): "1. Telephone Number Range Type: creating a new or selecting an existing one. 2. Telephone Number range: filling in the required supporting customer information. 3. Set up the block ranges or sub ranges – (10,000 block or subranges of 1,000). 4. Assigning individual telephone numbers from those blocks to customers." The guide comments: "We can consider the first 3 steps as part of an administrative role, whilst the last step is more of an end user assignment process."
- **Structure**: a Telephone Number Range Type (TNRT) "determines the country code and how many area code fields are required as part of the TNR"; Telephone Number Ranges (TNR) hold blocks/sub-ranges; "K blocks" are mentioned as a range granularity; ranges can carry a Customer association for filtering.
- **Access surface**: TNM is reached from the Node object ("Select the Node object → from its drop-down list, click on the Number management option"), opening the TelephoneNumberRangeForm with search/history/create actions.
- **Associations and reporting**: numbers associate to "circuits, customers, nodes, and sites"; reporting "by usage (allocated or not) and dates (created/expiry)"; API integration with 3rd-party systems.

### PortaOne PortaBilling — DID/MSISDN inventory (A-evidence)

- Positioning: the DID/MSISDN inventory lives under "Infrastructure > Inventory > DID/MSISDN inventory" — numbers as infrastructure alongside the switching/billing platform.
- **Acquisition from suppliers**: "Create a DID supplier vendor → Create a DID batch for the vendor → Upload DID numbers" (CSV/XLS upload). "A DID batch groups together a set of phone numbers (typically from the same city or area) provided by a particular vendor." On-demand acquisition also exists: numbers "auto-filled when a DID number is provisioned on-demand from DIDWW or DIDX" (upstream DID marketplaces).
- **Release to pool**: uploaded numbers are "Frozen" by default ("kept for later use… unavailable for immediate usage"); "Release DIDs to the pool" makes them "Available" to the operator's distribution machinery. "DID numbers cannot be released to the pool if they are used as an account ID, an account alias, or an access number."
- **Statuses** (directly observed vocabulary): "In use – already being used by an account; Not in use – available for use; Not in use since today – unassigned from the account for less than a day; Reserved – assigned to a specific customer but not yet in use; Frozen – kept for later use; Free for N days – the number of days this DID number has been unassigned from the account."
- **Assignment**: DIDs are assigned to accounts (phone lines) — search "through unallocated DIDs or among the DIDs assigned to this customer," filtered by "geographical location and DID number pattern"; group allocation via Account Generator; self-provisioning ("customers can self-provision DID numbers… log into the self-care portal, select the DIDs they need from an available list… If the customer decides they no longer need the DID number, they simply delete it… the number is released from this customer… The DID number can now be provisioned by other customers").
- **Reseller chains**: resellers/subresellers get pricing batches and provision from the pool to their own customers; "any DID number managed by a reseller can be released by this reseller back to the DID pool if the DID number is not used as an account ID, an account alias, or an access number"; charge distribution across reseller tiers is computed per party.
- **Money bindings on the number record**: per-number "Activation cost… paid by a service provider to a vendor"; "Recurring cost… charged by a vendor"; sale price/profit derived via pricing batches (markups, activation fees, recurring fees); "Recurring revenue – a monthly amount a customer is charged for [the DID]."
- **Porting** (directly observed, via Neustar): port-in ("a PortaBilling admin sends a request via the Port in button on the account details page… the phone number… the service password or PIN number of a customer's account within the previous service provider's system… the date by which this request must be completed… the customer's account within the previous service provider's system") and port-out ("PortaBilling… searches for the account associated with the requested number. If the account is found, PortaBilling terminates it and informs Neustar that the number is available… Once the number has been ported to this telecom, calls are routed to the new telecom network"; "administrators cannot affect the process"). Monitoring panels: "Number porting page (Infrastructure > Number porting)" with Port-in and Port-out panels. Configuration depends on the LERG database ("The system retrieves the LATA from the LERG database and uses this data to obtain the LRN").
- **Inventory reporting**: the DID panel shows totals — "Available… Frozen… In use" — with search by number pattern (contains/starts/ends/exactly), status, country, pricing batch, DID group.

Key reading: all five products — across operator BSS, operator OSS, VoIP/ISP billing, and two CPaaS platforms — hold the same three-part structure: a managed population of identified number records (organized through ranges/blocks/batches, with the individual number as the assignable unit), a recorded lifecycle with enforced uniqueness (acquire → hold/reserve → assign → in service → quarantine/release/recycle, with porting as a lifecycle event where public numbering applies), and the assignment binding that connects each in-service number to the subscriber/service/network endpoint that makes it callable. They differ in whose numbers, which acquisition channel, how deep the money bindings run, and which surface (BSS module, OSS module, billing-integrated inventory, API product) hosts the population.

## Abstraction Hierarchy

### L0 — Defining Invariant

Three jointly-held structures; remove any one and the product stops being recognizable as telecom number management:

1. **The number population of record.** The provider's callable addresses held as persistent, individually identified records — each number carrying its identity (the dialable number itself), a classification (type/category: geographic, toll-free, mobile, vanity, emergency-only…), a status, and its bindings (network element, service, customer/account). The population is organized through acquired ranges/blocks/batches — the unit in which numbers arrive and are allocated — while the individual number is the unit that is reserved, assigned, and tracked. Remove → dial-plan documentation or a contact list (numbers as attributes of people, not as managed resources); or a generic asset list without callable-address semantics.
2. **The recorded lifecycle with enforced uniqueness.** Numbers move through a managed lifecycle — acquired into inventory → held/frozen/reserved → assigned → in service → quarantined/released/recycled (ported in or out where public numbering applies) — through recorded state changes rather than free editing; a number can be in service for only one holder at a time, and holding/quarantine periods govern when a released number may be reused. Remove → a static list whose assignments collide and go stale.
3. **The assignment binding into service.** Each in-service number is bound to the subscriber/service/network endpoint that makes it callable — the binding that provisioning consumes to activate, billing consumes to charge, care consumes to answer "whose number is this," and porting consumes to move. The population's state is the reference these processes work from. Remove → an unbound number pool: a warehouse of addresses nobody can call.

Jointly-held is load-bearing: 1 alone = a number list/spreadsheet; 2 without 1 = a state machine over nothing; 3 without 1+2 = activation machinery with no population; 1+2 without 3 = a number warehouse nothing binds; 1+3 without 2 = a snapshot with collision risk; 2+3 without 1 = provisioning logic over no record.

Conceptual formula: **hold the numbers → change them only through recorded state → keep exactly one holder per in-service number → bind them into service.**

Not in L0 (deliberately): the public regulatory substrate (numbering plans, regulators, NANPA-class authorities), E.164 as the format, portability, per-number money bindings, self-service/reseller distribution, API-first delivery, specific state vocabularies, quarantine durations, reservation expiry windows. (See L1/L2.)

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

- **Range/block organization** — blocks, batches, pools, sub-ranges as the acquisition and allocation granularity; block-level operations (split, extend/shrink, attribute changes) at the operator pole.
- **Availability search and reservation** — find available numbers by pattern/area/type/features; reserve to block others (reservations commonly time-limited); order-time reservation groups during CSR assignment.
- **Bulk operations** — bulk edit, group operations, CSV import/export.
- **Classification and attributes** — number categories/types, vanity/premium markers, tags, country/area metadata, service-type markers for business policies.
- **Porting** — port-in/port-out as managed lifecycle events, integrated with portability authorities/clearinghouses; port-out authorization protection (PIN).
- **Regulatory requirements handling** — per-country/per-type documentation requirements gating activation; emergency-address management; reclaim of inactive numbers under regulatory pressure.
- **Utilization and inventory reporting** — counts by status, aging ("free for N days"), allocation/expiry reporting, per-number history.
- **Money bindings** — per-number recurring charges, activation fees, markups/pricing batches, vanity/premium pricing hooks (the computation itself belongs to billing/charging).
- **Self-service and downstream distribution** — self-provisioning portals, reseller/subreseller pools, embedded number-search APIs for downstream applications.
- **Integration surfaces** — order capture/CRM (assignment at order time), provisioning/activation, billing, portability registries, upstream DID suppliers/marketplaces.

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- **Whose numbers (the primary variant axis)**: a carrier's public numbering-plan resources (acquired from authorities/other carriers; portability and regulatory utilization apply) ↔ a CPaaS provider's commercial inventory (resold via API/portal to developers and enterprises) ↔ an enterprise's private ranges (internal telephony; no regulator, no portability) — one Type across all three.
- **Acquisition channel**: regulatory allocation (numbering-plan blocks) ↔ upstream suppliers and DID marketplaces ↔ own network stock ↔ manual sourcing teams (advanced orders).
- **Number kinds**: geographic/local, national, mobile/MSISDN, toll-free, vanity, emergency-only, access numbers.
- **Money depth**: none (pure resource tracking) ↔ per-number charges/markups ↔ premium/vanity pricing hooks.
- **Portability depth**: absent (private ranges) ↔ integrated clearinghouse (port-in/port-out panels) ↔ API-first automated porting.
- **Regulatory depth**: none ↔ documentation requirements gating activation ↔ enforced reclaim of inactive numbers.
- **Packaging**: BSS-embedded component (number manager inside billing/revenue platforms) ↔ OSS-suite module (TNM inside inventory suites) ↔ billing-integrated inventory (VoIP operator platforms) ↔ standalone API product (CPaaS) ↔ console product ↔ open-source plugin.
- **Era-current**: inventory-coverage APIs, embedded number search for downstream apps, automated porting workflows.

## Vendor-specific Findings

Two vendor structures flirt with Type-level significance and were adjudicated as variants:

1. **Quarantine as a first-class state** (Oracle BRM): an explicit quarantined state between assigned and unassigned, with a configurable default period and policy control over reuse. Other products realize the same discipline as aging attributes ("free for N days"), restore windows (10/15 days), or reclaim policies. The invariant is the controlled delay before reuse, not the quarantine state name.
2. **Reservation-with-expiry as a productized API step** (Telnyx; PeopleSoft's "Blocked" CSR groups; PortaOne's Reserved status): holding a number against other claimants during the assignment transaction. The realization ranges from API resources to CSR group-blocking; the invariant is the reservation itself.

## Rejected Findings

- **"Number management = number portability."** Rejected as identity: porting is one lifecycle event (and, at the operator pole, often an external system — NetCracker integrates "number portability" as a separate system; PortaOne integrates Neustar). A number-management product without porting (private enterprise ranges; Oracle BRM's core flow) is still recognizably number management. Dedicated portability clearinghouse systems center the inter-operator transaction, not the provider's own population — a different center (see Boundary Findings).
- **"Number management is a CPaaS-only concept."** Rejected: the operator pole (Oracle BRM, VC4, PortaOne) documents the same structure decades deep in BSS/OSS; the CPaaS pole is the modern commercial packaging of the same Type, not the definition.
- **"Numbers are just another inventory item."** Rejected as collapse into Telecom Inventory Management: numbers carry a state discipline generic estate items do not — enforced uniqueness, quarantine/holding before reuse, reservation during assignment, portability events, regulatory reclaim. The general estate may carry numbers as a slice (NetCracker lists number management inside its resource inventory), but the specialized population has its own center.
- **"The binding to subscribers belongs here."** Rejected as absorption of Subscriber Management: the number record carries an assignment binding (pointer), but the subscriber population, its service state, and its commercial lifecycle are the subscriber-management Type's record. PortaOne shows the seam cleanly: DID inventory (Infrastructure) vs accounts (customer side), linked by assignment.
- **"Per-number billing is part of the Type."** Rejected as money-loop absorption: number records carry price bindings (costs, markups, pricing batches, vanity types), but charge computation, invoicing, and balance draw-down are Telecom Charging/Billing machinery. Strip the money machinery → recognizably number management.
- **"E.164 / public numbering plans are definitional."** Rejected: enterprise private ranges and internal extensions satisfy the same structure (NetBox plugins; PBX DID plans); the public-plan substrate (NANPA blocks, regulators, portability) is the variant context, not the definition.
- Precise operational defaults observed in-sample (90-day quarantine, 30-minute reservations, 10/15-day restore windows) — recorded here and in product observations only; not asserted as Type-level facts in the final document.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs SIM/eSIM Management (§19, unprocessed) — FORWARD NOTE DISCHARGED from this side.** Parallel specialized resource populations with parallel lifecycles: SIMs are physical/eUICC credentials (inventory → assigned → activated → replaced/suspended); numbers are callable addresses (acquired → reserved → assigned → in service → quarantined/recycled → ported). Both bind to the subscriber record; a mobile operator runs both and they meet at the subscriber. The ratified seam (credential/number resource lifecycles vs the subscriber record that binds them) holds: this Type's center is the number population; the SIM population is the sibling leaf's center. Removal tests both ways: strip the SIM population from a mobile operator's stack → still number management; strip the number population → still SIM management.
2. **vs Subscriber Management (§19, processed) — seam holds from this side.** The subscriber record binds numbers; this Type holds the numbers' own population, states, and lifecycle. Evidence: PortaOne separates the DID/MSISDN inventory (Infrastructure) from accounts (customer side), linked by assignment; Oracle BRM assigns numbers (devices) to services inside Customer Center but the number inventory is managed by Number Administration Center, a separate operations surface; the subscriber pass itself recorded "the subscriber record that binds them" as the seam. Strip the subscriber population → still number management; strip the number population → subscriber management with a dangling identifier field.
3. **vs Telecom Inventory Management (§19, processed) — specialized-population seam holds.** TIM is the general estate (services/equipment/resources); numbers are a specialized address-resource population with a state discipline the general estate does not carry (uniqueness, quarantine, reservation, portability, reclaim). NetCracker's resource inventory lists number management as one capability — evidence that estates can embed numbers as a slice — while dedicated number products (all five samples) hold the population as their center. The TIM pass's own adjudication ("specialized populations can live inside an estate product as modules; the specialized leaves should hold their own centers") is ratified from this side.
4. **vs Telecom Provisioning Platform (§19, unprocessed)**: provisioning activates services on network elements; number management holds the number state activation consumes and updates (Oracle: numbers bound to network elements/HLR; PortaOne: DIDs provisioned onto SIP accounts; Telnyx: numbers assigned to connections). The activation act is not this Type's. Seam pre-held for the provisioning pass.
5. **vs Telecom Order Management (§19, unprocessed)**: number orders (Telnyx's Number Order API; PeopleSoft's order-time number assignment) are transactions against the population; the number record is what they change. The order as unit of work is order management's center. Seam pre-held.
6. **vs Telecom Charging Platform (§19, processed)**: charging computes money for service consumption; number records carry price bindings (PortaOne pricing batches; Oracle vanity types "for customized pricing and rating"; per-number recurring charges at Telnyx/Twilio) but the computation is charging's. Seam consistent with the charging pass's own boundary (money computation vs state).
7. **vs Telecom BSS (§19, processed)**: number management is a component capability inside the BSS chain (Oracle BRM embeds it; PeopleSoft CRM embeds it; PortaOne embeds it in a billing/switching platform). The BSS leaf holds the category; this leaf holds the slice.
8. **vs Number-portability clearinghouse systems**: clearinghouses/registries center the inter-operator porting transaction and the ported-number routing data (PortaOne's Neustar integration; NetCracker's external number-portability integration). The provider-side number population is this Type's center; the clearinghouse is the external authority both porting parties integrate. Evidence on clearinghouse products themselves was not fetched this pass — claim kept structural, not product-specific.
9. **vs IPAM / DNS & DHCP Management (§14)**: the same structural pattern over a different address space — IPAM holds address ranges/subnets → allocation → assignment → binding into service, with uniqueness and utilization tracking. Numbers are the telecom-domain instantiation (with portability and regulatory semantics IPAM lacks). Cross-domain structural sibling; different domain, different leaf.
10. **vs Virtual Phone Application (§01.03) and consumer number apps**: end-user products that obtain a number for personal/business use are the demand side; this Type is the provider-side resource system that makes such products possible (Telnyx/Twilio explicitly serve that downstream market — embedded search, subaccount distribution). Different user, different unit of record.
11. **vs Telecom Expense Management (§14/§19, processed)**: the enterprise buy-side tracks numbers it leases as estate items inside TEM/TIM inventories; this Type is the provider-side population those leases draw from. The same number appears on both sides of the market with different records.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check

Would older, regional, or differently-positioned implementations fit the L0?

- **The numbering-plan substrate predates software**: VC4's own guide documents the NANPA block structure (10,000-number NXX blocks, later split into 1,000-number blocks) as the industry context any TNM system must reflect — a paper-era reality before it was a database schema.
- **The paper-era form**: an operator's numbering ledger — the range allocation record (which blocks the operator held, from whom) and the termination record (which number terminates on which switch line, assigned to which subscriber), updated by hand as lines were installed, cancelled (marked "recently disconnected" — the quarantine of its day), and reassigned — satisfies all three L0 legs: identified number records, recorded state changes with one holder per in-service number, and the binding that made the number callable. No software, APIs, or portability required.
- **Regional variants**: numbering-plan specifics differ by country (NPA/NXX in North America; national numbering plans elsewhere), but the sampled products absorb this as configuration (VC4's TNRT defines country code and area-code fields; PeopleSoft's number formats define country structure; Telnyx's coverage API returns per-country attributes) — the plan is input, not definition.
- **Private-range implementations** (enterprise PBX DID plans; the NetBox plugin pole) satisfy the L0 without any public-plan machinery — confirming the regulatory substrate is variant, not definitional.
- **The CPaaS pole** is the newest packaging (API-first, self-service) of the same structure — an evolution of the population-keeping discipline, not a redefinition.

Conclusion: the L0 does not over-fit the current CPaaS or BSS implementation; the paper-era ledger and the private-range pole both pass.

## Uncertainties

1. No tier-1 mobile-operator number-management product page was publicly reachable (NetCracker/Amdocs/Ericsson do not expose public number-management product documentation; the NetCracker evidence this pass is a press release plus the prior inventory pass's whitepaper). The operator pole therefore rests on Oracle BRM (BSS), VC4 (OSS), and PortaOne (VoIP operator) documentation — all Tier 1, but the tier-1 mobile MSISDN-management depth (HLR/HLR-replacement bindings at scale) is evidenced only through Oracle BRM's network-element attribute and should not be generalized further.
2. Regulatory utilization thresholds (e.g., obligations to return under-used number blocks to authorities) are industry reality but were not evidenced on the fetched pages; the final document does not assert specific thresholds or obligations — only the observed requirement-gating and reclaim behaviors.
3. State vocabularies are product-specific (New/Assigned/Quarantined/Unassigned vs Frozen/Available/In use/Reserved vs pending/active); the lifecycle is asserted at the conceptual level only. Oracle explicitly supports custom states; PortaOne's vocabulary is its own.
4. Whether dedicated number-portability clearinghouse products should eventually be a separate leaf could not be adjudicated without product evidence; flagged as a taxonomy consideration, no change made.
5. The NetBox plugin evidence comes from project documentation (GitHub/PyPI) fetched via search results, not full-page fetches; used as supporting evidence for the private-range pole only.
6. PeopleSoft Number Management is a legacy product line (9.1 PeopleBook); used as corroboration of the order-capture seam, not as a market-weight claim.

## Final Synthesis

Telecom Number Management is the communications provider's number-resource system of record: the provider's callable addresses held as a persistent, individually identified population — organized through acquired ranges, blocks, batches, and pools, with the individual number as the unit that is reserved, assigned, and tracked; moved through a recorded lifecycle with enforced uniqueness (acquire → hold/reserve → assign → in service → quarantine/release/recycle, with port-in/port-out as lifecycle events where public numbering applies, and holding periods governing reuse); and bound, number by number, into the service that makes each one callable — the binding that provisioning consumes to activate, billing consumes to charge, care consumes to attribute, and porting consumes to move. The Type spans one market with several poles: the carrier's public numbering-plan resources managed inside BSS/OSS platforms, the VoIP/ISP operator's DID inventory with reseller distribution, the CPaaS provider's commercially packaged inventory sold through APIs and portals, and the enterprise's private ranges — all one structure, differing in whose numbers, which acquisition channel, how deep the money bindings run, and which surface hosts the population. Its neighbors hold the adjacent machinery: subscriber management the people the numbers bind to, SIM/eSIM management the parallel credential population, telecom inventory the general estate numbers live inside, provisioning the activation act, order management the transactions, charging the money computation, portability clearinghouses the inter-operator authority. Remove the number population and none of those processes has an address to work with; keep the population with its lifecycle and bindings, and the product is recognizably telecom number management regardless of era, pole, or packaging.
