# Research Notes — Sponsor Management (§26, events)

## Research Goal

Understand what the directory leaf "Sponsor Management" (§26 Travel, Hospitality, Food Service & Events, between Exhibitor Management and Speaker Management) refers to in the real market: what objects the system manages, who operates it (organizer vs sponsor), what workflow runs from sale to event day, and where its boundaries lie against the processed siblings — Sponsorship Management (§25, org-level program), Exhibitor Management (§26), Convention / Exhibition Management (§26), Event Management Platform, Event Mobile App, Creator Sponsorship Management (§27), and fundraising-event sponsor modules.

This pass also discharges three joint-review flags:
1. **sponsorship-management (§25, 2026-09-09)** — scope seam ratified from that side: single event edition (A2Z pole: floor-plan/app ads, exhibitor buyers, event-day fulfillment) vs the organization's standing program (Rhythm/ChamberMaster pole). Joint review recommended when this leaf is processed.
2. **exhibitor-management (§26, 2026-09-07)** — market labels bundle "exhibitor & sponsor" (Cvent product page, Whova nav); expect keep-both with the same containment pattern; populations and obligation sets differ (booth/logistics/documents vs packages/visibility/advertising). This document is the boundary counterparty.
3. **convention-exhibition-management (§26, 2026-09-07)** — "in this Type sponsorships are one purchased item alongside floor space" — overlapping slice.

## Initial Boundary

Working hypothesis: event-side Sponsor Management = the organizer's system for selling and delivering sponsorship of a specific event — sponsorship packages composed of the event's own visibility placements (app ads, banners, sponsored sessions, floor-plan ads, naming rights), sold to companies (often exhibitors), with money collected and benefits fulfilled by event day, plus post-event value reporting.

Likely confusions:
- (a) is this just a module of event suites rather than a Type?
- (b) same objects as §25 Sponsorship Management — scope seam or alias?
- (c) exhibitors vs sponsors — same population or different?
- (d) dedicated sponsorship-sales platforms (SponsorCX/SponsorFlo/Clarity) — in-type or a different (property-side) Type?
- (e) ad-sales blur — are app/floor-plan ads sponsorship inventory or advertising?

## Research Questions

1. What is the event's "sponsorship inventory" — what do organizers actually sell (placements, packages, levels, add-ons)?
2. Who buys — exhibitors only, or any company? How does the sale happen (gallery/checkout, outreach, application)?
3. What does the commitment record carry (money side, benefit side, contract)?
4. What is the fulfillment loop — asset collection, task assignment, deadlines, placement into event surfaces, post-event reporting?
5. What interfaces exist on each side (organizer console, sponsor gallery/portal, event app/website surfaces)?
6. What rules matter — quantity limits, deadlines bound to event day, visibility targeting, revenue-vs-gift treatment?
7. Where are the seams vs §25 (org program), Exhibitor Management, Convention/Exhibition Management, Event Mobile App, creator-side sponsorship?

## Canonical Model (L0 / L1 / L2 / L3)

### L0 — Defining Invariant (three jointly-held structures)

1. **The event's sponsorship inventory** — the event's own visibility placements, packaged into sellable opportunities (levels/bundles/one-off add-ons) with prices and commonly quantity limits. The inventory is composed of the event's own surfaces: app placements, website banners, sponsored sessions, floor-plan ads, profile upgrades, on-site signage/activations, naming rights. Remove → a generic CRM/deal tracker with no structured event inventory.
2. **The sponsor commitment of record** — a persistent binding of sponsor × package × event carrying BOTH sides of the exchange: the money side (invoiced/collected, commonly contracted) and the benefit side (the placements and assets owed). Remove → a price list or scattered sales notes; the "management" dies.
3. **The fulfillment-to-event loop** — sponsor assets collected (logos, artwork, descriptions), placements delivered into the event's surfaces on schedule toward event day, tracked as tasks with deadlines and reminders, closed by post-event value reporting (impressions, clicks, leads) that feeds renewal. Remove → a sales pipeline with no delivery, or a static sponsor list.

The sponsor as a managed party record is the shared substrate with §25 (held in all sampled products; without it the commitment has no counterparty). The differentiating leg vs §25 is the inventory's content (the event's own placements) and the fulfillment deadline (event day).

Jointly-held load-bearing: 1 alone = a media kit / price list; 2 without 1 = free-floating deals; 3 without 1+2 = task tool with nothing to deliver; 1+2 without 3 = sold packages never delivered; 1+3 without 2 = anonymous placements.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

- sponsor records commonly linked to the exhibitor/company database
- self-service purchase surfaces (real-time gallery with cart checkout) and/or staff-driven sales pipelines
- sponsor self-service portal (view deliverables, upload assets, track status)
- contracts/e-signature and centralized agreement storage
- post-event ROI reporting (impressions, clicks, leads, engagement exports)
- revenue analytics (sales by package/level, outstanding balances, upsell signals)
- renewal orientation (history feeding next edition's offers)

### L2 — Variant / Optional

- packaging: suite module vs dedicated platform vs two-sided marketplace
- scope: single event edition vs event+season portfolio
- buyer: exhibitor-anchored vs open brand marketplace
- sales posture: staff-driven outreach/application vs sponsor self-service
- inventory flavor: trade-show pole (floor-plan ads, booth logos) vs conference pole (app ads, sponsored sessions) vs festival pole (activations, hospitality) vs sports/venue pole (signage, suites)
- AI assistance (deck builders, contract extraction, renewal scoring)

## Vendor-specific / Rejected Findings

- **"Sponsorship = advertising sales"** — rejected as identity: ad placements (app banners, floor-plan ads) are the dominant inventory *content* in this market, but they are sold as sponsorship benefits within packages; the Type's structure is the package/commitment/fulfillment grammar, not ad-serving machinery (no ad server: no auction, no impression-based pricing engine — Cvent's impression algorithm is display scheduling, not trading).
- **"Sponsors are just exhibitors"** — rejected: products bundle the populations (Cvent, Whova) but the obligation sets differ (booth/logistics/documents vs packages/visibility/assets); dedicated sponsor-only products exist (SponsorCX, Experia).
- **"Two-sided marketplace is definitional"** — rejected: staff-driven poles (SponsorCX, Experia pipeline) and suite-module poles (A2Z, Cvent) are equally in-type; Clarity is a posture variant.
- **"Portfolio/season scope is definitional"** — rejected: single-edition poles (A2Z, Cvent, Whova) and portfolio poles (SponsorCX, Clarity) both satisfy the core; scope is a variant axis.
- **"Property-side sponsorship asset management (sports teams) is this Type"** — partially rejected: SponsorFlo/SponsorCX serve properties selling sponsorship inventory across seasons; when the center is the standing partnership portfolio rather than event-bound inventory, the product sits on the §25/§26 seam. Recorded as straddler, not folded.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Sponsorship Management (§25) — scope/center-of-gravity seam, CONFIRMED from this side; keep-both.** The §25 pass ratified: "the seam is the scope of the program, not the objects." This pass confirms: the object grammar (sponsor/offering/commitment/fulfillment) is identical; §26's center is the event's own placement inventory with event-day-bound fulfillment and post-event value reporting (A2Z, Cvent, Whova poles); §25's center is the organization's standing program with annual levels, member pricing, and renewal relationships (Rhythm/ChamberMaster poles per that pass). Straddlers exist in both directions (Novi per §25; SponsorCX/Clarity per this pass). Removal test: strip the event-placement inventory and event-day fulfillment from an event-suite sponsor module → an org-level sponsorship program remains (§25); strip the standing-program machinery (annual levels, member pricing, multi-year renewal) from a sponsorship product → event-side sponsor management remains (§26). Keep-both ratified.
2. **vs Exhibitor Management (§26) — parallel sibling slice, CONFIRMED; keep-both.** Direct bundling evidence: Cvent's product page and Whova's nav label cover "exhibitor & sponsor" jointly; Cvent configures sponsorship levels inside Exhibitor Management. But populations and obligation sets differ: exhibitor management = company records + participation obligation loop (booth logistics, documents, staff registration) + exhibitor portal; sponsor management = packages/visibility/advertising + asset collection + placement delivery. A sponsor need not exhibit; an exhibitor need not sponsor. Removal test: remove the sponsorship inventory/fulfillment → exhibitor management remains; remove the exhibitor ops → sponsor management remains. Keep-both with the containment pattern the exhibitor pass predicted.
3. **vs Convention / Exhibition Management (§26) — overlapping slice, CONFIRMED; keep-both.** In the show OS, sponsorships are one purchased item alongside floor space (that pass's own note). The show OS's defining core is the produced show + sellable floor + allocation binding; sponsor management is the sponsorship revenue/fulfillment layer that the show OS may embed or omit.
4. **vs Event Management Platform — module-vs-host seam.** EMP is the organizer-side event lifecycle system; sponsor management is one revenue program inside it (A2Z ships it as one solution among many). Remove sponsorship objects → EMP remains; keep-both.
5. **vs Event Mobile App — surface-vs-management seam.** The event app is where delivered sponsor placements *appear* (banners, splash screens, sponsored sessions); sponsor management is the organizer-side machinery that sells and delivers them. The app consumes the commitment records' output.
6. **vs Creator Sponsorship Management (§27) — same word, different object; CONFIRMED (that pass already confirmed from its side).** Creator-side manages a creator's brand deals whose deliverables are content; this Type manages an event's sponsorship inventory sold to companies. Different operator, different object, different fulfillment.
7. **vs fundraising-event sponsor modules (OneCause/GiveSmart/RallyUp) — consistent with the fundraising pass:** sponsorship packages appear there as one event revenue line; the benefit-exchange center belongs here/§25. No conflict.
8. **vs advertising sales (no dedicated leaf)** — app/floor-plan/banner ads are commonly sold *as* sponsorship benefits; the ad-sales blur is content overlap, not a taxonomy conflict (consistent with §25's finding).

## Historical / Market-Sample Check

Pre-digital event sponsorship satisfies all three legs with no software: a printed sponsorship prospectus/level-and-benefit sheet (the inventory), sponsor files with signed agreements and invoice ledgers (the commitment's two sides), and a fulfillment checklist — collect the logo by a date, print the banners, allocate the tickets, staff the hospitality table — executed toward event day, followed by a thank-you letter or attendance figure as the value report. Early web-era events ran the same loop with static PDF prospectuses, email, spreadsheets, and manually placed web/app banners. Therefore: online galleries, cart checkout, e-signatures, automated task assignment, impression/click analytics, AI deck builders, and sponsor portals are all era- or packaging-specific realizations, NOT definitional. The definition names no sales posture, no contract machinery, no marketplace, no analytics depth, no AI.

## Final Synthesis

Sponsor Management (§26, events) is the event organizer's system of record for selling and delivering event sponsorship. Its defining core is three jointly-held structures: the event's sponsorship inventory (the event's own visibility placements packaged into priced, commonly quantity-limited opportunities), the sponsor commitment of record (binding sponsor × package × event, carrying both the money owed and the benefits owed), and the fulfillment-to-event loop (assets collected, placements delivered into the event's surfaces by event day, value reported after). Everything else — galleries, checkouts, portals, contracts, ROI dashboards, AI assistance — is common mature structure or variant machinery. The Type shares its object grammar with §25 Sponsorship Management (scope seam confirmed, keep-both) and is bundled with Exhibitor Management in the market while remaining a distinct population slice (keep-both confirmed). The historical check passes: the paper-era prospectus + sponsor file + fulfillment checklist satisfies the core with no software.
