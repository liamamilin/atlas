## Research Goal

Understand the Application Type **Peer-to-peer Fundraising Platform** (§25) from real products: what its world consists of (supporter pages, teams, campaigns/events, gifts, attribution), who does what (organization staff vs supporter-fundraisers vs donors), how a supporter-led fundraiser runs end-to-end, which rules govern money flow and attribution, and where its boundaries sit against the §25 fundraising-family siblings (**Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform**, **Online Donation Platform** — both processed with standing flags for this pass; **Fundraising Management Platform** — unprocessed), plus **Nonprofit Event Management**, personal/consumer crowdfunding, and social-media fundraising.

## Initial Boundary

Initial hypothesis: the Type is the **supporter-side slice of nonprofit fundraising** — the organization opens a fundraising context (campaign, event, or standing invitation); supporters create and own their own fundraising pages; supporters solicit their personal networks; gifts made through a supporter's page are attributed to that supporter and the money settles to the organization. Nearest neighbors: Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform (org runs the campaign page), Online Donation Platform (org runs the standing give capability), Fundraising Management Platform (org-side program machinery), Nonprofit Event Management (registration/attendance center), personal crowdfunding (GoFundMe-class, money to the page owner), social-media fundraising (platform-native birthday fundraisers).

Key prior risks:

1. This leaf could be merely a Variant of Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform ("crowdfunding where supporters make pages"). The research must test whether supporter page-ownership + delegated solicitation is a genuinely different center.
2. The campaign container might be assumed definitional (inherited from the crowdfunding sibling) — but DIY/third-party fundraising appears to run supporter pages year-round with no campaign. Must test.
3. The donation checkout layer is shared machinery with Online Donation Platform — must not let checkout semantics leak into the P2P definition.

## Research Questions

1. What is the unit of record — the supporter's page, the team, the campaign/event, or the gift? What does a supporter page contain?
2. Who creates pages, how (self-signup, invitation, registration), and under whose branding?
3. What happens when a donor gives through a supporter's page — attribution, roll-up, money destination, receipts?
4. What is the team structure (captains, members, team pages, team goals) and is it definitional?
5. What org context do supporter pages attach to (campaign, event, giving day, year-round invitation, tribute) — and is a campaign container required?
6. What machinery equips and motivates supporters (sharing tools, coaching, gamification, checklists)?
7. What does the org-side console do (setup, templates, approval, participant management, comms, reporting)?
8. Where is the line vs Nonprofit Crowdfunding / Online Donation / Fundraising Management / Event Management / personal crowdfunding / social-media fundraising?

## Canonical Abstraction Hierarchy

### L0 — Defining Invariant (candidate, minimal)

Three jointly-held structures:

1. **The supporter-held fundraising page.** A persistent, individually identified fundraising page created and owned by a supporter — an individual or a team — inside the organization's fundraising context (a campaign, an event, or an always-on invitation), carrying the supporter's own story and goal on a template that carries the organization's branding and donation experience. Remove → an org-run campaign page (Nonprofit Crowdfunding territory) or a standing donation form (Online Donation territory); remove the organization context → personal crowdfunding.
2. **The attribution-and-roll-up gift flow.** Donors give through a supporter's page; each gift is recorded against that page — crediting the fundraiser (and team) who brought it in — and rolls up into the campaign/organization totals, with the money settling to the organization's account. The supporter never holds the funds. Remove the attribution → a generic donation form with a name on it; remove the organization settlement → personal crowdfunding (money to the page owner).
3. **The delegated solicitation role.** The ask to donors is made by the supporter to their own personal network — the platform hands the solicitation job to the supporter and equips it (personal page link, share tools, donor visibility, thank-you and update tools). Remove → the organization soliciting its own donors (Online Donation / Crowdfunding territory); remove the equipping → a bare page with no solicitation machinery.

Jointly-held is load-bearing: 1 alone (supporter page, money to the page owner) = personal/consumer crowdfunding; 1+2 without 3 = a named/tribute donation page the org promotes — the peer dynamic gone; 2+3 without 1 = an org-run campaign with crowd dynamics = Nonprofit Crowdfunding; 3 without 1+2 = social-media fundraising (a birthday fundraiser on a social platform — CauseVox's own FAQ contrast); 1+3 without 2 = a supporter soliciting for their own pocket.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure (B-evidence)

- **Team layer**: team pages with shared goals, captains who manage member pages without org login, recruitment goals, organization/company pages gathering teams (CauseVox org pages; Raisely organisation pages; Give Lively Team Fundraising product)
- **Campaign/event home page as the hub**: total raised, leaderboards, momentum; every page rolls up to it
- **Gamification**: thermometers/progress bars, leaderboards, milestone badges, contests, matching gifts reflected in progress
- **Solicitation equipment**: pre-filled personal links, social/email/SMS sharing with personalized previews, QR generators
- **Supporter stewardship tools**: donor list on the page, one-tap thank-yous, update walls/posts
- **Supporter coaching**: onboarding email journeys, portal checklists, tips/templates, segmented comms to fundraisers (e.g., inactive-fundraiser segments)
- **Org-side console**: campaign/event setup, page templates with branding inheritance, participant management (search/filter by team/status/progress), custom fields, reporting/exports by fundraiser/team
- **Registration configuration** for event P2P: registration types, fees, discount codes, fundraising defaults/minimums, guided registration paths
- **Offline gift entry** counted in totals; **automatic receipts**; donor-covered fees; recurring gifts (inherited from the donation-checkout layer)
- **CRM soft credits**: donor record + fundraiser attribution flowing into the org's donor system
- **Activity tracking**: activity metrics (miles/hours/laps), fitness integrations (Strava-class), real-time activity tracking
- **Donation cart** (give to several fundraisers in one checkout) — single-product evidence (CauseVox), held optional

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- **Org-context shape**: event-based (walk/run/ride with registration), giving/awareness days, DIY/third-party year-round invitation, tribute & memorial pages, ambassador programs, virtual challenges/time-based campaigns
- **Registration coupling**: registration fee + fundraising commitment (event pole) vs open page creation with no campaign (DIY pole)

## Vendor-specific Findings

- **Org-ownership of the donor relationship** is the differentiated philosophy pitch (CauseVox: "every donor lands in your own records attached to the supporter who brought them in… rather than receiving a payout and a list of first names"). This is a philosophy + packaging commonality (B), not a definitional leg — the L0 survives without an in-product CRM (settlement + export satisfies "money to org").
- **The campaign container is NOT definitional for this Type** — the DIY/third-party mode (CauseVox dedicated page; OneCause DIY Supporter Driven; Raisely DIY Fundraising) runs supporter pages year-round with no campaign. This is the sharpest structural difference from the crowdfunding sibling, where the campaign container is the center.
- **Straddling pole re-confirmed**: Bloomerang Fundraising (Qgiv) sells thermometers/leaderboards/personal dashboards under Peer-to-Peer with no top-level crowdfunding product (matches the crowdfunding pass's recording).
- **Marketing uplift claims** ("3x", "65%/10%", "100% more raised") are vendor figures — recorded, not asserted.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform (§25 sibling, processed — DISCHARGES its forward flag).** Keep-both confirmed from this side. The seam is **page ownership + who solicits**: there, the organization runs the campaign and donors give to it (page-owner = org); here, supporters own pages and solicit their networks (page-owner = supporter). The campaign container is definitional there but optional here (DIY year-round mode, A-evidenced). Supporter pages appear in crowdfunding only as an optional overlay rolling up to the org's campaign (CauseVox "Let your supporters carry it"; Chuffed fundraiser/team pages — sibling-pass evidence); in this Type they are the center. Bloomerang Fundraising re-confirmed as the straddling pole (same machinery sold under P2P). Removal tests: strip supporter pages + delegated solicitation from a P2P platform → an org-run campaign/donation capability remains; strip the org-run campaign from crowdfunding → supporter pages remain (P2P). **Verdict: keep both Types.**
2. **vs Online Donation Platform (§25 sibling, processed — DISCHARGES its flag).** Keep-both confirmed. There, the organization runs the give capability and donors give to the org (transaction center, evergreen surfaces); here, supporters run pages and do the asking (page center, delegated solicitation). The donation checkout layer (gift semantics, receipts, donor-covered fees, recurring) is shared machinery — P2P platforms inherit it rather than redefine it; the difference is who holds the page and who solicits. Donation platforms ship P2P as optional modules that roll up to the org (Fundraise Up P2P feature; Raisely P2P/DIY — sibling-pass evidence); P2P platforms ship standing donation forms as suite overlap (CauseVox Donation Forms module). **Verdict: keep both Types; page-owner/solicitor discriminator stands.**
3. **vs Fundraising Management Platform (§25 sibling, UNPROCESSED — flag stands).** Adopting the family grid: this Type is the supporter-facing campaign machinery (supporter pages + delegated solicitation); fundraising management is org-side program machinery across the whole fundraising program (planning, appeals, pipeline, performance). P2P platforms ship org-side slices (participant management, reports, CRM soft credits) — suite drift, not fusion. Joint review when that leaf is processed.
4. **vs Nonprofit Event Management (§26-adjacent §25 sibling, processed — overlap flag addressed).** Events center on registration/attendance with ticket purchases; P2P centers on supporter pages recruiting personal networks. For walks/runs/rides the two are genuinely intertwined (registration creates the fundraiser; the event is the participation vehicle) — vendors themselves split product lines (Qgiv Events vs P2P; OneCause Auctions & Events vs P2P; CauseVox Event Ticketing vs P2P), which supports keep-both with a documented thin seam for endurance events. The giving machinery (pages, attribution, roll-up) is this Type's contribution; logistics (capacity, ticketing, check-in) belong to event management. **Verdict: keep both; thin-seam note stands for walk/run events.**
5. **vs Personal/consumer crowdfunding (no DIRECTORY leaf; consumer territory).** Structural markers: beneficiary (organization-for-cause vs individual/rewards), money destination (org account vs page owner's account), org context (org-controlled branded context vs free-standing page), semantics (charitable gift with receipt vs personal-cause contribution). JustGiving (boundary sample, sibling-pass evidence) runs individual pages for registered charities as its "Fundraising" line — the consumer-side realization of the same page structure — and personal-cause crowdfunding (money to the page owner) as a separate product line; the vendor's own split matches this boundary. When consumer platforms serve organizations with org consoles, they cross into this family.
6. **vs Social-media fundraising (no leaf; platform-native).** Facebook/Instagram birthday fundraisers: the supporter solicits their network but on the social platform's machinery, with the org receiving "a payout and a list of first names" (CauseVox FAQ contrast, A). Bridge features exist (Raisely Facebook Fundraising sync — "we'll keep the two 100% in sync"), confirming adjacency rather than fusion.
7. **vs Donor Management System (processed).** P2P platforms keep light donor records with fundraiser attribution (soft credits) and hand gifts outward to CRMs (CauseVox CRM soft-credit flow; Give Lively Salesforce integration) — the constituency-database center belongs to donor-management-system. Held per the donor pass's discriminators.
8. **vs Church Giving Platform (processed).** Alignment noted: the church pass's cross-reference ("adjacent campaign tooling — supporter-led pages; the giving platform's center is the ongoing congregational giving relationship") matches this pass's center. No open flag; two Types stand.
9. **Taxonomy check (keep-both verdict).** Is this leaf merely a Variant of Nonprofit Crowdfunding Platform? Research verdict: no — supporter page-ownership + delegated solicitation is a different center, realized by products whose P2P line is the *primary* fundraising engine (CauseVox P2P as flagship use case; OneCause P2P solution family; Give Lively's peer-based line), and the market names the category ("peer-to-peer fundraising software" — OneCause; "Peer-to-Peer Fundraising" product lines across the sample). The four §25 leaves form the family grid recorded by the sibling passes; this pass confirms the grid from the supporter side.

## §24 Historical / Market-Sample Check

- **Walk-a-thon / pledge-sheet era (analog)**: a supporter (walker) collects pledges from friends, family, and colleagues on a pledge form the charity provides (org context + branding); each pledge is attributed to the walker's sheet; money is turned in to the charity (never held by the supporter); the supporter does the asking. Satisfies all three L0 legs at analog level. ✓
- **"Friends asking friends" practice**: the name of Blackbaud's heritage peer-to-peer product line is literally the pre-software practice — supporters asking their personal networks on behalf of a cause. The practice predates the software; the software industrializes it. ✓
- **Sponsor forms for charity challenges** (runs, swims, reads): same structure — supporter-held form, per-supporter attribution, money to the organization. ✓
- The definition therefore names **no digital implementation**: no thermometer, no leaderboard, no social network, no registration, no fitness tracking, no team hierarchy. Goal targets, deadlines, thermometers, badges, approval workflows are common implementations (L1); event-vs-DIY-vs-tribute context is a variant axis (L2).
- Era-bound machinery correctly excluded from L0: social sharing previews, QR codes, Strava integrations, Facebook-fundraiser sync, portal checklists, AI assistance.

## Final Synthesis

A **Peer-to-peer Fundraising Platform** is the supporter-side slice of nonprofit fundraising technology: the organization opens a fundraising context — a campaign, an event, or an always-on invitation — and its supporters create and own their own fundraising pages inside it, each carrying the supporter's own story and goal on the organization's branding. The supporter, not the organization, does the asking: the platform equips each supporter to solicit their personal network (personal link, share tools, donor visibility, thank-yous, updates). When a donor gives through a supporter's page, the gift is recorded against that page — crediting the fundraiser and team who brought it in — rolls up into the campaign and organization totals, and the money settles to the organization; the supporter never holds the funds, and the org keeps both the donor and the attribution record. Teams, gamification, coaching journeys, registration, approval workflows, activity tracking, and CRM soft credits are the common mature machinery wrapped around that core; event-based, giving-day, DIY year-round, tribute, ambassador, and virtual-challenge contexts are the variant axes. The Type is era-stable: the walk-a-thon pledge sheet — supporter-held form, per-supporter attribution, money turned in to the charity, supporter doing the asking — satisfies the same three-legged core, so the definition names no digital implementation. Who owns the fundraising page and who does the asking separate it from its siblings: the org owns both in crowdfunding and donation platforms; the program machinery belongs to fundraising management; the constituency database to donor management.
