## Initial Boundary (pre-research hypothesis)

- Hypothesis: Survey Platform is the survey-software Type — an authored question instrument fielded to a respondent population, responses captured as records, results compiled into per-question measurement. This is expected to be the same Type the questionnaire-application pass documented under the instrument-first name; the alias verdict is the default expectation, to be tested against this pass's own product evidence.
- Competing hypothesis: a distinct market meaning exists — e.g., "survey platform" as enterprise experience-management suites (Qualtrics-class) where the program, not the instrument, is the center; or as field-data-collection systems (ODK-class) with enumerator-led deployment.
- Likely confusions: Online Form Builder (per-submission record center), Polling Application (small instrument + instant aggregate), Employee Survey Platform (workforce-bound), Voice of Customer Platform (standing experience program), Consumer Research / Research Panel platforms (audience supply), ePRO/eCOA (regulated study administration), Examination Platform (grading-centered), Audience Response System (live session), A/B Testing Platform (revealed vs stated preference).

## Research Questions

1. What is the survey as a stored object in products that lead with "survey" — questions, answer formats, pages/blocks/groups, logic?
2. Who authors it, and from what (scratch, templates, question banks, import, AI)?
3. How is it fielded — launch/publish state, distribution channels, open/close, quotas, contact lists, panels?
4. What is a response — record structure, partial saves, identity/anonymity posture?
5. What is the loop's output — per-question aggregates, dashboards, exports, statistical formats? Is measurement (aggregate analysis and reporting as the deliverable) the center that separates this Type from the form builder?
6. Does the alias hold from this side: do "survey" products carry exactly the questionnaire machinery, and do "questionnaire"/"form"-vocabulary products carry survey machinery?
7. Historical check: do the paper/mail-survey era and CATI telephone instruments satisfy the derived core without software?
8. Removal tests: what exactly drops out at each seam (form builder, polling, employee survey, VoC, consumer research, ePRO, examination)?
9. Does the field-data-collection family (KoboToolbox/ODK) realize the same core with deployment variants, or constitute a different Type?

## Product Observations

### Qualtrics (evidence layer A — support docs, fetched 2026-09-09)

- The object is the **survey project**. "Using a survey project, you can pick from a wide array of question types, customize what the respondents see as they answer questions, and screen respondents who may not fit your research needs. You can distribute your survey, automate it, analyze the data you've collected, and create reports, all from right inside one project."
- Project tabs: **Survey** (build), **Workflows** (automations), **Distributions** (send), **Data & Analysis** (responses), **Results** (dashboard), **Reports** (paginated PDF-friendly reports). The three-phase loop (build → distribute → analyze) is extended by automation and reporting tabs.
- Survey Builder: questions live in **blocks** ("All survey questions are created, edited, and stored inside blocks"); block options include question randomization and loop & merge; the **Survey Flow** gives a block-level view with elements: Embedded Data, Branch (branch logic), End of Survey, Authenticator ("verify respondents' identities before they take the survey"), Randomizer ("randomly present question blocks"), Web Service. Consent blocks documented for research use.
- Question machinery: "more than a dozen question types (e.g., multiple choice, rank order, NPS)"; matrix table with Likert/constant-sum variations; display logic, skip logic, carry forward choices, recode values ("the coding you set here will be reflected in your reports and raw data, and is used to calculate all statistics"); response requirements (required/format validation); piped text (dynamic pull from previous questions, contact list, embedded data).
- Configuration: Look & Feel (themes, fonts, progress bar); Survey Options (availability timeframe, back button, question numbers; scoring, quotas, translations); **Quotas**; **Translations** ("add multiple languages to a single survey… all of the results will appear in the same data set").
- Lifecycle: Preview → **Publish** ("changes won't be live until you publish"; versions restorable). Live-edit rule: "Only those who have not yet started the survey will see the new changes… We recommend **never deleting** answer choice options or questions… Instead… hide unwanted questions and answer options."
- Distribution: "send your survey to your respondents in many ways, such as email, SMS, or even a single, anonymous link"; contact lists created per distribution method.
- Data & Analysis: "take a look at the individual responses you collected and make edits to them. You can look at data right inside Qualtrics, or export it for analysis in another software."
- Results/Reports: results dashboard ("build your own dashboard pages, complete with unique charts and filters"); paginated PDF reports.
- Workflows (automation): "send another survey a certain number of days after the respondent completes the first survey, or you can create a ticket for your support team if a customer expresses dissatisfaction" — the VoC seam's Tier-1 evidence originates here.

- Survey export: QSF (re-importable), Word (questions/logic/coded values), Print/PDF.

## Canonical Abstraction

### L0 — Defining Invariant (deliberately minimal)

Four jointly-held structures:

1. **The authored question instrument** — a stored, named, re-editable survey: a set of questions, each with a defined answer format (closed options, scales, open text, and commonly matrix batteries, rankings, media), organized into an ordered structure (pages, blocks, or question groups), carrying presentation settings and response-shaping logic (skip/branch, required, validation). Remove it → a distribution tool or a statistics package with nothing to field; what the Type authors no longer exists.
2. **Fielding the instrument to a respondent population** — the survey is launched/published, distributed through channels (link/QR, email/SMS invitations, embeds, panels, kiosks, enumerator devices), and collection opens and closes. Remove it → a questionnaire design document; nothing is ever asked.
3. **The response** — each completion is captured as a record of answers bound to the instrument's questions, with metadata (time, completion state, identity/anonymity posture). Remove it → ephemeral interaction; nothing accumulates.
4. **The compiled results** — responses aggregate into per-question results and an exportable dataset (spreadsheets universally; statistical formats at the research pole), the measurement deliverable the loop exists to produce. Remove it → per-submission records processed one by one (form-builder territory) or a raw log.

Jointly-held is load-bearing:

- 1 alone = a question bank / design document.
- 1+2 without 3+4 = distribution with nothing retained.
- 1+3 without 4 = per-submission records as the center → **Online Form Builder**.
- 2+3 without 1 = capture with no instrument.
- 1+2+3+4 with a single-question instrument and an instant ephemeral aggregate → **Polling Application**.
- The same four legs bound to the workforce population with program machinery → **Employee Survey Platform**.
- The same four legs wrapped in a standing experience program with own-customer anchoring, experience metrics, and follow-up routing → **Voice of Customer Platform**.
- The same four legs plus platform-supplied respondent audiences → **Consumer Research / Research Panel platforms**.

Historical / market-sample check (§24): the paper/mail survey satisfies all four legs without software — a printed instrument (questions, answer options, scales), postal or field administration, returned sheets bound to the instrument, and per-question hand tabulation. Snap's Scanning Edition and printable-instrument exports (LimeSurvey QueXML, recorded by the sibling pass) are the software's own bridges back to that practice. The CATI telephone instrument and the modern web instrument fit the same core. The definition names no channel, no cloud, no AI, no question-type count, and no analysis depth.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure (cross-product, not definitional)

- Creation accelerators: template libraries, question banks/libraries (Qualtrics certified library questions; Alchemer common-answer library; KoboToolbox question library with public collections), duplicate-and-adapt, document import (Alchemer Word import), paste-in authoring, AI drafting (era-current).
- Broad question-type palette: closed choice, rating/Likert/NPS scales, matrix batteries, ranking, open text, numeric/date, file upload, media display; per-question settings (required, validation, randomization).
- Logic: skip/branch, display logic, piping (Qualtrics piped text, Alchemer merge codes, Snap text substitution), carry-forward choices, calculated values.
- Presentation: themes/branding, progress indication, mobile-responsive fill; conversational/one-question-per-page poles (Typeform pole).
- Distribution machinery: link/QR, email/SMS invitations with templates and reminders, embeds, kiosk, offline apps; response windows and expiration (Qualtrics survey options timeframe; Snap schedules).
- Respondent-population machinery: contact lists, tokens/participant tracking, quotas (Qualtrics quotas; Snap quotas/screenouts), one-response controls, partial save-and-resume (recorded by the sibling pass for LimeSurvey/SoSci).
- Results & analysis: per-question summaries/charts, individual response browsing (with owner-side editing at the enterprise pole — Qualtrics), cross-tabs, dashboards, bulk/segmented report generation (Snap Smart Reports).
- Data exit: Excel/CSV universally; SPSS/R/Stata-class formats at the research pole (KoboToolbox SPSS/Stata conversion; SoSci/LimeSurvey recorded by the sibling pass); APIs.
- Collaboration & governance: teams, roles/permissions, project sharing, enterprise security packaging (SSO, encryption, compliance certifications — Snap ISO 27001), multilingual instruments (Qualtrics single-survey translations; Snap RTL support).
- Methodology-grade extras at the research pole: randomization/rotation, weighting, in-product statistical analysis, printable instrument documentation, consent blocks (Qualtrics consent-block guidance).

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- **Analysis depth axis**: simple in-product summaries (mass-market) ↔ in-product statistics/weighting (research-grade) ↔ external-statistics handoff as the documented workflow (academic pole). Depth is a variant axis; the existence of compiled results is not.
- **Multi-mode collection**: paper scanning (Snap Scanning Edition), offline interviewing (Snap Offline Interviewer, KoboCollect), kiosks, CATI heritage — mixed-mode integration as a differentiator of the research-grade pole.
- **Field/enumerator deployment**: interviewer-led, offline-capable collection in development/humanitarian contexts (KoboToolbox pole) — same core, deployment variant.
- **Paid respondent supply**: audience/panel purchase (SurveyMonkey Audience and Alchemer Panel Services class) — a commercial variant, not the center.
- **Self-hosting / on-premises**: open-source cores (LimeSurvey, KoboToolbox), licensed on-premises editions (Snap, SoSci server, WJX local deployment).
- **Regional super-instrument packaging**: questionnaire platforms extending into exams/votes/forms/assessments as sibling modes (WJX; Alchemer's Create Form/Poll/Quiz as capability-level straddle).
- **Conversational/design-led presentation** as the differentiating philosophy (Typeform).
- **Enterprise suite embedding**: the survey core inside experience-management suites (Qualtrics XM) — when program machinery (metrics, follow-up routing, dashboards-as-programs) becomes the center, the product crosses into VoC/CEM territory.

## Final Synthesis

The Survey Platform is the survey-software Type: an authored question instrument (questions with defined answer formats, ordered structure, response-shaping logic) is fielded to a population of respondents through distribution channels under open/close control; each completion is captured as a response record bound to the instrument; and responses compile into per-question results and an exportable dataset — the measurement deliverable the loop exists to produce. The alias with Questionnaire Application is ratified from this side: the two directory entries name one Type (activity/measurement angle vs instrument angle), with a different product sample independently reaching the same core. The defining seams hold: the form builder keeps the per-submission record as its center; the poll keeps the small instrument with the instant aggregate; the workforce-bound, program-wrapped, audience-supplying, protocol-scheduled, and grading-centered neighbors each add structure this generic core does not carry. The field-data-collection family (KoboToolbox) verifies as a variant of this Type, not a separate one. The core predates all current machinery: the paper/mail survey and the CATI instrument satisfy it without software, so the definition names no channel, era, or analysis depth.
