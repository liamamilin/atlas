# Research Notes — PACS

Research date: 2026-09-08
Directory leaf: "PACS" (§22 Healthcare & Life Sciences)
Slug: pacs

## Research Goal

Understand, from real products, what a PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System) actually is and how it works: the objects of its world (patient, study, series, image/instance, modality, worklist, archive, route rule, report link), the canonical acquisition→archive→reading→distribution lifecycle, its interfaces and roles, and — critically — its boundary against the neighboring leaves: Radiology Information System / RIS (unprocessed sibling, directly adjacent), Medical Image Analysis Platform (unprocessed), Electronic Health Record / EHR, Health Information Exchange / HIE, plus non-directory neighbors (VNA / enterprise imaging archive, teleradiology services, generic media asset management).

## Initial Boundary Hypothesis (Step 1)

- Core use: the imaging department's image system of record — images acquired from modalities (CT/MR/US/CR/DX/PET), archived per patient/exam, and delivered for diagnostic reading and clinical distribution.
- Primary users: radiologists (readers), radiographers/technologists (acquisition, QC), referring clinicians (view results), PACS/IT administrators (devices, routing, storage, access).
- Nearest Types: RIS (administrative/workflow side: scheduling, orders, report text), Medical Image Analysis Platform (computation on images), EHR (care record), HIE (cross-organization exchange), VNA/enterprise imaging (archive decoupled from department), MAM/DAM (generic media assets — same word "archive", different world).
- Suspected seam: "RIS/PACS" suite packaging may blur the RIS boundary; "enterprise imaging" may blur the archive boundary. Unknowns going in: whether device-ingest is definitional or just universal; whether DICOM is invariant or implementation; whether reporting belongs in the core; how the emergency/unknown-patient path works; whether the reading worklist is PACS or RIS territory.

## Research Questions (Step 2)

1. What objects exist in a PACS world and how are they related (patient, study, series, instance, modality, worklist entry, route, report link)?
2. How do images get in — what does the device→PACS interface look like, and is it definitional?
3. What is the canonical study hierarchy and how are studies identified (UIDs, patient identity, accession numbers, emergency/reconciliation paths)?
4. How does diagnostic reading work (worklist, viewer tools, display/hanging protocols, prior comparison)?
5. How does distribution to clinicians/referrers/patients work (web viewers, portals, share links, exports)?
6. What archive machinery exists (short-term vs long-term tiers, redundancy, migration, retrieval)?
7. What rules govern integrity, metadata correctness, access, audit, and privacy?
8. How does it interface with RIS/EHR (modality worklist, orders, ADT, report linkage), and where does the RIS boundary sit?
9. What roles exist and how do they differ (radiologist, technologist, clinician, administrator, patient)?
10. Where are the boundaries vs RIS, Medical Image Analysis, VNA/enterprise imaging, teleradiology, EHR/HIE, MAM?

## Representative Products (Step 3)

Chosen for market representation + different philosophies + different customer tiers + documentation accessibility:

1. **Orthanc** (open-source standalone DICOM server) — the minimal pole. Its official Orthanc Book is the only true operator-level documentation reached this pass; it documents the device-interface protocol, the study hierarchy, and query/retrieve mechanics at full depth. Serves the §24 historical/minimal-shape check: the core with no cloud, no AI, no RIS, no VNA.
2. **RamSoft PowerServer** (cloud-based RIS/PACS suite; imaging centers, teleradiology groups, small/mid hospitals; since 1994) — the mid-market cloud suite pole with an edition ladder (LITE PACS → PACS → RIS/PACS → TELE PLUS) and explicit routing/pre-caching/worklist behavior.
3. **Intelerad IntelePACS** (mid-market/enterprise radiology groups and hospitals; now part of GE HealthCare) — the reading-workflow pole (InteleViewer, Read From Anywhere); worklist orchestration and VNA packaged as separate products, which cleanly evidences the PACS/VNA and PACS/worklist seams.
4. **Sectra IDS7 / Sectra Enterprise Imaging** (hospital/enterprise pole; Best in KLAS claims; 2,500+ sites) — the security-first enterprise pole; richest evidence on viewer structure (Information/Image/Quick windows), display protocols, audit machinery, and STS→LTS archive tiering.
5. **PostDICOM** (small-practice cloud PACS, SaaS) — the small-clinic pole with public operational FAQ documentation: Medical Device Communicator, auto-routing after acquisition, zero-footprint CE Class IIb viewer, share links, multi-site routing.

Rejected/adjusted samples: Epic, Agfa, GE Centricity, Siemens not attempted — after the five samples the stop conditions were already met (the same three-part core reappeared in every product across all poles; new samples would mostly repeat evidence), and the LIS pass recorded the same enterprise-EHR-vendor access pattern as blocked. No sample was abandoned mid-research this pass.

## Product Observations

### Orthanc (evidence layer A)

- Self-description: "a lightweight, yet powerful standalone DICOM server for healthcare and medical research" — i.e., the ingest+archive+Q/R core of a PACS without the department workflow.
- Device ingestion, step by step: the modality's own network settings contain a panel "in which you can configure a PACS or a target DICOM Server"; the administrator registers the server's AET/IP/port, tests with C-Echo, and the modality then "send[s] data from your modality to your Orthanc Server." The server side declares each modality (AET, IP, port) in its configuration.
- DICOM model of the real world (citing the DICOM standard itself): a patient has a set of imaging studies over life; each study is made of series; each series is a set of instances (single DICOM files). A series can be "a single 2D image (standard digital radiography), a single 3D volume (CT-scan), or a 2D+t cine sequence", or even "a single PDF report, a structured report, a 3D+t image". Pixel data of one series spreads across many instance files ("a single huge image… 4GB… hundreds of small files").
- Identifiers: PatientID (unique within a hospital, NOT globally), StudyInstanceUID / SeriesInstanceUID / SOPInstanceUID globally unique by standard mandate; AccessionNumber associates a study when the RIS "might not have previously encountered the imaged patient" (emergency imaging) — "the patient module will be injected later on in the PACS once the administrative information is available, as part of a reconciliation process."
- Metadata travels with pixels: each DICOM file embeds a data set (tags key-value with sequences) alongside PixelData; mandatory/optional tags per modality defined by storage SOP classes; lossless compression "should always be favored to avoid any loss of medical information"; few devices besides the PACS itself support compression.
- Network services: C-Echo (connectivity test), C-Store (send images), C-Find (search at patient/study/series query levels with tag filters), C-Move (three-party query/retrieve — "the most popular and widespread way to initiate a query/retrieve against a PACS server"), C-Get; DICOMweb (STOW/QIDO/WADO) as the HTTP alternative; SCU/SCP roles; AET unique within a hospital intranet; "the DICOM protocol inherently targets the Intranet of a single hospital, not the Internet or the cloud."
- Viewing: Orthanc Explorer web UI browses patient→study→series→instance with per-image preview (on-the-fly PNG conversion); Stone Web Viewer plugin exists as a full web viewer. Sending: any DICOM resource can be manually "sent to remote modality" (C-Store SCU).
- No RIS, no reading worklist, no archive tiering, no display protocols observed — the minimal shape documents exactly which parts are core machinery vs department furniture.

## Canonical Model (Step 5–7)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

Three jointly-held structures; the whole bound as the imaging facility's image system of record:

1. **Direct acquisition-device ingest** — images enter the system directly from the imaging modalities as they are produced: each device is configured with the system as its send destination, and each image carries patient/exam metadata stamped at acquisition (device→system push over a device-interface protocol; DICOM is the universal current realization). Remove → a post-hoc import store / VNA-style enterprise archive / a pile of files nobody's devices feed.
2. **The imaging study archive of record** — every study is held persistently, bound to an identified patient and organized in the exam hierarchy (patient → study → series → image), retained beyond the live exam as the facility's authoritative copy of what was acquired. Remove → live viewing/relay with no memory; the "Archive" of the name disappears.
3. **Diagnostic-grade delivery of studies** — the archive is served to readers: radiologists read through diagnostic viewers (series navigation, window/level, measurement, layout/display protocols, prior comparison), and studies are distributed onward to referring clinicians and increasingly patients through lighter viewers. Remove → a storage appliance nobody reads from.

Jointly-held load-bearing tests:
- 1+2 without 3 = modality-fed archive with no reading/distribution surface — a storage appliance, not a communication system.
- 1+3 without 2 = live device-to-viewer relay — a teleradiology relay/mini-viewer, not the archiving system.
- 2+3 without 1 = archive + viewers fed by import/CD — a VNA/enterprise archive or a viewer with a bucket, not the department's acquisition-integrated system.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

- **DICOM as the universal realization** of the device interface and study format: file format (embedded metadata tags + pixel data, per-modality storage classes, lossless compression preferred) and network services (C-Echo/C-Store/C-Find/C-Move, increasingly DICOMweb STOW/QIDO/WADO). Universal in the current market — but the canonical leg is "device-interface ingest with per-exam metadata," not DICOM itself (see anti-overfitting note below).
- **Query/retrieve and routing** — studies found by patient/study attributes and moved between systems; rules-based auto-routing and pre-fetching of relevant studies (e.g., priors) to reading stations.
- **Reading worklist** — the department's queue of studies to read, with prioritization; often fed from the scheduling/ordering system (RIS); in mature products carries status/SLA indicators.
- **Diagnostic viewer toolkit** — series navigation and layout, window/level adjustment, zoom/rotate, measurement and annotation, MPR/3D and fusion in advanced form, display (hanging) protocols configured per role/user/modality.
- **Prior comparison** — the longitudinal archive is consulted for prior studies/reports during reading.
- **Ingest-time image QC** — technologist quality/completeness checks before studies enter the record.
- **Clinical distribution surface** — web/zero-footprint viewers for referrers, EMR-embedded access, share links, export/CD-publisher replacement.
- **Report linkage** — studies read together with their reports; report creation may sit in the PACS (integrated reporting), in a RIS, or as an add-on — but the study↔report association is standard.
- **RIS/HIS/EHR interfaces** — modality worklist/orders (where a scheduling system exists), ADT updates, HL7/DICOM/IHE/FHIR integration.
- **Archive tiering and redundancy** — fast short-term storage for recent work, long-term archive behind it (hybrid or cloud), redundancy, migration.
- **Security/audit/privacy machinery** — role-based access, audit trails, encryption, break-the-glass, privacy-regime compliance (HIPAA/GDPR-class postures named across the sample).
- **Non-DICOM content alongside DICOM** — photos, videos, PDFs, reports attached to the imaging record (strong at the enterprise and cloud poles).

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- Deployment: on-premises / cloud / hybrid (all poles in-sample; cloud the fastest-growing posture per vendor pages).
- Scale and edition ladder: small-practice PACS → imaging-center PACS → RIS/PACS suite → enterprise platform (explicit ladder at RamSoft; tier pricing at PostDICOM).
- Department scope: radiology-first vs multi-specialty enterprise imaging (cardiology, breast, digital pathology, ophthalmology, orthopaedics) — the enterprise-imaging convergence is the major current boundary blur.
- VNA relationship: PACS-embedded archive vs standalone VNA product paired with PACS (Sectra VNA, InteleShare VNA, OmegaAI RIS/PACS/VNA).
- Teleradiology packaging: dedicated editions/solutions for distributed reading workforces.
- Patient access: portals / share links (common at consumer-facing poles, absent elsewhere).
- AI depth: none → partner integration → marketplace/orchestration.
- Teaching/research: education portals, teaching libraries, anonymized research modules.
- Subject breadth: human patients dominant; veterinary poles exist (PostDICOM veterinarians solution; Intelerad veterinary customer).
- Regulatory regimes: FDA 510(k)/CE class/ISO 13485 postures; regional privacy regimes.

## Anti-overfitting Note

All five sampled products (and the whole current market) realize the device interface and study format in DICOM — but DICOM is the standard realization, not the invariant: the canonical leg is "images enter directly from acquisition devices carrying patient/exam metadata." Early departmental PACS predate the current standard (the DICOM protocol's own documentation describes it as historically point-to-point and one of the earliest service protocols, long predating modern web services — Orthanc Book, layer A), and a device link on a proprietary protocol would still make the system recognizably a PACS. Equally, "radiology" is the dominant context but not the boundary: cardiology, mammography, digital pathology, ophthalmology PACS modules and veterinary poles all satisfy the core. The L0 deliberately names neither DICOM versions nor radiology.

## Vendor-specific Findings

- Sectra's viewer is organized as three named windows (Information/Image/Quick) with QC as a dedicated surface; RamSoft expresses QC as "full QC capabilities" in its mini-PACS viewer; the others do not expose a named QC surface on fetched pages — ingest-QC machinery is common in the market but its surface shape varies (→ L1 with shape variance).
- Intelerad sells worklist orchestration and the enterprise archive as separate products from IntelePACS — the clearest packaging evidence that worklist orchestration and VNA are separable from the PACS core.
- RamSoft and PostDICOM both make routing rules a first-class product concern (Gateway Router; auto-routing after acquisition with per-location tagging); Orthanc documents only manual send on fetched pages — routing sophistication varies but rules-based routing is standard in department products.
- PostDICOM states a deletion-is-permanent caveat and recommends an independent secondary archive — a cloud-SaaS custody nuance absent from on-prem-pole pages.
- Reporting ownership varies structurally: integrated add-on (Sectra Reporting), RIS-owned (PowerServer RIS/PACS), viewer-integrated report creation (PostDICOM) — report custody is not standardized within the Type.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Radiology Information System / RIS** (nearest sibling; unprocessed leaf). The RIS is the department's administrative/workflow system of record: scheduling, orders, patient registration context, report text, billing handoff. The PACS is the image system of record: acquisition ingest, archive, viewing. They interlock — the modality worklist and orders flow RIS→modality, images flow modality→PACS, the report is created in/linked from the workflow side and displayed with the study. Remove the images from a PACS and you have something RIS-shaped (order/report workflow); remove scheduling/orders/report-text from a RIS/PACS suite and you have the PACS. Suites bundle both ("RIS/PACS"), and the boundary is vendor-acknowledged: RamSoft sells PowerServer PACS and PowerServer RIS/PACS as separate editions; Sectra sells Reporting as an add-on; Intelerad keeps worklist orchestration separate. **Forward flag for the RIS pass: the reading-worklist/orders/report-linkage seam and RIS/PACS suite packaging should be jointly reviewed; this pass holds the seam at "images vs workflow."**
2. **vs Medical Image Analysis Platform** (unprocessed leaf). Analysis products compute on images (detection, quantification, planning) and produce derived output; the PACS holds and delivers the primary record. In-sample evidence of separateness: AI/analysis is packaged as separate products/marketplaces (InteleGence, Sectra Amplifier, RamSoft AI solutions) that integrate *into* PACS viewers. The PACS's computation is confined to display-domain transforms and measurement tools in the viewer.
3. **vs VNA / enterprise imaging archive.** A VNA holds multi-department, multi-vendor archives in vendor-neutral form and does not own acquisition or diagnostic reading; the PACS owns the device link and the reading workflow. Market straddling is heavy (Sectra VNA + IDS7; InteleShare VNA + IntelePACS; OmegaAI "RIS/PACS/VNA"; Sectra Enterprise archive module) — recorded as the Type's main current boundary blur (enterprise-imaging convergence), not a merge.
4. **vs EHR.** The EHR holds the care record; images remain custodied in the PACS, surfaced through EMR-embedded viewers and links (Sectra "universal viewer… EMR integration"; RamSoft HL7/IHE EMR integration). No in-sample product suggests the EHR becomes the image archive.
5. **vs Health Information Exchange / HIE.** HIE moves records/images across organizations; the PACS is the intra-facility system of record that feeds such exchange (Sectra Image Exchange Portal as a separate sharing product).
6. **vs Media Asset Management / DAM.** Same word "archive," different world: MAM holds media assets for creative/broadcast purposes; the PACS holds patient-bound diagnostic studies with medical metadata and regulatory custody. The patient/exam binding and diagnostic purpose are the discriminator.
7. **Standalone DICOM viewers** (desktop/web viewers without archive or device ingest) sit below the Type — a delivery surface without the record.
8. **Teleradiology services/platforms** have no directory leaf; distributed reading is realized as a packaging variant of this Type (TELE PLUS, Read From Anywhere, teleradiology solution poles) rather than a separate one.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check (§24)

- **Film-less radiology department (pre-cloud, pre-AI era)** — modalities push images to a central server, radiologists read on diagnostic workstations with prior comparison, clinicians view on lighter clients: satisfies all three L0 legs with none of the modern machinery (cloud, AI, VNA, portals, DICOMweb). Conceptual check; no fetched source asserts dates (Wikipedia unreachable) — kept qualitative.
- **Minimal pole today** — Orthanc: device ingest + indexed archive + query/retrieve + web viewing with no RIS, no tiering, no protocols: satisfies the core. The extra machinery in commercial products is demonstrably additive.
- **Protocol-era check** — the device-interface leg does not depend on the current DICOM standard (early systems predate it; the standard's own documentation describes the older point-to-point heritage). Defining "PACS" by DICOM would be era-overfitting; the L0 abstracts to device-interface ingest.
- **Scope check** — cardiology, breast, pathology, ophthalmology PACS modules (Sectra) and veterinary poles (PostDICOM, Intelerad customer) satisfy the core; "radiology-only" would be market-overfitting. The L0 says "medical imaging."

## Final Synthesis

A PACS is the medical-imaging facility's image system of record, defined by three jointly-held structures: images enter directly from the acquisition devices as they are produced, each carrying patient/exam metadata; those images are held persistently as per-patient studies organized in the patient→study→series→image hierarchy, as the facility's authoritative copy; and the archive is served to people — radiologists through diagnostic viewers on worklists, referrers and increasingly patients through lighter viewers. DICOM is the universal current realization of the device interface and study format but is not the invariant; radiology is the dominant context but not the boundary. Everything else — worklists, display protocols, QC, routing rules, archive tiering, EMR integration, reporting, distribution portals, AI, cloud deployment, multi-specialty breadth — is standard or optional machinery layered on that core. The nearest Type is the RIS (workflow/report side of the same department); the blurriest edge is the enterprise-imaging convergence with VNA archives.
