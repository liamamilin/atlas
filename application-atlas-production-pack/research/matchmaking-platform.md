# Research Notes — Matchmaking Platform

Cross-references: research/dating-application.md §Boundary Findings (records the intra-family §01.07 joint-review flag with this leaf: "who selects the candidates" test); research/dating-community-platform.md §Boundary Findings (seam vs this leaf recorded as unchanged). This pass applies and discharges the matchmaking-platform side of that flag.

## Research Goal

Understand the directory leaf "Matchmaking Platform" (§01.07 Dating & Relationship Discovery) as a real market of products: what a matchmaking platform actually is, who uses it, what structures exist inside it, how the matchmaking loop actually runs, and where it ends relative to the Dating Application sibling and other neighbors.

## Initial Boundary (working hypothesis before research)

- Working hypothesis: a Matchmaking Platform is a service-led partner-finding product — a service (human matchmaker and/or curated machinery) selects and introduces candidates, in contrast with the Dating Application where the user self-serves the candidate loop (profile → discover → like/pass → gate → chat).
- Expected neighbors: Dating Application (sharpest seam), Relationship Discovery Application and Dating Community Platform (siblings), Friend Discovery Application, professional/B2B products that borrow the word "matchmaking" (recruiting, event buyer–seller appointments).
- Open questions at start: is the human matchmaker definitional or is curated machinery enough? Is in-app chat part of the core (as it is in Dating Application)? Is selective admission definitional? Where do matrimonial platforms sit? Does an offline agency with thin software still count as a "platform"?

## Research Questions

1. Who are the users, and is the population symmetric (everyone both selects and is selected) or asymmetric (served clients vs candidate pool)?
2. What does the service actually hold? (member records, intake depth, screening data)
3. What is the unit of delivery — a browsable stream, a deck, or a named introduction?
4. How does an introduction become a real contact/meeting? Who controls that activation?
5. What happens after a date, and how do outcomes feed back into selection?
6. What role does software play vs the human service? How thin can the member-facing surface be and still be a "platform"?
7. What is screened/vetted, and what is confidential until when?
8. How is the service sold (packages, quotas, guarantees, refunds)?
9. Where are the boundaries with Dating Application, matrimonial platforms, community platforms, and B2B "matchmaking"?
10. Would older/regional/offline matchmaking (shadchan-class, arranged-marriage introducers, lunch-date agencies) still fit the definition?

## Product Observations

### Tawkify — evidence layer A (how-it-works + FAQ)

- Positioning: "#1 matchmaker in America"; "we handle the searching, screening, and scheduling for you"; explicitly anti-app: "Forget endless swiping"; "We don't rely on impersonal algorithms or profile browsing."
- **Two-sided, asymmetric population**: clients (served; dedicated matchmaker; packages) vs members ("Members join our candidate pool and may be matched with clients, but they do not work directly with a matchmaker"). FAQ frames it as "gym membership versus hiring a personal trainer."
- Intake: application/onboarding flow ("We only accept candidates we believe we can match"); 60-minute onboarding call with the matchmaker; matchmaker "spend[s] time to understand you, your values, and what you're looking for."
- Selection: matchmaker "hand selects matches"; searches the network (claimed 3M+) "and beyond our network to find exceptional candidates."
- Vetting: "criminal record screenings & video screenings for all candidates"; team "vet[s] and video screen[s] each potential match."
- Delivery: one match at a time (claimed ~1/month); **blind-date model** — "you receive a verbal description of your match, but no photos before meeting" (per VIDA's comparison, corroborated by Tawkify's own "Why does Tawkify use a blind date approach?" FAQ: judgments based solely on photos end potential matches; physical preferences still considered).
- Activation: service curates and plans the entire date ("curate the entire date experience based on your preferences"; "handle all the logistics from scheduling and planning your date"; live date support). Contact info confidential "until both parties choose to share."
- Feedback: "Share feedback after each date … so they can keep improving your matches"; "Ongoing refinement of your matching criteria based on feedback."
- Commercial: packages by features/match count; Premier tier (custom dates, multiple/unlimited search locations, second-date planning, expanded criteria); trial (3 or 6 hand-selected matches); "guaranteed matches"; money-back framing ("or your money back").
- Extras: relationship coaching (optional purchase), private events, tools; member portal/app login exists (app.tawkify.com).
- Confidentiality: "Your profile remains confidential within our network, and you'll never have to worry about colleagues or friends stumbling across your dating profile online."

### Lunch Actually — evidence layer A (home + service page + FAQs)

- Positioning: "Dating & Matchmaking Agency"; "Asia's first and largest lunch dating company"; since 2004; presence in 6 countries; targets busy single professionals seeking long-term relationships; discretion emphasized ("Many of our members would never use less discreet dating platforms").
- Intake: registration form → invitation to office; "We invite every potential member to our office for a chat to verify each person's identification and also to sense out unsuitable individuals" (strict verification); complimentary consultation with a dating consultant ("learn more about your profile, preference and dating goals").
- Selection: "We will match you based on your profile and preferences and arrange the date for you"; "screening through every profile, handpicking your compatible matches" within the Lunch Actually database; consultants trained in the Matchmaking Institute system; "well-tested and well-proven matching algorithm" assisting (hybrid human + algorithm).
- Delivery/activation: **arranged date** — service books the restaurant, sends reminders ("We do all the work for you and your date, from matching, restaurant booking and date reminder. You just have all the fun!"); date formats: lunch, afternoon tea, after-work drinks, weekend brunch — "you dictate the time of your dates."
- Confidentiality: "Lunch Actually does not show your picture to your potential date, and vice versa. When arranging for you to meet your date in a restaurant, your last name and telephone number are both kept confidential as well. After your date, it is both of your prerogatives to decide whether you wish to exchange last names and contact details." (Blind-date posture + post-date mutual disclosure.)
- Feedback: "Give us feedback afterwards as we get closer to finding The One"; "Receive constant follow-up after each date as we will take your feedback."
- Commercial: custom-priced "Dating Success Program"; **6-Month No Match Policy** (refund of a percentage of membership fees if no match within stated Basic Criteria in 6 months; voided by presented/rejected match, refined criteria, marriage, etc.); guaranteed dates framing; fees non-refundable/non-transferable otherwise.
- Success framing: measured by member match ratings (claimed 85% satisfactory-to-excellent), not marriages; "we do not position ourselves as a marriage agency."
- Sourcing hint: coaching "helped thousands of singles find … relationships, even with other singles who are not in our database."
- Software surface: member app exists ("LA App — Dating For Professionals" on app stores); CRM investment mentioned (back-office); the core loop runs through consultants.

### VIDA Select — evidence layer A (difference page + FAQ)

- Positioning: "modern matchmaking service for accomplished professionals"; "Most matchmakers limit you to small pools and rigid contracts"; month-to-month flexibility.
- Intake: brief assessment (claimed <5 min) → qualification gate → complimentary consultation; "We say no more than we say yes. In fact, we turn away about 7 out of 10 applicants"; assessment on "whether your preferences, mindset, and goals align with our success patterns."
- Selection: dedicated matchmaker; "29-factor compatibility framework"; **hybrid/dual sourcing** — internal matchmaker-vetted database + "active scouting across today's dating and social platforms"; six sourcing channels claimed (internal database, nationwide scout network, direct outreach, social platforms, select dating apps, client referrals); "your matchmaker can find the right person whether they're already in our network or not."
- Delivery: **client pre-approval model** — "You review complete profiles with photos before agreeing to any introduction. Nothing proceeds without your explicit approval"; on guaranteed packages "you can decline up to 2 out of 3 matches we present." Explicit anti-blind-date positioning vs Tawkify.
- Activation: "We handle all communications, scheduling, and logistics. You simply show up" — the service mediates outreach to (possibly externally sourced) candidates and schedules the date.
- Feedback: "Your feedback after each date sharpens our search"; "continuously improves with your feedback."
- Commercial: monthly memberships (claimed from $1,695/month) up to results-guaranteed programs; guarantee framing ("at least 5 dating opportunities with approved matches in 3 months or we continue at no extra cost"); optional coaching/image consultation.
- Cadence claims: first match usually 7–10 days after setup; 82% meet someone within 90 days, typically after 4–6 introductions (vendor claims).

### Stable commonalities (all three, layer A)

1. A service-run member/candidate base with service-operated intake (consultation/interview/assessment) deeper than a self-serve profile.
2. The service — not the user — selects the candidates and delivers them as **named introductions**.
3. The user does **not** comb a searchable candidate pool; browsing is explicitly replaced ("We don't rely on … profile browsing"; "handpicking"; "we search … to find your ideal match").
4. Activation of an introduction into a real meeting runs through **service-mediated steps** (date planning/booking/scheduling; communications handled by the service).
5. Identity/contact disclosure is **withheld until a mediated point** (blind descriptions; first names only; contact shared only when both choose / after the date).
6. A **post-date feedback loop** feeds outcomes back into future selection.
7. Candidate **screening/vetting** before introduction (ID verification, background/video screening, matchmaker-vetted database).
8. **Selective admission** (application/consultation; "we only accept …"; turning away applicants).
9. Commercial packages with **introduction quotas or guarantees** and refund policies tied to match delivery.
10. A **served-client population distinct from the candidate population** (candidate-pool members and/or externally sourced candidates).
11. Optional human-development add-ons: coaching, date support, events.

### Pole-specific differences

- Who approves the introduction: nobody pre-meets (Tawkify blind) ↔ service-arranged without photos (LA) ↔ client pre-approval with full profile (VIDA). A spectrum of client control over the gate.
- Where candidates come from: closed member base (LA) ↔ base + beyond-network search (Tawkify) ↔ base + aggressive external scouting (VIDA).
- Software thickness: portal/app (Tawkify, LA) ↔ service-mediated funnel (VIDA — thinnest observed member-facing software).
- Geography/format: US network ↔ Asian arranged-lunch format ↔ US virtual/modern.

## Canonical Model (four-layer abstraction)

### L0 — Defining Invariant

A Matchmaking Platform is a software-operated partner-matching service. Three jointly-held structures; remove any one and the product stops being this Type:

1. **The service-run member base of record** — the platform holds member profiles built through service-operated intake (consultation, interview, questionnaire), carrying enough depth (values, goals, preferences, life context) for a third party to select on the member's behalf. The profile is written to be **evaluated by the service**, not primarily browsed by other members. (Remove → a dating application's self-serve profile pool, or a directory.)
2. **Service-led selection delivered as introductions** — the service (human matchmaker and/or service-operated curation machinery) selects specific candidates for romantic partnership on a member's behalf and presents them as **named introductions**; the member does not comb a searchable candidate pool. (Remove → Dating Application: the user self-serves discovery.)
3. **Service-mediated activation of the introduction** — an introduction becomes a real contact/meeting only through steps the service controls: acceptance/approval gates and/or service-arranged date coordination, with identity and contact details withheld until that mediated point (or exchanged by both parties' choice afterward). (Remove → a listings/directory surface, or a plain referral service with no managed loop.)

The romantic/marital-partnership purpose is carried inside structures 1–2 (intake and selection are oriented to partner selection). Software is the substrate of all three structures — that is what makes it a platform rather than an offline agency; the offline agency is the historical ancestor (see Historical Check).

Note what is NOT in L0: in-app chat (not observed as the primary contact mechanism in any sampled product — the conversation happens on the service-arranged date or off-platform), swiping/likes, user search, algorithms as such, subscriptions, mobile apps.

### L1 — Common Mature Structure

Present across the researched sample; expected in mature products but not definitional:

- Post-introduction feedback loop refining future selections (3/3 sampled; the operational loop that makes matchmaking a managed process rather than a one-shot delivery)
- Candidate screening/vetting before introduction: identity verification, background checks, video screening, matchmaker-vetted databases
- Confidentiality posture: no publicly browsable profiles; first names / verbal descriptions; contact withheld until the mediated point or mutual choice
- Date coordination and logistics handled by the service (booking, scheduling, reminders, live date support)
- Selective admission (application/consultation; rejection of unsuitable applicants)
- A dedicated matchmaker/consultant relationship for served clients
- Asymmetric population: served clients vs candidate pool (members and/or externally sourced candidates)
- Commercial packages with introduction quotas/guarantees and delivery-tied refund policies
- Coaching / date-support services and member events (optional add-ons in all three)
- Member-facing software surfaces (portal/app), thin to moderate

### L2 — Variant / Optional Structure

- **Selection agent**: human matchmaker (all sampled) vs curated machinery (algorithm-delivered matches without a human — eHarmony-class; NOT directly evidenced this pass; see Uncertainties) vs hybrid (human + algorithm assist — LA's "matching algorithm")
- **Pre-introduction disclosure posture**: blind (verbal description only) ↔ no photos but arranged date ↔ full profile + photo pre-approval — a spectrum of client control over the gate
- **Candidate sourcing**: closed member base ↔ base + beyond-network search ↔ external scouting across dating apps/social platforms/scouts/referrals
- **Where the consent gate sits**: service-decided (blind) ↔ client pre-approval (explicit accept/decline, possibly with decline caps) ↔ post-date mutual disclosure of contact
- Introduction cadence/quotas (one-at-a-time; monthly; guaranteed counts) — shape common, numbers vendor-specific
- Regional/community formats: arranged lunch/tea dates (Asian agencies); religious/community matchmaker networks (shadchan-class; market context); marriage-hunting (konkatsu) services (market context); assisted-matrimony relationship-manager services (market context)
- Intent specialization: long-term relationship vs marriage-oriented
- Offline member events; guarantee/refund structures; membership tiers

### L3 — Vendor-specific Structure (research notes only)

- Tawkify: Premier tier feature set; claimed 3M+ network, 6.5× effectiveness, 92% satisfaction, 80% client success, ~1 match/month, 60-minute onboarding; "gym membership vs personal trainer" framing.
- VIDA Select: 29-factor framework; claimed 82%/90-day success, 11,000+ relationships since 2009, 7-of-10 rejection rate, $1,695/month starting price, decline-2-of-3 rule, six sourcing channels, 7-day search launch.
- Lunch Actually: claimed 160,000 dates arranged, 87% satisfaction, 4,500 couples, 85% match-rating measure, 6-month no-match 70% refund mechanics, Matchmaking Institute certification lineage, "1 million happy marriages" goal, LA App branding.
- VIDA's comparative claims about Tawkify (blind-date model, package pricing) are competitive statements; the blind-date model is corroborated by Tawkify's own FAQ, the numbers are not.

## Rejected Findings

- **"In-app chat is the core"** — rejected. In all sampled products the conversation layer is thin or off-platform: contact info is withheld until the mediated point (Tawkify, LA), and the service handles communications (VIDA). Gated candidate-loop chat is the Dating Application's L0, not this Type's. The matchmaking conversation happens on the arranged date or through the matchmaker.
- **"User search/browse of the pool is part of matchmaking"** — rejected. Sampled products explicitly replace browsing with service selection; the member base is deliberately not publicly browsable.
- **"Algorithmic matching makes it a matchmaking platform"** — rejected as sufficient. Algorithmic curation of a candidate stream exists inside Dating Applications (questionnaire/compatibility pole documented by the dating-application pass; CMB self-identifies as a dating app). The Type requires the service layer: introductions + activation mediation + a service accountable for outcomes.
- **"Marriage intent defines the Type"** — rejected. Intent is orthogonal to selection authority: user-led matrimonial sites stay in the Dating Application family; relationship-oriented matchmaking services are this Type. (Refines the dating-application pass's "matrimonial leans matchmaking" note.)
- **"Selective admission is definitional"** — rejected for L0 (universal in sample but a matter of degree; held at L1).
- **"B2B 'matchmaking' (events, recruiting, mentorship) belongs to this Type"** — rejected. Polysemy only; different intent, objects, and workflows (see Boundary Findings 5).
- **"A matchmaking platform needs a rich member-facing app"** — rejected. VIDA runs the loop with a service-mediated funnel; LA's app is secondary to the consultant loop. The software floor is the member base + introductions of record, however thinly surfaced.

## Historical / Market-Sample Check (§24)

Question: would older, regional, offline, or differently positioned matchmaking still fit the L0?

- **Offline/traditional matchmakers** (village matchmaker, Jewish shadchan, arranged-marriage introducer): a paper index-card member file satisfies structure 1 (service-held member records built through interviews), introductions delivered to both families/parties satisfy structure 2, and the matchmaker's mediation of contact/chaperoned meetings satisfies structure 3 — with no software, no algorithms, no chat. The L0 therefore does not over-fit the modern platform packaging; the "platform" is the software instantiation of a much older service shape. The offline agency with no member-facing software is the thin ancestor, not a counter-example.
- **Regional formats**: the Asian arranged-lunch agency is directly evidenced (Lunch Actually). Japanese konkatsu (marriage-hunting) services, South Asian assisted-matrimony relationship-manager services, and religious-community matchmaker networks (shadchan-network platforms) share the same shape (service-led selection, introductions, mediated contact) — market context only this pass (unreachable), so no operational claims are drawn from them.
- **Era check**: nothing in L0 requires apps, algorithms, subscriptions, or even digital delivery. Modern packaging (portals, background-check integrations, pricing tiers) is era-implementation.

Conclusion: L0 holds across eras and regions; software surfaces, algorithms, and commercial packaging stay out of the definition.

## Boundary Findings

1. **vs Dating Application (§01.07 sibling — sharpest seam; discharges the joint-review flag)**. The discriminator is **who selects the candidates and who activates the contact**. In a Dating Application the user self-serves the whole loop: combs the candidate stream (deck/search/questionnaire feed), expresses interest unilaterally, passes the platform's consent gate, and chats in-product. In a Matchmaking Platform the service selects and *introduces*, and the introduction is activated through service-mediated steps (approval gates and/or arranged dates) rather than user-initiated messaging on an open pool. Tests: replace service-led selection + mediated activation with user-combed discovery + self-served gate/chat → Dating Application. Add a service that selects, introduces, and coordinates the meeting → crosses into Matchmaking Platform. Refinements to the flag as recorded by the dating-application pass: (a) algorithmic curation alone (daily batches, Top Picks, questionnaire streams) does NOT cross the seam — the user still combs and acts unilaterally, and no service owns introductions; (b) "assisted communication" is demoted from the seam definition to an optional capability — the sampled products activate contact via arranged dates, not in-product chat; (c) matrimonial platforms: user-led matrimonial sites remain Dating Application family; assisted-matrimony services (relationship-manager-led) are Matchmaking Platform. Intent (casual/relationship/marriage) does not decide the seam.
2. **vs Relationship Discovery Application (§01.07 sibling, unprocessed)**. Probable umbrella alias of the user-led loop per the dating-application pass; unchanged by this pass. Joint review still pending when that leaf is processed.
3. **vs Dating Community Platform (§01.07 sibling, processed)**. Community surfaces (groups/events/discussions) as first-class structures vs service-led introductions as the organizing structure. No overlap observed in this sample; the seam from the dating-community pass stands unchanged.
4. **vs Friend Discovery Application (§01.05)**. Friendship intent + user-led loop; no service-led introductions. Multi-intent embedding (a matchmaking service adding friend-making) would be a variant, not a Type flip.
5. **vs B2B "matchmaking" products (event buyer–seller appointments, recruiting candidate matching, mentorship matching)**. The word is polysemous. Those products match companies-to-meetings or candidates-to-roles; this Type's intake, selection, and activation are oriented to romantic/marital partnership, with date coordination and relationship outcomes as the deliverable. Different intent, objects, and workflows — different Types (see research/exhibitor-management.md, which explicitly rejected attendee↔exhibitor matchmaking as its core).
6. **vs the offline matchmaking agency (not an Application Type)**. The software floor: the member base and introductions must live in a software system the service operates (any member-facing surface, however thin). A pure offline agency is the historical ancestor. The thin pole (agency-led loop with a thin app/portal — Lunch Actually) stays inside the Type.
7. **vs matchmaker back-office tooling (CRM/candidate databases)**. Internal tooling serves the service; the Type is defined by the member-facing loop. Not separately researched this pass.

## Uncertainties

- **The curated-machinery pole is not directly evidenced.** eHarmony-class products (algorithm delivers matches; no human matchmaker) were unreachable on every official surface tried (bot-blocked, geo-blocked, app-store geo-redirect). Whether a pure-machinery delivery product (no human, no date coordination, no service accountability) belongs in this Type or in the Dating Application's questionnaire pole is recorded as an **open boundary question**. The who-selects + service-accountability test suggests such products sit on the Dating Application side unless a service layer (introductions, activation mediation, feedback accountability) exists. No claims about those products are made in the final document.
- **The matchmaker-network/religious pole is not directly evidenced.** SawYouAtSinai-class platforms (network of matchmakers over a community member base) were unreachable including via archive. The pole is described from market context only.
- **In-app chat in this Type** — not observed as a first-class surface in the sample; whether some products offer member-led chat after mutual acceptance is unverified beyond the sample.
- **Member-side experience depth** — the sampled evidence is service-positioning (marketing/FAQ); the actual member portal UX (what a member sees and clicks) is not documented at screenshot depth for any product.
- **All quantitative claims** (network sizes, success rates, prices, quotas, refund percentages) are vendor claims recorded in research notes only; none are asserted in the final document.
- **VIDA's comparative claims about Tawkify** are competitive statements; used only where Tawkify's own pages corroborate (blind-date model).

## Final Synthesis

Canonical Matchmaking Platform:

```text
L0 (defining invariant)
- Service-run member base of record (intake-built profiles held for
  service evaluation, not public browsing)
- Service-led selection delivered as named introductions for romantic
  partnership (the member does not comb a searchable pool)
- Service-mediated activation of the introduction into contact/meeting
  (approval gates and/or arranged dates; identity/contact withheld
  until the mediated point)

L1 (common mature structure)
- post-introduction feedback loop refining future selection
- candidate screening/vetting (ID, background, video, vetted databases)
- confidentiality posture (no public profiles; names/contact withheld)
- service-handled date coordination and logistics
- selective admission; dedicated matchmaker/consultant relationship
- asymmetric population (served clients vs candidate pool/sourcing)
- packages with introduction quotas/guarantees + delivery-tied refunds
- coaching/date-support add-ons; member events; member portal/app

L2 (variant / optional)
- selection agent: human matchmaker vs curated machinery vs hybrid
- disclosure posture: blind ↔ arranged-no-photos ↔ full pre-approval
- sourcing: closed base ↔ beyond-network search ↔ external scouting
- consent-gate placement; introduction cadence/quotas
- regional/community formats (lunch-date agencies, shadchan networks,
  konkatsu, assisted matrimony); intent specialization; events;
  guarantee/refund structures

L3 (vendor-specific)
- Premier tiers, factor frameworks, success-rate and network-size
  claims, specific refund mechanics, branded app surfaces
```

The Type is the software instantiation of a pre-digital service shape (the matchmaker). Its defining difference from the Dating Application is not features but **authority**: in a Dating Application the user is the selector; in a Matchmaking Platform the service is the selector, and the introduction — not the message — is the unit of work.
