# AI Video Generator

## Overview

An **AI Video Generator** produces video footage that did not exist before. The user describes what the video should show — a text prompt, optionally together with images, reference pictures, or start/end frames — and the system synthesizes the moving imagery: scenes, subjects, actions, camera movement, and, in newer models, the accompanying sound. The user reviews the result, refines the description or the settings, and regenerates until the clip matches the intent.

The defining core is small:

```text
Described intent (prompt, optionally conditioned on images/references)
└── Model-synthesized footage (moving imagery generated by the system)
    └── Generated video clip as deliverable
```

Everything else commonly associated with these products — prompting guides, image-to-video conditioning, model catalogs, credits, output libraries, APIs, native audio, communities, conversational agents — is standard capability layered around that core, not what makes the product this Type.

What separates it from neighbors:

- It is **not** an avatar video tool: nothing here binds a depicted presenter to authored spoken content; the imagery itself is the deliverable, and any speech is co-generated rather than scripted for a cast presenter.
- It is **not** a video editor: the material does not exist until the system generates it. Editing generated clips is part of the refinement loop, not the core.
- It is **not** an image generator: the deliverable moves. (Image generation is commonly bundled because generated stills serve as starting frames and supporting assets.)
- It is **not** a live medium: the output is a rendered clip, not a real-time session.

## Users & Context

The typical user has an idea or a need for footage and no way to film it:

- **Filmmakers and creative professionals** — concept films, previsualization, shots that would be impractical or impossible to shoot; multi-shot scenes assembled from generated clips.
- **Marketers, advertisers, and social teams** — product visuals, ad variants, campaign assets, vertical social clips, localized versions.
- **Content creators and consumers** — entertainment clips, photo-to-video transformations, trend formats, personalized videos.
- **Developers and product teams** — embedding generation into their own products through APIs; studios wiring generation into production pipelines.
- **Enterprises with data or compliance constraints** — some products can be run on the customer's own infrastructure or locally.

The working context is experimental and iterative: a prompt rarely produces the intended shot on the first try, so the work is a cycle of describing, generating, reviewing, and refining — closer to drafting than to shooting. Outputs are short clips; longer pieces are built by generating several clips and joining them.

## Core Model

### The Defining Core

**Described intent.** The user supplies a description of the desired video before it exists. The canonical form is a text prompt. Conditioning inputs are common and conceptually the same thing — ways of pinning down the description:

- an **image** to animate (image-to-video), where the prompt then describes only the motion
- **reference images** of a character, scene, object, or visual style, to steer appearance and keep it consistent
- **first and last frames**, between which the system generates the transition
- multiple images combined as a composite description

The description is the invariant; text, image, and references are implementation points on the same concept.

**Model-synthesized footage.** The defining step: the application generates moving imagery that did not previously exist. Nothing was filmed, drawn, or animated by the user. The system decides how described elements look and move, guided by the prompt and any conditioning inputs. Because the imagery is synthesized, results are inherently variable — the same prompt can produce different videos, and vendors state this plainly.

**Generated video clip as deliverable.** The output is a video clip — a downloadable file or a streamable result held in the product's library. Clips are short-form; a "video" longer than one clip is produced by generating several clips and assembling them (automatically or in a light timeline), or by extending a clip from its final moment so the story continues with visual and audio consistency.

### Standard Capabilities of Mature Products

These appear across the researched sample and are expected in the current market, but a product lacking some of them can still clearly be this Type:

- **Prompting craft as documented practice** — official prompting guides that break a prompt into visual components (subject, environment, lighting, framing, style) and motion components (action, environmental motion, camera movement, timing and speed); camera-terminology vocabularies; timestamp-style prompts to sequence actions within a clip.
- **Generation parameters** — aspect ratio, resolution, and duration chosen before generating; exact availability depends on the selected model. Post-generation **upscaling** to higher resolution is a common pattern, with the deliberate workflow of iterating cheaply at lower quality and upscaling only the final keeper.
- **Model catalog** — a choice among several generation models with different cost, speed, and quality tradeoffs, selected via a model switcher. Platform products increasingly aggregate third-party models alongside their own, and conversational agents pick the best model per step. Enterprise plans may restrict which models are available.
- **Iterate-to-quality loop** — regenerate, adjust the prompt to reinforce missing elements, continue working from a completed generation, or produce variations. Vendors explicitly frame iteration as a normal drafting phase, not a failure state.
- **Continuation and assembly** — extending a clip from its last second; multi-shot sequences auto-assembled into a cut; scene- or storyboard-level organization for longer narratives.
- **Output management** — persistent libraries with grouping structures (sessions, projects, assets) that keep the many variations of a shot organized and comparable; sharing within a team.
- **Metered generation** — generation consumes credits or subscription quota; cost varies by model and output type, so model choice is also an economic decision.
- **Delivery** — download, share links, embeds; public community showcases and trending feeds in consumer-oriented products.
- **API access** — programmatic generation (submit a request, await completion, retrieve the result) for embedding generation into other products and pipelines.
- **Native audio co-generation** — newer models generate dialogue, ambient sound, effects, and music together with the picture; where absent, audio is added separately or generated by companion tools.
- **Safety and provenance machinery** — moderation of prompts and outputs, AI-content usage notices, and provenance watermarking of generated video where implemented.
- **Conversational agent orchestration** — an emerging pattern in which a chat-based agent plans a creative project, generates the pieces step by step, and assembles them, with the user approving or adjusting the plan.

### One Structure, Many Implementations

The core model is conceptual. Products realize each concept differently:

```text
Concept:   Described intent
Variants:  text prompt · image + motion prompt · reference images
           (character/scene/style) · first/last frame · multi-image composite

Concept:   Generation
Variants:  single model catalog · third-party model aggregation ·
           open weights run locally · agent-selected models

Concept:   Refinement
Variants:  prompt reiteration · regenerate/variation · extend from
           last moment · upscale · agent-mediated re-instruction

Concept:   Deliverable
Variants:  single clip · auto-assembled multi-shot cut ·
           storyboard-to-scene production · API result
```

A reader who has only seen one style — say, a consumer app that animates a photo — should still be able to recognize a professional multi-model platform or an API-only generation endpoint as the same Type.

## How It Works

### Generate a clip

```text
Describe the video (write a prompt; optionally add an image,
   reference images, or first/last frames)
→ choose the model and set parameters (aspect ratio, resolution, duration)
→ generate (metered; asynchronous processing)
→ review the result in the output surface
→ refine: adjust the prompt or settings and regenerate,
   or continue working from the result
→ keep the keeper; upscale if needed; download or share
```

Two properties shape this loop. First, **iteration is the norm**: vendors document that a prompt may need reinforcement ("add the missing camera angle explicitly") and that several generations of the same idea are expected — the library of variations is a working material, not clutter. Second, **generation is metered and asynchronous**: submitting a prompt creates a job that consumes credits and completes in the background while the user keeps working, which is why planning prompts and iterating at lower settings before a final high-resolution pass are documented cost-saving practices.

### Build a longer piece from clips

```text
Generate clip 1 → extend it from its final moment, or generate
   the next shot conditioned on the same characters/style
→ repeat for each shot
→ assemble: automatic joining of generated clips into a cut,
   or manual arrangement on a light timeline
→ add audio (co-generated, or generated by companion tools)
→ deliver the finished piece
```

Character and style consistency across shots is a known hard problem; mature products address it with reference images of the character or style, and by continuing from the previous clip's final frame.

### Work through an agent

In products that offer it, the user describes the goal in conversation ("make three variations of this ad", "create a multi-shot scene with this character"). The agent proposes a plan — which models, which prompts, estimated cost — then generates step by step, assembling the results into a cut. The user can require approval before each generation or let it run, and can override model choices. Vendors are explicit that the agent's plan expresses intent, not a guaranteed outcome.

### Generate via API

Developer-facing products expose the same structure programmatically: submit a prompt (and conditioning inputs) with parameters, receive a job identifier and status, poll or await a webhook, and retrieve the generated video. The object model mirrors the app: description, parameters, job, result.

## Interfaces

The following surfaces are described conceptually; exact layouts and names vary by product.

### Prompt / generation canvas

The primary working surface: the prompt input, conditioning-input slots (image upload, references, first/last frames), and the generation controls (model selection, aspect ratio, resolution, duration). Primary actions: describe, attach, configure, generate.

### Results / session view

Where completed generations appear as they finish, usually as a scrollable or grid collection grouped by the session or project that produced them. Primary actions: play, compare variations, continue working from a result (extend, upscale, use as input), favorite, organize.

### Output library

The persistent store of everything generated, organized by sessions, projects, or folders, with search and filters. Primary actions: retrieve, rename, share with the team, download, delete.

### Assembly surface

In products that support longer pieces: a light timeline or scene view where generated clips are joined, trimmed, and layered with audio. Primary actions: arrange, trim, split, add tracks, undo.

### Community / showcase

In consumer-oriented products: a public feed of generated work with trending signals, plus packaged template galleries (photo-to-video formats, style transfers, ad formats) that serve as guided entry points. Primary actions: browse, remix a template, publish.

### Admin / developer surfaces

Team-oriented products add workspace and role management and model-availability governance. Developer products add API consoles, keys, usage meters, and documentation.

## Important Rules / Behaviors

### Outputs are provisional by design

Generated footage is inherently variable: the same prompt can yield different results, and vendors state that plans and expectations describe intent, not guaranteed outcomes. The refine-and-regenerate loop is therefore a required part of the workflow, not an optional extra.

### Generation is metered and model-dependent

Rendering consumes credits or quota, and cost varies by model and output type. Model choice is simultaneously a quality, speed, and cost decision. A documented pattern: iterate at cheaper/lower settings, then regenerate or upscale the final selection at full quality.

### Parameters are model-scoped

Aspect ratios, resolutions, durations, and audio support differ per model. A setting available on one model may not exist on another; products surface model-specific availability rather than a uniform parameter set.

### Longer videos are assembled, not generated whole

A single generation yields a short clip. Multi-shot pieces come from extending clips, conditioning later shots on the same characters or style, and assembling clips — automatically or manually. Consistency across shots is an active concern that reference inputs address.

### Provenance and moderation apply

Generated videos may be invisibly watermarked for AI-content detection, outputs pass safety evaluations, and prompts and results are subject to moderation. Consumer products attach explicit usage notices requiring lawful and respectful use.

### Ownership follows the product's terms

Where documented, users retain the rights to their generations and may use them commercially, with attribution optional. Exact terms are product-specific and plan-dependent.

## Variants

- **Professional creative platform** — multi-model catalogs, precise parameter control, sessions/projects organization, agent and workflow orchestration, enterprise governance; oriented to filmmakers, studios, and creative teams.
- **Platform-embedded model** — the generation model is exposed through several host surfaces (a general assistant, a dedicated filmmaking tool, a work-document tool, an API) rather than (only) its own app.
- **Consumer template app** — packaged photo-to-video formats, trend templates, and a public trending feed; generation as entertainment and social content.
- **Regional creator platform** — the same structural core operated for a regional market with its own community layer and a separate developer platform.
- **Open-weights platform** — the model's weights are published; the same capability is reachable as a hosted studio, an API, a local desktop application, or a self-hosted deployment, appealing where data control and customization matter.
- **Agent-first posture** — conversation replaces the parameter panel as the primary entry; the system plans, selects models, generates, and assembles.

A variant remains a variant of this Type as long as the defining core holds: described intent in, system-synthesized footage out, delivered as generated video clips.

## Related Application Types

| Application Type | Distinction |
|---|---|
| AI Avatar Video Generator | derives a video from authored spoken content delivered by a depicted presenter; here the imagery itself is the deliverable and speech is optional/co-generated. Products borrow in both directions (avatar tools add generated scenes; generators add talking characters), so the boundary is one of center of gravity |
| AI Video Editing Application | transforms footage the user already supplies; here the footage is created from a description. Editing one's own generated clips is refinement, not a Type collapse |
| AI Image Generator | still-image deliverable; image generation is commonly bundled here because stills feed generation as starting frames and references |
| Video Editor / NLE | human-executed timeline editing of existing footage as the primary interaction; assembly timelines in generators are delivery machinery for generated clips |
| Motion Graphics Application | user designs and animates graphic elements; here the system synthesizes footage from a description |
| AI Music / AI Audio Generator | audio-only deliverables; audio co-generation inside video generation is a capability, not a Type overlap |
| AI Design Generator | composed design artifacts (layouts, graphics, text) rather than synthesized moving footage |

The most important boundary is with the **AI Avatar Video Generator**, its sibling under generative video: both produce video from descriptions, and products increasingly borrow from each other. The structural difference is whether the video is bound to authored speech delivered by a depicted presenter (avatar) or is imagined imagery from a described scene (this Type).

## Representative Products

- **Runway** — professional creative platform; own model line plus third-party models (including Kling and Veo) selectable per generation; tools, apps, agent, and workflow modes; sessions/projects organization.
- **Google Veo** — platform-embedded generation model with native audio; reachable through a general assistant, a dedicated filmmaking tool, a work-video tool, and an API; reference-image conditioning, clip extension, camera controls; provenance watermarking.
- **Kling AI (Kuaishou)** — regional creator platform (China-based, international version available); text/image-to-video, multi-image reference, first/last frame, camera and motion control, lip sync, audio-video co-generation; separate developer platform.
- **LTX (Lightricks)** — open-weights posture; the same generation capability offered as a hosted studio, an API, a local desktop app, and self-hosted weights; storyboard-to-film studio for creative teams.
- **Hailuo AI (MiniMax)** — consumer template-driven posture; reference-conditioned generation, packaged photo-to-video formats, agent entry, public trending feed.

Market note: OpenAI's Sora — an early defining product of the category — was discontinued in 2026 (consumer surfaces in April, API in September). The Type's structure is stable across vendors, but individual products turn over quickly; product-specific claims in this category age faster than in mature categories.
