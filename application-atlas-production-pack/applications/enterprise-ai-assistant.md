# Enterprise AI Assistant

## Overview

An **Enterprise AI Assistant** is an AI assistant that an organization buys, operates, and governs for its own workforce. Employees interact with it in natural language — asking questions, drafting work, summarizing material, analyzing information, and triggering actions in the organization's systems — and the assistant's responses are generated by AI models rather than fixed scripted flows.

The defining core is small:

```text
Organization-operated        the org provisions its people, administers, governs
└── Workforce-facing         serves the org's own employees in their work context
    └── AI-driven conversational assistance
                             natural-language requests → model-generated responses
```

Everything else the market associates with the category — grounding in company knowledge with citations, actions in connected systems, agents, embedding inside productivity apps, admin consoles, usage analytics — is standard equipment of mature products, not what makes the product an enterprise AI assistant. A minimally configured organization assistant with none of those extras is still recognizable as one; a consumer chat account, a customer-facing support bot, or a scripted FAQ bot is not, no matter how capable.

The Type sits at the top of the enterprise AI stack: enterprise search and data platforms supply what it draws on, AI gateways and governance layers route and restrain it, agent platforms extend it — but the assistant itself is the end-user product employees actually open.

## Users & Context

The primary user is an **employee** — any member of the workforce whose work depends on company context: finding what the organization knows, drafting and summarizing in the organization's voice, and getting routine work done across the systems they use. Sampled products address the whole workforce (engineering, sales, support, HR, operations), not a specialist role.

Around the end user, mature deployments carry a distinct set of operating roles:

- **Administrator** — provisions users through workforce identity (single sign-on / identity provider), connects data sources, sets policies (what the assistant may ground on, which models and features are available), and monitors usage. Every sampled product ships a dedicated admin console for this.
- **Agent or tool builder** — in most current products, someone (a developer or a trained business user) extends the assistant with agents, plugins, or tools that connect it to specific systems and workflows.
- **Knowledge manager** — at the knowledge-platform pole, curates and verifies the content the assistant answers from.
- **Program owner** — tracks adoption, usage, and impact, and drives rollout.

The work context is the employee's normal workday: the assistant is opened as its own web/desktop surface, but it also appears inside the tools employees already use — productivity apps, chat tools, browsers, intranets, and portals.

## Core Model

### The Defining Core

Three properties, held together:

- **Organization-operated.** The customer is the organization, not the individual. The organization attaches the assistant to its workforce identity, pays per its own arrangement, administers it, and governs it. This is what separates an enterprise assistant from a consumer AI chat account — the same underlying model technology serves both, but the consumer surface has no organizational operator, no workforce identity, and no admin layer.
- **Workforce-facing.** The served population is the organization's own people acting in their work context. This is what separates it from customer-facing chatbots and guest-facing concierges, which serve the organization's customers and visitors. Where a product offers a public-facing mode, it is an explicit exception configuration, not the center.
- **AI-driven conversational assistance.** The employee states what they need in natural language; the assistant understands the request and generates its response. This is what separates it from scripted decision-tree bots (which can only follow pre-authored paths) and from search UIs (which return lists, not composed assistance).

### Standard Capabilities of Mature Products

These are what current products overwhelmingly add around the core. They make the assistant useful; they do not define it.

**Organization grounding.** The assistant draws on the organization's own knowledge — documents, messages, tickets, policies, data — typically by connecting to the company's applications through connectors and retrieving from what it finds. Two rules govern this layer:

- *Permission scoping*: the assistant answers from content the asking user may already access. A coworker with different permissions asking the same question can receive a different answer. The assistant inherits the organization's access model rather than bypassing it.
- *Citations*: grounded answers cite the sources they were generated from, so the employee can verify before acting.

**Work actions.** Beyond answering, the assistant does things: drafts emails and documents, summarizes threads and meetings, creates tickets, submits requests, updates records — executing in the connected systems on the employee's behalf. The industry's own shorthand for this pairing is "search and action."

**Productivity embedding.** Assistance appears inside the surfaces where work happens: drafting and summarizing inside documents, spreadsheets, presentations, and email; meeting summaries and action items inside meeting tools; a sidebar or command surface inside the browser; a bot inside chat tools.

**Admin and governance console.** A separate administrative surface where the organization manages users and access, connects and disconnects data sources, sets grounding and feature policies, selects or restricts AI models, governs agents, and reviews usage and audit records.

**Conversation history and multi-surface delivery.** Conversations persist for a bounded period and can be resumed; the same assistant is reachable from web, desktop, mobile, embedded surfaces, and — increasingly — through APIs and standard connectors so other AI tools can use the organization's assistant as context.

**Knowledge layer.** At the mature end, the organization curates what the assistant answers from: verified documents, authored answers, collections — signals that tell the assistant (and employees) what is authoritative.

**Builder layer.** Agents and plugins extend the assistant into scoped, task-focused helpers, built by the organization's own builders or installed from catalogs.

## How It Works

The Type's working loop has four phases: the organization sets it up, employees ask, the assistant answers and acts, and the organization governs what happened.

### 1. Deploy and connect (administrator)

```text
Attach workforce identity (SSO / identity provider / directory)
→ provision users and access
→ connect data sources (company applications, storage, data)
→ set policies (grounding posture, web access, models, features)
→ optionally configure agents, plugins, and the assistant's presentation
→ share the assistant with the workforce
```

In mature products this is self-service from the admin console: the administrator, not the vendor, decides which sources feed the assistant and what it may do. User access is typically subscription- or license-based and administered by the organization.

### 2. Ask (employee)

```text
Open the assistant (web app, in-app panel, extension, chat tool)
→ state the request in natural language
→ the assistant retrieves from permitted company sources
  (and web / model knowledge, per the organization's policy)
→ a generated response returns, citing the sources used
→ the employee verifies the citations and continues the conversation
```

The retrieval step is permission-checked: the assistant sees the organization's content through the asking user's access rights. If the answer is not in the permitted material, the assistant says so — unless the organization has allowed it to fall back on general model knowledge.

### 3. Act (employee + assistant)

```text
Refine a draft, summary, or analysis in the conversation
→ or hand the assistant a task: create the ticket, submit the request,
  update the record, schedule the meeting
→ the assistant executes in the connected system through its
  action integrations, or through an agent built for that workflow
→ the outcome returns into the conversation
```

At the agentic end, the assistant plans and executes multi-step work — understanding the request, choosing tools, carrying the task through, and adapting — while staying inside the permissions and policies the organization set.

### 4. Govern (administrator)

```text
Review usage and adoption (who uses it, what they ask, what it does)
→ adjust policies, models, agents, and sources
→ audit queries, answers, and actions where required
→ drive adoption and correct gaps (training, knowledge curation)
```

## Interfaces

The surfaces below are described conceptually; exact layouts and names vary by product.

### Assistant chat surface

The primary employee surface — a conversational workspace, usually web and desktop.

- conversation thread with the assistant, prior conversations, suggested prompts
- source controls (which knowledge the assistant may use), file attachment, citations on answers
- primary actions: ask, refine, verify sources, attach material, hand off a task

### Embedded assistance

Assistance inside the employee's existing tools.

- drafting, rewriting, and summarizing inside documents, spreadsheets, presentations, email
- meeting summaries, transcripts, and action items inside meeting and chat tools
- primary actions: generate, transform, or insert content in place

### Browser extension and chat-tool surfaces

The assistant reachable where the employee already is.

- sidebar or quick-ask surface in the browser; bot in the organization's chat tool
- primary actions: ask about the current page or context, search, continue a conversation

### Search within the assistant

Finding people, documents, and answers across connected sources.

- results limited to the employee's permissions; people and team discovery
- primary actions: search, open sources, ask a follow-up

### Admin console

The organization's control surface.

- user and access management, data-source connections, policy configuration, model management, agent governance, usage and adoption analytics, audit records
- primary actions: provision, connect, configure, restrict, review

### Builder surfaces

Where the organization extends the assistant.

- agent/plugin builders and catalogs; task-app creation
- primary actions: build, install, govern, retire

### API / connector supply

The assistant (and the organization's grounded context) exposed to other tools through APIs and standard AI-connectivity protocols, under the same access rules.

## Important Rules / Behaviors

### The assistant is an access-control surface, not a bypass

Permission scoping is the load-bearing rule of the Type: answers are composed from content the asking user may already access, and permission changes in the source systems are reflected in what the assistant will surface. This makes the assistant simultaneously a productivity tool and an extension of the organization's information-access model — which is why its deployment is an administrative decision rather than an individual one.

### Grounding posture is an organization decision

Whether the assistant may answer from enterprise data only, or may also use the public web, or may fall back on general model knowledge, is configured by the administrator. When the answer is outside the permitted material, the assistant reports that it could not find it rather than improvising — unless the organization allowed the fallback.

### Citations are the verification mechanism

Grounded answers carry their sources. The employee is expected to check them before acting; the organization expects the trail for trust and audit.

### The assistant supports, does not replace, human decisions

Products state this explicitly for high-stakes domains (for example, employment or evaluative decisions): the assistant informs and executes, while accountable humans decide. Guardrails and governance machinery express the same posture elsewhere.

### Administration gates capability

Features are enabled or removed at the organization level: web access, image generation, agent creation and use, model selection, even the assistant itself. An employee's experience is the intersection of their permissions and the organization's policy.

### Conversations persist, briefly

Conversation history is retained for a bounded period (windows vary by product) so employees can resume and search prior exchanges; retention is part of the organization's data-governance surface. Queries, answers, and actions are commonly logged for audit.

## Variants

- **Suite-embedded assistant** — the assistant lives inside an existing productivity suite and draws on the suite's own work data; its distinct flavor is the deepest in-app embedding and license-tier packaging.
- **Standalone work-AI platform** — an independent platform whose job is to connect the whole company estate and serve the whole workforce; its distinct flavor is the breadth of connectors and the knowledge-management layer.
- **Cloud-provider assistant** — an assistant service operated on a cloud platform's infrastructure, configured as an "application" with explicit data sources and plugins; its distinct flavor is infrastructure-grade configuration and the option of public-facing (anonymous) deployments.
- **Service-automation-first assistant** — grown out of IT/HR service resolution, strongest at executing requests in service systems; its distinct flavor is omnichannel delivery (chat, portals, intranet) and built-in service workflows.
- **Model-vendor enterprise tier** — a general-purpose assistant sold to organizations with enterprise administrative controls; thinnest organizational grounding, strongest general capability.
- **Scope axis** — general-purpose (the whole workforce, any kind of work) versus domain-scoped (centered on one function, such as IT/HR support); domain-scoped assistants are the same machinery with a narrower center.
- **Autonomy axis** — assistance-first (answers, drafts, summaries on request) versus agentic-first (multi-step execution of delegated work); current products span the gradient, and vendors ship both postures in one product line.

## Related Application Types

| Application Type | Relationship | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise Knowledge Assistant | closest sibling | centered on grounded answering from the organization's knowledge — the knowledge corpus and answer trust are the center; here, answering is one capability beside drafting, analysis, and actions. Products heavily straddle both; the seam is center of gravity |
| Enterprise Search Platform / Internal Knowledge Search | substrate | search returns result lists over the organization's estate; the assistant converses, composes, and acts. Search platforms supply the index and permission layer the assistant inherits; the assistant's built-in search is a capability, not the Type |
| Knowledge Question Answering Application | adjacent | owner-defined knowledge base defines what is answerable and the grounded answer is the deliverable; the enterprise assistant's scope is broader and its grounding is the live connected estate rather than a curated base |
| Customer Service Chatbot Platform | audience neighbor | serves the organization's external customers; this Type serves the organization's own workforce |
| Digital Concierge | audience neighbor | serves a venue's external guests with service-fulfillment semantics; workforce-facing assistance is a different served population |
| AI Coding Assistant / AI Coding Agent | domain-scoped neighbor | centered on the development workflow (IDE-embedded, code context); the enterprise assistant is general-purpose. Vendors sell them as separate products even when both are organization-licensed |
| Agent Development Platform | builder neighbor | builds and runs custom agents; the enterprise assistant is the ready-made assistant employees use. Assistants embed agent capability, which is the convergence zone |
| Business Intelligence Platform | data neighbor | governed analytics content and dashboards remain BI's center; in-BI AI question answering is a BI capability. Assistants may consume BI/warehouses as data connections |
| AI Gateway / AI Governance / AI Safety Guardrail Platform | infrastructure neighbors | routing, cost, policy, and screening layers around AI usage; the assistant is the end-user product those layers govern |
| AI Meeting Assistant | scope neighbor | centered on the meeting; meeting help inside an enterprise assistant is one capability among many |
| Consumer AI chat | outside the atlas | same model technology, no organizational operator, no workforce identity, no admin layer |

The two most important boundaries: against **Enterprise Knowledge Assistant** (same products, different center of gravity — flagged for joint review), and against **Enterprise Search** (the substrate relationship: assistants inherit the index and permission layer rather than replacing it).
