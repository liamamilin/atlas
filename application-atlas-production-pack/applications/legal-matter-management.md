# Legal Matter Management

## Overview

A **Legal Matter Management** application is a legal function's system of record for its own work. It organizes everything the legal team does — disputes, contract negotiations, reviews, advice requests, investigations, compliance questions — into **matters**: persistent, individually identified records that each carry an accumulated file, a set of responsible people, and a tracked state from opening to closure.

The defining core is small. Remove the matter as the unit of record and the product becomes a task tracker. Remove the accumulated file and it becomes a bare register. Remove managed progression and accountability, and it becomes a shared drive plus a to-do list. What makes the Type is the conjunction: one place where a legal department's entire workload exists as identifiable matters, each with its file, its people, and its status, inspectable as a population at any moment.

Matters are not contracts (agreements are a different object, even when managed in the same suite) and not court cases (the court keeps its own register). The matter is the legal department's own working container for a piece of legal work. In mature products the matter's file also carries the money attributed to it — budgets, invoices from outside counsel, internal time — but spend is a capability layered onto the matter, not what defines it.

## Users & Context

The primary operator is an **in-house legal department** (corporate, government, or institutional legal function) running its own workload. This is the audience every researched product defines itself by; the market usually sells this Type as part of a broader "enterprise legal management" bundle together with spend and vendor management.

Typical roles and their relationship to the system:

- **Matter owner / responsible lawyer** — accountable for a matter: controls its file, invites participants, drives it to closure.
- **Matter manager / working counsel** — handles the matter day to day; may be an internal colleague or a lawyer at an outside firm participating directly in the system.
- **Legal operations / department administrator** — configures matter types, intake routing, permissions, and reporting; often runs the intake queue that triages incoming requests.
- **Business users (internal clients)** — the departments legal serves. They typically request work, receive status, and sometimes self-serve; most see only a limited, portal-style view rather than the full workspace.
- **Outside counsel** — firm lawyers engaged on a matter; they collaborate in the system through dedicated portals or invited access, and submit invoices against the matter.

Secondary consumers are leadership (the general counsel's office reading dashboards) and adjacent functions such as finance (receiving approved invoices) and records/compliance (relying on the matter file as the department's record).

## Core Model

### The matter

The **matter** is the central object: a persistent, individually identified record of one discrete piece of legal work. It carries:

- a **name** and a **system-assigned identifier** (in the researched sample, the identifier is generated by the system and immutable — the anchor other records and invoices cite);
- a **matter type** drawn from the department's own classification (litigation, employment, contracts, IP, regulatory, advice requests, and so on), with **configurable data fields per type** — what must be captured about a dispute differs from what must be captured about a contract review;
- **people**: an accountable owner, usually a manager running the work, and members — internal colleagues, business stakeholders where appropriate, and outside-firm lawyers;
- a **state** (see below);
- and, most importantly, **its file**.

### The matter file

The matter is a container that accumulates the working record of the engagement:

- **documents and emails** — stored, organized in folders, searchable, previewable, often captured directly from the lawyer's inbox;
- **notes** — decisions, reasoning, and internal context recorded on the matter;
- **tasks** and **actions** — the work items that move the matter forward, often reusable as templates per matter type;
- **key dates and events** — deadlines, hearings, milestones, synced to personal calendars;
- **conversations** — threaded discussions on the matter, frequently bridged to email so that even people not named on the matter can participate by replying;
- **activity history** — a log of what happened on the matter, attributable and exportable;
- and, where the department engages outside counsel, **invoices and time** attributed to the matter, plus budgets or scopes negotiated for the work.

The file is what turns a name on a list into the department's record of an engagement: someone who has never touched the matter can open it and reconstruct what was done, by whom, and why.

### Accountability and progression

Every matter sits in a **state** — conceptually: opened, actively worked, possibly paused, closed. Exact labels vary by product; the researched sample's documented ladder runs from an active state on creation, through a hold state for matters awaiting input, to a completed state that leaves live reporting. Products with deeper outside-counsel machinery add negotiation states for agreeing scope and reviewing scope changes. State changes are a governed act: ordinary business users cannot flip a matter's state, and closure is typically the most controlled transition.

The population of matters is a first-class concern: lists and grids filterable by state, owner, type, and date; dashboards over volume, cycle time, deadlines, and spend; and exports for leadership reporting.

### What gathers around the matter

In mature products, three further structures attach to the matter without being part of its definition:

- **Requests (intake)** — a front door through which the business asks legal for help; requests are triaged in queues and routed to the right person, often converting into matters.
- **Outside counsel and vendors** — firms engaged on matters under the department's terms, collaborating through portals and submitting invoices that attribute to the matter.
- **Contracts as sibling objects** — many suites manage contracts in the same platform and link them to the matters that produced or concern them; the two remain distinct object types.

## How It Works

### Open: a matter comes into existence

A matter enters the system by one of three paths: a lawyer creates it directly; a business request arrives through the intake front door (a form, an email, a chat channel) and is triaged into a matter; or a request is captured from the inbox and converted. On creation it receives its identity, its type, and usually an accountable owner. Where intake is used, requests submitted by business users land in a triage queue that a designated intake role reviews and routes to the right lawyer; matters created by the legal team itself typically skip triage.

### Work: the matter as the daily workspace

The responsible lawyer and assigned members work **from the matter**: tasks are created and completed, key dates logged, documents filed (often straight from email), notes written, status posted or requested from collaborators. Conversations with the internal client and with outside counsel happen on the matter so the file stays complete. Status updates — ad hoc or on a schedule — keep requesters informed without them having to chase. External counsel participate through the same matter, invited with scoped access.

### Close: a governed ending

Closing a matter is a deliberate transition, not a checkbox: some products gate it — prompting about unfinished tasks, requiring specific fields to be completed, or reminding the user to confirm billing is finished. Closed matters leave live reporting but remain part of the record, retrievable and reportable afterward. Some products also collect feedback from the internal client at closure.

### The management loop

Around individual matters runs the department-level loop that gives the Type its name: the legal-operations view of the whole population — what is coming in, who is overloaded, what is stalled, which matters are approaching deadlines, what spend has been committed against which matters — inspected on a recurring cadence and acted on by reassigning work, adjusting outside counsel, or reporting to leadership.

## Interfaces

- **Matter workspace (detail page)** — the core surface. Purpose: one place holding everything about a matter. Typical contents: header with name, identifier, owner/manager, current state, and status-update area; details section with type-specific fields; tabs or sections for tasks, documents, conversations, notes, people/members, security, dates, invoices, and activity. Primary actions: edit details, add files and tasks, post or request status, add members, change state.
- **Matter list / grid** — the population surface. Purpose: see and work the department's whole workload. Typical information: name, type, state, owner, key dates, open tasks, spend. Primary actions: filter and save views, bulk-edit states, open matters, export.
- **Intake / triage queue** — the front-door surface for legal operations. Typical information: incoming requests with submitter, requested service, urgency. Primary actions: review, route/assign, convert to matter, respond to requester.
- **Requester / business portal** — the internal client's surface. Purpose: submit requests, track their status, self-serve for routine needs. Deliberately limited compared to the legal workspace.
- **Outside counsel portal** — the firm's surface. Purpose: work assigned matters, upload invoices, communicate with the department under its guidelines.
- **Dashboards and reports** — the leadership surface. Purpose: volume, cycle time, deadlines, workload distribution, spend by matter/firm/practice area.
- **Administration** — matter types and per-type fields, intake routing rules, templates, roles and permissions, integrations (email, calendar, document management, finance systems).

## Important Rules / Behaviors

- **Membership is not the same as access.** A matter has people actively working on it (members, who see it in their own worklist and get notifications) and separately people or teams granted visibility. Many products also let a matter be **restricted**, hiding it from everyone except explicitly granted parties — confidentiality posture is built into the object, not bolted on.
- **State changes are privileged.** Ordinary users can work a matter but cannot generally move its state; closure in particular is reserved to accountable roles and may be blocked until required completion data is entered.
- **The matter file is expected to be complete.** The department relies on the matter (not email) as its record; the system therefore pulls work into the matter — inbox filing, email-to-matter bridges, invoice uploads attributed to the matter — and prompts at closure about unfinished tasks and outstanding billing.
- **Outside-counsel money flows through matter attribution.** Invoices from firms cite the matter; approval, budget, and reporting machinery hangs off that attribution.
- **Business users see a bounded world.** Internal clients can request, view status, and self-serve, but they do not operate matters; the legal function retains control over triage, assignment, and closure.
- **The identifier is stable.** Matters are numbered by the system and that identity does not change, because invoices, links, and history all cite it.

## Variants

- **Matter-first standalone** — lean products for small and mid-sized teams where matter tracking, intake, and simple spend visibility are the whole offer.
- **ELM-suite pillar** — matter management as one pillar of a wider legal operations platform alongside e-billing, vendor management, contract management, and reporting; the most common market packaging.
- **Spend-led** — products that grew out of legal e-billing, where the matter workspace exists to anchor invoice review and vendor analytics.
- **Enterprise/global** — highly configurable deployments with custom matter types per practice area and jurisdiction, multi-language and multi-entity operation, government e-invoicing compliance, and cloud or on-premises delivery.
- **Litigation-tilted deployments** — departments that run most of their load as disputes extend matters with proceeding-level detail (stages, parties, claim amounts, insurance coverage, limitation periods); this depth is a matter-type instantiation rather than a separate core.

## Related Application Types

| Application Type | Relationship | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Law Practice Management System | closest sibling | A law firm's client-anchored business system: the matter is fused with the money loop (time capture → client billing → payments → trust accounting) and firm operations. Here the legal function runs its own workload with spend *oversight*, not client billing. Strip client billing and trust from the firm system and what remains is this Type. |
| Outside Counsel Management | adjacent, usually bundled | Centers the law-firm relationship lifecycle (firm registry, engagement terms, invoice review gate, evaluation) and needs only matter *attribution*. This Type centers matter depth and does not require the firm-relationship machinery. |
| Legal Spend Management | adjacent, usually bundled | Centers the money across all legal spend (budgets, accruals, invoice analytics). This Type centers the matter record; the money layer reads from it. |
| Legal Docket Management | adjacent | Centers the register of dated court obligations with rules-computed deadlines. Here deadlines are one attribute of the matter; the matter breadth is the record. |
| Legal Hold Management | adjacent | A preservation-duty workflow that attaches to matters (or stands alone). The matter is the anchor, not the system of record. |
| Litigation Management Platform | narrower cousin | Oversight of litigated matters specifically (claims posture, insurer/corporate litigation budgets). In matter management, litigation is one matter type among many. |
| Contract Lifecycle Management | sibling object | Contracts are agreements; matters are work engagements. Suites ship both and link them. |
| Court Case Management System | name collision only | The court's official register of judicial work, run by the court. The matter file is the legal function's own working record. |
| Project / Task Management | generic analogue | A project lacks the legal-work unit of record: matter-type taxonomy, outside counsel participation, spend attribution, and the confidentiality posture of a legal file. |
