# Hotel Front Desk Application

## Overview

A **Hotel Front Desk Application** is the staff-facing operational application of a lodging property's front office. It holds the property's rooms as live, individually assignable inventory, moves each guest stay through **arrival (check-in with room assignment) → in-house service (room moves, extensions, charges posted during the stay) → departure (check-out with a settled folio)**, and keeps the **guest folio** — the stay's running account of charges, payments and balance — as the desk's money record.

Two product shapes carry this definition in the market:

- **Desk-first systems for small properties** (independent hotels, inns, B&Bs, hostels, vacation rentals), where the front desk loop is most of the product; and
- **The front desk module of a full property management system (PMS)**, which surrounds it with distribution, rate management, housekeeping operations, reporting and multi-property management.

The boundary in both directions: this is not the reservation-selling layer (booking engine, central reservation system, channel manager), which ends when the guest arrives; it is not the housekeeping system, which services rooms and feeds the desk their readiness; and it is not the guest-facing experience layer (mobile check-in apps, concierge chat), which sits on the other side of the desk.

## Users & Context

The primary user is the **front desk agent / receptionist**, working shifts at the property's front desk. Their daily surface is the arrivals and departures queues, the live room picture, and each in-house stay with its folio. Typical work: checking guests in and out, assigning and changing rooms, taking payments, posting charges, answering stay-related questions with the reservation in front of them.

Around the agent:

- **desk supervisor / duty manager** — handles exceptions: rate overrides, refunds, folio disputes, VIP attention, walk-in decisions during high occupancy.
- **cashier / accounting roles** (larger properties) — dedicated payment handling and end-of-day money reconciliation against the desk's accounts.
- **housekeeping staff** — not desk users, but their room-status updates are consumed by the desk in real time; the desk cannot assign rooms without them.
- **owner / general manager** — in small properties, often the same person configuring rooms, rates and documents.

The work context is shift-based and interruption-driven: the desk alternates between queue work (the day's arrivals and departures) and transactional work (one guest standing in front of the desk). Modern products deliver the full desk on desktop web, with mobile companions and self-service surfaces (kiosks, guest phones) extending the same records beyond the desk counter.

## Core Model

The application's world is built on three structures. Remove any one and the product is no longer a front desk application.

### 1. Rooms as live, individually assignable inventory

The property's accommodations are held as concrete units — individual rooms (or other bookable spaces), grouped into categories — each carrying its current **occupancy state** (who is in it, arriving, departing) and **readiness state** (whether it can be given to a guest: clean, inspected, out of service, occupied). This is the desk's ground truth: the board a 1980s hotel kept on the wall is the same object, now live and shared with housekeeping.

The readiness state is typically maintained on the housekeeping side and consumed by the desk; occupancy state is maintained by the desk itself as stays start and end.

### 2. The stay, operated from arrival to departure

The desk's unit of work is the **guest stay** — not the reservation and not the room alone. A reservation becomes a stay when the guest is registered and assigned to a specific room at check-in (the reservation context may come from any source — booking engine, OTA, phone — or not exist at all, in the case of a walk-in). During the stay, the desk maintains it: room moves, extensions and shortening, guest and companion details, special requests. At the end, the stay is closed at check-out.

```text
Reservation (from any channel) or walk-in
        ↓
Check-in: register guest → assign a specific room from live inventory
        ↓
In-house stay: maintain (move / extend / adjust) · charges post to the folio
        ↓
Check-out: settle the folio → release the room
```

Exact terminology varies (some systems speak of "starting" and "processing" a reservation), but the arrival → in-house → departure arc is universal.

### 3. The guest folio — the stay's running account

Each stay carries a **folio**: an account that accumulates the room charges generated by the reservation and the ancillary charges incurred during the stay (restaurant, bar, spa, phone, parking — posted manually or automatically from the property's outlets), against which deposits, prepayments and payments are recorded, and whose balance is settled at check-out. A closed folio is assumed settled; some products allow corrections for a defined window after closure, then freeze the record.

The desk's money view over all of this is the **guest ledger**: in-house guests and their current balances, deposits held from future guests, and unpaid items. In mature products this structure extends to accounts without stays — company (city-ledger style) billing where multiple guests' charges are compiled onto one company account, and house accounts for local, non-staying customers.

### What mature products add

Standard capabilities layered on the defining core (widespread in current products, but not what makes a desk a desk):

- **arrivals / departures lists** — the day's expected check-ins and check-outs as the desk's work queues
- **guest profiles** — persistent per-guest records enabling repeat-guest recognition and preferences; in some products created automatically from reservations
- **reservation handling at the desk** — creating, modifying, cancelling reservations (including walk-ins and, in some products, day-use/hourly bookings)
- **deposits and preauthorizations** — money taken before or at arrival against the future folio
- **folio splitting** — dividing a stay's charges across multiple folios or parties for separate invoicing
- **group handling at the desk** — room blocks, rooming lists, group master folios, group check-in/out
- **desk reminders** — alarm/reminder calendars, reservation notes, VIP flags
- **housekeeping-status integration** — the live readiness picture the desk assigns rooms against
- **role/permission structure** — what an agent may do (override rates, refund, close) versus supervisors
- **end-of-day / night-audit adjacency** — the accounting roll-over that full PMS products perform; the desk's daily money close is its front edge

Concepts vs. implementations: the same structure appears under different names across products — the room may be called a unit, space or resource; the folio may be called a bill or guest account; the desk may sit inside a product named "PMS" with the desk loop as one named module. A reader should be able to recognize the front desk in all of these shapes.

## How It Works

### The arrival (check-in)

```text
Guest arrives (or opens the online check-in they started earlier)
→ retrieve the reservation: name, confirmation number, or today's arrivals list
→ verify identity and stay terms; complete registration
→ assign a specific room from live inventory (respecting readiness state
   and any pre-assigned room)
→ check in: the stay becomes active, the room becomes occupied
→ keys / access credentials issued; registration documents produced
   where required
```

A confirmed reservation does not by itself put the guest in the room: assignment is constrained by live availability and readiness, which is why the desk works against the shared room-status picture rather than a static list. Walk-ins skip the retrieval step: the desk creates the stay directly, if inventory allows.

### In-house

During the stay the folio grows: room charges post automatically per night; ancillary charges arrive from the property's outlets (restaurant, spa, phone) — posted by desk staff or pushed automatically through POS integration. The desk also executes stay changes on request: moving the guest to another room, extending or shortening the stay (which changes future room availability and, at some properties, the rate), adding or removing companions, recording service requests and notes for the next shift.

### The departure (check-out)

```text
Today's departures list
→ open the departing stay's folio
→ review accumulated charges; correct or split as needed
→ settle the balance (payment taken, or charges moved to a company account)
→ check out: the stay closes, the folio closes (assumed settled)
→ the room is released and flagged for housekeeping
```

### The working day

Across the day the desk moves between the arrivals and departures queues, the live room picture, and individual folios. Money discipline is continuous: deposits held against future stays, preauthorizations on arriving guests, balances on in-house guests visible in the guest ledger at any moment. At day's end, the property's accounting roll-over (night audit in fuller products) closes the day's position — a function that borders the desk's money work but belongs to the wider system.

### Core, common, optional

- **Defining core:** live room inventory with occupancy/readiness state; check-in with room assignment; in-house stay maintenance; check-out with folio settlement; the folio with posting and the guest-ledger view.
- **Standard capabilities:** arrivals/departures queues, profiles, deposits and preauths, refunds and receipts, folio splitting, company/house accounts, groups and rooming lists, desk reminders and VIP flags, housekeeping-status integration, role permissions, night-audit adjacency.
- **Common variants:** self-service surfaces (online/mobile check-in, kiosks, digital keys, contactless registration with e-signature), day-use/hourly bookings, guest messaging at the desk, ID-document capture for statutory registration, AI assistance for identity verification, payment and room assignment.

## Interfaces

Exact layouts and names vary by product; the following surfaces are described conceptually.

### Arrivals / departures queues

The desk's daily work lists.

- typical information: guest name, reservation reference, room assignment or requested category, nights, balance/deposit status, special flags
- primary actions: check in, check out, retrieve full reservation, assign a room, add a walk-in

### Room picture (availability / room rack)

The live state of the property's rooms.

- typical information: each unit's occupancy state (occupied / arriving / departing / vacant) and readiness state (clean, inspected, out of service, do-not-disturb), often as a grid or calendar; in some products drag-and-drop
- primary actions: assign or move guests between specific rooms, read readiness before assigning, spot conflicts

### Stay / reservation detail with folio

The record the desk lives in for any transaction with one guest.

- typical information: guest(s) and companions, stay dates, room, rate, reservation source, notes and flags, and the folio — charges by type and date, payments, deposits, current balance
- primary actions: modify the stay (dates, room, guests), post or correct a charge, split the folio, take a payment or preauthorization, print or send receipts, add notes

### Guest ledger / balances view

The money picture over all stays.

- typical information: in-house guests with balances owing, deposits held from future guests, outstanding company or house accounts
- primary actions: take payment on a balance, review aging, transfer charges to company accounts

### Housekeeping status view

The readiness picture the desk assigns against (consumed; owned by the housekeeping side).

- typical information: per-room service status and occupancy, zones, current work
- primary actions: request or escalate a room's servicing, confirm a room is ready

### Groups

Where present, the desk's window on blocks and parties.

- typical information: block inventory, rooming list, group master folio versus individual folios
- primary actions: pick up rooms from the block, check groups in or out, post charges to the master account

### Configuration / settings

Rooms and categories, rates visible at the desk, taxes and charges, document templates, user roles and permissions. In desk-first small-property products this configuration is part of the same application; in full suites it sits with the PMS administration modules.

A note on self-service surfaces: online check-in, kiosks and guest mobile screens present the *same* stay and folio records from the guest's side. They are best understood as delivery surfaces of the desk loop rather than a separate system, though dedicated guest-experience products also exist.

## Important Rules / Behaviors

- **A confirmed reservation is not an occupied room.** Check-in depends on a specific room being both assignable (free for the dates) and ready (housekeeping status). Room readiness is the desk's dependency on housekeeping, updated continuously.
- **The folio is open while the stay is open; closed at check-out.** Charges post to the open folio throughout the stay; at check-out the folio must be settled before it closes. A closed folio is assumed balanced; some products allow corrections in a defined window after closure, then freeze the record thereafter.
- **Room assignment is a tracked change, not an overwrite.** Moving a guest updates the room states of both rooms involved and preserves the stay's history; the old room returns to the readiness cycle.
- **Money taken before arrival secures the stay.** Deposits and preauthorizations are recorded against the reservation/folio and settle against the balance at departure; they are the desk's answer to no-show and walk-away risk.
- **Not everything posted belongs to a stay.** Non-resident charges (a restaurant guest, a company event) post to separate accounts — house accounts or company/paymaster-style accounts — keeping the guest ledger's in-house picture clean.
- **Desk power is permission-gated.** Rate overrides, refunds, folio corrections and closing actions are typically restricted to supervisory roles; larger properties add explicit cashier accountability for the drawer's day.
- **Exact labels and states vary.** Occupancy and readiness vocabularies (occupied, vacant, clean, dirty, inspected, out of service…), folio states and audit names differ by product; the states described here are conceptual, not a standard.

## Variants

- **Desk-first small-property systems** — independents, inns, B&Bs, hostels, vacation rentals. The desk loop plus basic configuration is most of the product; distribution and revenue tools arrive as add-on modules.
- **Front desk within enterprise PMS suites** — the same loop as a module among reservations, rate management, housekeeping, sales-and-catering, reporting; heavier group handling, complex folio structures, compliance-grade registration documents.
- **On-premise vs cloud** — historically the desk was sold as on-premise software (several products still carry "FrontDesk" in their names); current market is dominated by cloud platforms with browser and mobile desk clients.
- **Self-service-forward deployments** — properties that move most arrivals to guest phones or kiosks; the desk shifts to exception handling, and digital keys replace the key rack.
- **Property-type semantics** — hostel bed-level inventory and per-person pricing, campground sites, day-use/hourly rooms; per related research, these are inventory configurations of the same desk loop.
- **Regional compliance shapes** — statutory registration cards, police/guest reporting, fiscal receipt formats; implemented as document and reporting layers on the desk loop.

## Related Application Types

| Application Type | Relationship | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Hotel Property Management System / PMS | broader system of record | PMS manages the whole property — reservations and distribution coordination, rates/inventory, housekeeping operations, reporting, multi-property. The front desk application is its operational core; every full PMS contains it, and desk-first small-property products carry little else. |
| Hotel Central Reservation System / Booking Engine / Channel Manager | adjacent, pre-arrival | These sell and capture reservations and sync availability before arrival; the desk operates the stay after arrival. Remove the stay/folio/settlement loop and you have distribution. |
| Hotel Housekeeping Management | adjacent, operations | Owns room servicing operations (zones, checklists, inspections, work orders) and feeds room readiness to the desk; the desk consumes status but does not run the service work. |
| Hotel Guest Experience Platform / Digital Concierge | guest-facing counterpart | Guest-side surfaces (mobile check-in, chat, portal) over the same records; the desk application is staff-facing at the counter. Self check-in straddles the two. |
| Hotel CRM / Loyalty Platform | adjacent, relationship layer | Owns the guest profile of record and the loyalty program across stays and properties; the desk touches profiles at arrival but does not manage the relationship or program. |
| Hostel Management System | sibling Type | Same desk loop; the hostel leaf's structural delta is bed-as-unit inventory and per-person rate semantics. |
| Hotel Revenue Management System | upstream | Decides pricing and availability controls; the desk applies the resulting rate at the counter. |

The closest and most important boundary is with the Hotel PMS: the desk application is the PMS with everything but the front office removed — and, historically and in the small-property market, products sold as "front desk software" are exactly that shape.

## Representative Products

- **Mews** — cloud PMS for independent and group hotels; publicly documented definitions of check-in, check-out, folio and cashier structures.
- **WebRezPro** — long-established cloud PMS that markets a dedicated "Hotel Front Desk" feature set spanning pre check-in to post check-out.
- **Yanolja Cloud Solution (eZee lineage)** — cloud platform for small independents; the vendor's historical on-premise product carried the "FrontDesk" name, and the current platform presents a dedicated front-desk-manager offering.
- **HotelDruid** — free/open-source property software from B&Bs to large hotels; shows the minimal skeleton of rooms, assignment, documents and charges.

The core model was also checked against the classic front-office structure that modern products' own glossaries still carry (folio, guest ledger, city-ledger-style company billing, cashier) to avoid over-fitting to the current cloud and self-service era.
